# Project Lifecycle

## Lifecycle model

The lifecycle is a shared operating vocabulary, not a rigid sequence of screens. Each project progresses through:

**Prospect -> Client -> Planning -> Formation -> Registration -> Tax -> Banking -> Operational -> Completed**

Country and project-type templates may skip, repeat, or overlap phases. For example, banking preparation may begin during formation, and tax registration may run in parallel with banking. A phase is complete only when its exit policy is met; a percentage alone never advances a project.

Alongside phase, every project has an operating state:

- **Proposed**: scope is being evaluated.
- **Active**: work may progress.
- **Waiting**: progress depends on an identified party or external event.
- **Blocked**: progress cannot continue and intervention is required.
- **Paused**: the project is intentionally stopped and SLA clocks follow pause rules.
- **Cancelled**: work ended without the intended outcome.
- **Completed**: the contracted outcome and handoff are accepted.

The client-facing experience always presents current phase, last meaningful change, next action, responsible party, and predicted timeframe.

## 1. Prospect

**Goals**

- Understand the founder's objective, target jurisdiction, timing, eligibility, and risk.
- Determine whether Hajime can serve the request responsibly.
- Recommend an appropriate project type and package without implying guaranteed outcomes.

**Entry criteria**: A person or organization expresses a formation or expansion intent and permits Hajime to retain initial discovery information.

**Exit criteria**: The prospect is qualified, the proposed scope and assumptions are documented, required pre-engagement checks pass, and the prospect accepts or declines the offer.

**Possible blockers**: Unsupported jurisdiction or activity, unrealistic timeline, prohibited or high-risk industry, unclear beneficial ownership, unavailable specialist capacity, inability to establish a lawful route.

**Responsible parties**: Prospect, sales or intake coordinator, risk reviewer when required.

**Estimated duration**: Same day to 10 business days, depending on complexity and checks.

**Automation opportunities**: Structured intake, eligibility triage, duplicate detection, package recommendation, capacity check, follow-up scheduling, and proposal drafting. All adverse eligibility decisions require a review path.

## 2. Client

**Goals**

- Establish the client account, stakeholders, communication preferences, purchased scope, and accountable coordinator.
- Convert discovery assumptions into explicit project facts.
- Secure required consents and authorizations.

**Entry criteria**: Offer accepted or engagement approved; client identity and commercial scope are known.

**Exit criteria**: Client membership is established, package version is accepted, coordinator is assigned, required consents are recorded, and the project can be planned.

**Possible blockers**: Missing authority to engage, payment or contracting exception, unresolved identity mismatch, unaccepted terms, no coordinator capacity.

**Responsible parties**: Client representative, coordinator, commercial operations, risk or compliance reviewer.

**Estimated duration**: 1–5 business days.

**Automation opportunities**: Welcome sequence, role invitations, coordinator routing, workspace setup, consent collection, and package-to-workflow selection.

## 3. Planning

**Goals**

- Resolve the formation route, entity type, ownership, capital, location, immigration dependencies, and target dates.
- Instantiate a country-specific workflow from stable templates.
- Identify every requirement, partner capability, client action, and material risk before critical filings.

**Entry criteria**: Client setup complete and minimum discovery data available.

**Exit criteria**: Route decision approved, business profile sufficiently complete, workflow resolved, critical requirements identified, target timeline accepted, and required partner assignments initiated.

**Possible blockers**: Entity choice unresolved, ownership complexity, regulated activity, capital uncertainty, immigration dependency, location constraints, conflicting country rules, missing decision-maker.

**Responsible parties**: Coordinator accountable; client approves business decisions; legal, tax, and immigration partners advise within scope.

**Estimated duration**: 3–20 business days.

**Automation opportunities**: Requirement generation, workflow composition, conflict detection, timeline prediction, route comparison, partner matching, and risk flagging. Professional advice remains attributed to the responsible partner.

## 4. Formation

**Goals**

- Prepare formation instruments and complete prerequisites such as capital, address, officers, signatures, translations, and certifications.
- Produce an internally consistent, submission-ready formation packet.

**Entry criteria**: Approved route, entity facts, workflow, and responsible partners; all critical planning blockers resolved or formally accepted.

**Exit criteria**: Formation packet passes completion policy, required approvals are recorded, capital and office obligations are satisfied, and submission is accepted by the responsible filing party or agency.

**Possible blockers**: Name rejection, unavailable registered office, capital evidence issue, missing signature, expired identity document, translation or notarization delay, partner review delay, changing ownership.

**Responsible parties**: Client for decisions and evidence; coordinator for orchestration; attorney, company secretary, scrivener, filing service, or equivalent partner for professional work.

**Estimated duration**: 1–8 weeks, highly jurisdiction-dependent.

**Automation opportunities**: Packet readiness checks, document routing, signature sequencing, deadline reminders, version comparison, partner SLA monitoring, and status summaries.

## 5. Registration

**Goals**

- Obtain legal recognition, incorporation number, licenses, seals, registrations, or equivalent official artifacts.
- Track external submission and government review without manufacturing false precision.

**Entry criteria**: Submission-ready packet accepted and filing authority confirmed.

**Exit criteria**: Required agency decisions are received and verified; business legal identifiers and effective dates are recorded; rejection or remediation paths are closed.

**Possible blockers**: Government backlog, filing rejection, request for additional information, agency outage, holiday calendar, inconsistency between submitted records, physical appearance requirement.

**Responsible parties**: Government agency decides; filing partner responds; coordinator tracks and communicates; client supplies remediation evidence when needed.

**Estimated duration**: 3 business days to 12 weeks.

**Automation opportunities**: Submission acknowledgment capture, agency turnaround prediction, request-for-information routing, status polling where lawful integrations later exist, and client-safe progress updates.

## 6. Tax

**Goals**

- Complete mandatory tax registrations, elections, payroll or social registrations, and accountant handoff.
- Ensure the client understands recurring obligations and filing calendars.

**Entry criteria**: Required legal business identifiers are available; some preparatory tasks may start earlier.

**Exit criteria**: Mandatory registrations are accepted or filed, tax identifiers are captured, recurring obligations are assigned, and the operating accountant or responsible party accepts handoff.

**Possible blockers**: Missing incorporation evidence, unclear fiscal choices, permanent-establishment questions, payroll decisions, agency delay, unavailable accountant capacity.

**Responsible parties**: Accountant or tax partner; client for elections and operating facts; coordinator for handoff and schedule clarity.

**Estimated duration**: 1–8 weeks, often parallel with banking.

**Automation opportunities**: Applicability rules, registration packet assembly, deadline calendars, recurring task generation, handoff checklist, and obligation reminders.

## 7. Banking

**Goals**

- Prepare and submit a credible banking application and complete account-opening steps.
- Make dependencies, bank discretion, and client interview responsibilities explicit.

**Entry criteria**: Sufficient business, ownership, and formation evidence; chosen banking route; client and partner availability.

**Exit criteria**: Required account outcome is recorded, account details are securely handed off, or a documented alternate route is accepted.

**Possible blockers**: Bank risk decision, limited local presence, weak business evidence, beneficial-owner complexity, interview availability, address mismatch, translation need, request for additional evidence.

**Responsible parties**: Bank makes the decision; client owns interview and attestations; banking partner and coordinator prepare and route evidence.

**Estimated duration**: 2–12 weeks.

**Automation opportunities**: Readiness scoring, evidence reuse suggestions, interview preparation, request routing, application status summaries, and alternate-bank triggers. Approval must never be represented as guaranteed.

## 8. Operational

**Goals**

- Confirm the business can operate with required addresses, accounting, payroll, insurance, mail, records, and recurring compliance ownership.
- Transfer knowledge and credentials from formation mode into ongoing operations.

**Entry criteria**: Legal entity exists and minimum post-formation dependencies are known.

**Exit criteria**: Contracted operational services are active, credentials and records are delivered, recurring obligations have owners, and no launch-critical task remains open.

**Possible blockers**: Account not available, lease or insurance delay, missing payroll decision, incomplete tax setup, unclear ongoing owner, credentials not delivered securely.

**Responsible parties**: Client operating team, coordinator, accountant, insurance, office, payroll, mail, and other assigned partners.

**Estimated duration**: 1–6 weeks, often overlapping prior phases.

**Automation opportunities**: Operational readiness checklist, recurring obligation creation, credential handoff tracking, service activation reminders, and final gap analysis.

## 9. Completed

**Goals**

- Confirm the purchased outcome is delivered and understood.
- Provide a durable operating packet and preserve a complete audit record.
- Transition continuing obligations into a support or compliance project where applicable.

**Entry criteria**: All launch-critical milestones achieved or formally waived; deliverables assembled; remaining noncritical items assigned outside the formation scope.

**Exit criteria**: Client accepts handoff or acceptance window expires under package terms; project retrospective and partner measurements are recorded; project is closed to routine mutation.

**Possible blockers**: Unaccepted deliverable, unresolved critical issue, missing credential handoff, open partner invoice or assignment, disagreement about scope.

**Responsible parties**: Coordinator accountable; client accepts; admin closes commercial and operational records; partners complete final handoffs.

**Estimated duration**: 2–10 business days after operational readiness.

**Automation opportunities**: Completion policy evaluation, operating packet assembly, acceptance request, partner score capture, retrospective drafting, retention scheduling, and future-obligation project proposal.

## Cross-phase rules

### Phase progression

The workflow engine evaluates exit policies from authoritative facts. A coordinator may request an override only when the template permits it. Every override requires a reason, approver, scope, and expiration or permanent disposition.

### Parallel work

Phases may overlap when their tasks are ready. The project displays one primary current phase based on critical-path work and may display secondary active phases. Completion prediction uses task dependencies rather than phase order alone.

### Waiting versus blocked

- **Waiting on client, partner, or government** means the responsible party and expected response window are known.
- **Blocked** means the next valid action is unavailable, the responsible intervention is unclear or overdue, or a dependency has failed.

Waiting becomes blocked when an SLA or escalation policy is breached or when the expected event can no longer satisfy downstream dates.

### Pausing

A pause records scope, reason, initiator, effective time, affected SLA clocks, next review date, and client visibility. Independent work may continue if the pause scope allows it. Resumption re-evaluates requirements, document validity, partner availability, and predicted dates.

### Cancellation

Cancellation stops new work generation, revokes unnecessary partner access, closes or cancels assignments, preserves records, and creates a final disposition for open requirements and documents. It does not erase history.

### Duration estimates

Every estimate distinguishes:

- Controllable working time.
- Waiting time by responsible party.
- Government or external review range.
- Confidence level and assumptions.

Predicted completion is a range informed by critical-path tasks, calendars, current capacity, observed turnaround, and unresolved risk. Clients receive honest uncertainty rather than a false exact date.
