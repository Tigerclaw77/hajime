# Hajime Japan Business Model

## Company definition

Hajime Japan is a premium business launch concierge for international founders and companies establishing an operating presence in Japan.

Hajime does not replace attorneys, accountants, immigration professionals, banks, insurers, real-estate providers, translators, or government agencies. Hajime coordinates them. The company turns a fragmented, unfamiliar launch into one managed project with a clear plan, accountable owners, visible dependencies, and one trusted point of contact.

The customer buys confidence, orchestration, and reduced management burden. Professional advice and regulated services remain the responsibility of qualified partners under their own engagements and professional duties.

## Mission

Make starting and operating an international business feel clear, coordinated, and human.

For Japan, this means helping a founder move from intent to a launch-ready company without having to personally assemble and manage every specialist, deadline, document request, and handoff.

## Vision

Become the trusted operating layer for launching businesses across borders: one relationship that coordinates local experts, requirements, evidence, and execution in every supported market.

The long-term vision is not a global professional-services firm. It is a country-neutral concierge operating system supported by exceptional local partner networks.

## Core philosophy

### One accountable guide

Every client has a named coordinator who owns the clarity and momentum of the project. The coordinator may not own every professional decision, but always owns explaining who does, what happens next, and when the client should expect movement.

### Calm is a service outcome

Clients should never have to decode provider emails, reconcile separate checklists, or chase every party for updates. Hajime absorbs operational complexity and presents a truthful, concise view.

### No false certainty

Government, bank, licensing, immigration, and professional outcomes cannot be guaranteed. Hajime gives ranges, assumptions, dependencies, and early warning rather than promising dates or approvals it does not control.

### Specialists remain specialists

Hajime routes questions requiring licensed or regulated judgment to the appropriate professional. The coordinator organizes advice but does not impersonate it.

### Evidence before status

Progress reflects completed obligations and accepted work, not activity theater. “Submitted,” “under review,” and “approved” are distinct states.

### Premium means fewer burdens

Premium service is not more meetings or messages. It is better preparation, fewer repeated requests, faster resolution, stronger partners, and thoughtful intervention before a problem becomes a crisis.

## What Hajime sells

Hajime sells coordinated business-launch outcomes through project packages, optional service modules, and ongoing concierge retainers.

The core paid value includes:

- Launch planning and responsibility mapping.
- Project management across client and partner work.
- Partner sourcing, assignment, and handoff coordination.
- Document-request consolidation and readiness tracking.
- Timeline management and expectation setting.
- Risk, delay, and dependency escalation.
- Client preparation for professional, banking, and agency interactions.
- Launch handoff and ongoing-obligation coordination.

Partner professional fees, government charges, banking decisions, property costs, insurance premiums, translation fees, notarization, and other external costs may be included, passed through, or contracted separately depending on the package and engagement. The commercial arrangement must always make this explicit.

## What Hajime does not sell

Hajime does not sell legal opinions, immigration advice, tax advice, accounting opinions, audit services, regulated financial advice, banking approval, insurance advice, real-estate brokerage, or government decisions.

Hajime does not guarantee incorporation, visas, licenses, bank accounts, tax outcomes, government timing, or partner acceptance.

Hajime can coordinate these services and help the client prepare, but the qualified provider or relevant institution remains responsible for the underlying decision and professional work.

## Target customer

The primary customer is an international founder, owner-operator, or expansion leader who has decided Japan is strategically important and values time, clarity, and execution quality more than finding the lowest possible cost.

Priority customer groups include:

- Founder-led technology, professional-services, creative, and commerce businesses entering Japan.
- Profitable small and midsize companies opening a Japanese subsidiary or branch.
- Remote-first companies establishing a local operating presence.
- International entrepreneurs whose business formation has interconnected office, banking, tax, and immigration needs.
- Investors, accelerators, and corporate-development teams sponsoring repeat market entries.

Hajime should initially avoid customer categories whose needs exceed the partner network or operating controls, including highly regulated industries, unclear beneficial ownership, urgent “guaranteed” bank or visa requests, prohibited activities, and customers unwilling to disclose required facts.

## Ideal customer profile

An ideal client:

- Has a credible business purpose and a decision-maker available.
- Understands that launch is a project, not a single filing.
- Values a single coordinating relationship.
- Is willing to use qualified local professionals.
- Can provide transparent ownership, source-of-funds, and identity information.
- Has realistic capital and time expectations.
- Responds promptly to decisions and document requests.
- Prefers premium clarity over bargain shopping.
- Has future operational or multi-country needs that can deepen the relationship.

Signals of strong fit include prior international operations, outside investment, a clear market-entry thesis, a Japan-based customer or partner, a relocation plan tied to the business, or a management team that cannot personally coordinate the launch.

## Primary pain points

### Fragmentation

Customers must locate and manage separate attorneys, accountants, scriveners, translators, office providers, banks, insurers, and government processes. No one owns the whole journey.

### Unclear sequence

The client does not know which decisions must happen first, which tasks can run in parallel, or why one missing document delays several downstream steps.

### Language and context gaps

Even when individual providers communicate well, the customer must translate legal, operational, cultural, and administrative context across parties.

### Repeated evidence requests

Different parties request similar identity, ownership, business, and capital evidence in different formats, creating duplicate work and inconsistency.

### Uncertain timing

Government review, partner turnaround, and bank discretion make exact dates unreliable. Customers lack a credible range and early warning when assumptions change.

### Provider-selection risk

Customers have difficulty evaluating credentials, fit, availability, communication quality, and fair scope in an unfamiliar market.

### Founder distraction

The launch competes with product, sales, hiring, fundraising, and relocation. Coordination consumes high-value founder time.

### No operational handoff

Formation may finish without accounting, banking, insurance, mail, payroll, and recurring obligations having clear owners.

## Value proposition

**Hajime gives international founders one accountable launch team, one coordinated plan, and one clear answer to “what happens next?”**

Functional value:

- Fewer providers for the client to source and manage directly.
- Clear ownership, dependencies, and timelines.
- Better-prepared partner engagements and fewer avoidable handoff failures.
- Consolidated document and decision requests.
- Faster escalation when work stalls.
- A complete operational handoff rather than a filing-only outcome.

Emotional value:

- Confidence that nothing important is being quietly missed.
- Relief from managing unfamiliar administrative complexity.
- Trust in a named human who understands the whole project.
- Honest expectations without alarmist or legalistic communication.

Economic value:

- Founder and executive time returned to core business work.
- Lower risk of rework, missed deadlines, unsuitable providers, and avoidable delay.
- More predictable total launch effort and fewer surprise coordination costs.
- A reusable operating relationship for future services and countries.

## Why customers pay Hajime instead of doing it themselves

A determined founder can coordinate formation independently. Hajime does not claim otherwise. Customers pay because self-coordination has hidden costs:

- Learning which providers and processes apply.
- Comparing providers without reliable context.
- Managing multiple scopes, calendars, and communication styles.
- Detecting dependencies before they cause delays.
- Converting professional requests into an actionable founder checklist.
- Following up without knowing when escalation is appropriate.
- Maintaining consistent evidence across parties.
- Distinguishing normal waiting from a project at risk.
- Preparing an operational system after incorporation.

Hajime should win when the value of the client's time, the importance of the launch, and the cost of mistakes exceed the concierge fee. It should not pressure highly price-sensitive founders whose only need is a simple filing and who are comfortable managing it themselves.

## Revenue model

### Project packages

Fixed or scoped project fees cover planning, coordination, client service, partner management, and defined launch deliverables. Packages are the primary entry revenue.

### Service modules and add-ons

Optional modules cover work outside the package assumptions, such as complex translation coordination, additional founders, accelerated document recovery, multiple banking paths, licensing coordination, or expanded operational setup.

### Ongoing concierge

Monthly or annual recurring fees cover compliance-calendar coordination, accountant and provider handoffs, recurring registrations, officer or address changes, document renewals, and operational support. Hajime coordinates; qualified providers perform regulated work.

### Enterprise programs

Custom retainers, launch portfolios, and framework agreements support organizations with repeated formations, multiple countries, procurement needs, centralized reporting, and service-level commitments.

### Partner economics

Partner fees should be transparent. Hajime may charge coordination fees, negotiate wholesale arrangements, or receive disclosed referral compensation where lawful and ethically appropriate. Partner selection must never be distorted by undisclosed economics.

## Cost structure

Primary costs include:

- Coordinator and operations-team compensation.
- Partner-management and quality-assurance work.
- Professional and provider costs included in package scope.
- Client support and translation of operational communication.
- Business development and trusted referral channels.
- Insurance, compliance, contracts, and data handling.
- Eventually, software and automation supporting project throughput.

The key economic discipline is coordinator leverage: improving the number and complexity of projects one coordinator can manage without reducing clarity, response quality, or exception handling.

## Competitive alternatives

Customers may choose:

- Do-it-yourself research and direct provider management.
- A single law, accounting, or immigration firm.
- A formation agent focused on filings.
- A relocation provider focused on the founder's move.
- A large professional-services firm.
- Multiple independent specialists assembled by referral.

Hajime should not compete by claiming deeper legal or tax expertise than specialists. It competes by integrating the full launch, making work legible, selecting fit-for-purpose partners, and remaining accountable across organizational boundaries.

## Competitive advantages

### End-to-end coordination

Most providers optimize their own scope. Hajime manages the dependencies between scopes and carries the client through operational readiness.

### Curated, measured partner network

Partners are selected for capability, availability, communication, turnaround, and fit. Performance is reviewed from real assignments rather than directory claims.

### Premium client experience

One coordinator, concise updates, consolidated requests, prepared meetings, and proactive escalation create a materially different experience from email-driven service delivery.

### Process intelligence

Over time, Hajime learns realistic durations, common blockers, evidence reuse patterns, and partner capacity across project types. That operating knowledge improves planning and intervention.

### Country-neutral method

The coordination model can expand without pretending professional rules are universal. Each country receives its own validated services, boundaries, requirements, and partner network.

### Trust through boundaries

Clear statements about what Hajime does not do increase credibility. The business escalates professional questions rather than answering beyond competence.

## Success measures

Business health should be measured through:

- Qualified-lead to engagement conversion.
- Gross margin by package and project type.
- Coordinator capacity adjusted for complexity.
- Time to launch and prediction accuracy.
- Client action turnaround and partner turnaround.
- Preventable rework and escalation rates.
- Client confidence, referral, and renewal.
- Partner quality and capacity resilience.
- Ongoing-support attachment rate.
- Percentage of completed projects with a clean operational handoff.

Raw project volume and task completion are not sufficient if clients remain confused or coordinators are overloaded.

## Future expansion strategy

### Stage 1: Prove Japan

Validate one premium, repeatable formation journey with a narrow ideal customer profile, curated partners, disciplined package boundaries, and strong referrals. Learn which work is truly repeatable and which requires judgment.

### Stage 2: Deepen Japan

Add ongoing compliance coordination, operational changes, repeat founder services, enterprise reporting, and carefully selected project variants. Build redundancy in every critical partner category.

### Stage 3: Add one country at a time

Choose markets with strong customer overlap, predictable service demand, available partners, and a manageable regulatory perimeter. Singapore, UAE, Portugal, Estonia, and the United States are candidates, not promises.

Each country launch requires:

- Defined service boundaries and local counsel review.
- Country-specific workflow and requirement manual.
- Curated partner coverage with redundancy.
- Tested package economics and duration assumptions.
- Local communication, calendar, and escalation standards.
- A limited pilot before general availability.

### Stage 4: Multi-country programs

Offer one client relationship for coordinated launches across multiple markets. Central reporting and consistent service standards become the enterprise value, while local partners remain responsible for jurisdiction-specific professional work.

## Strategic guardrails

1. Hajime remains a coordinator and concierge, not an unlicensed professional-services substitute.
2. Growth does not outrun partner quality, coordinator capacity, or boundary controls.
3. Country expansion follows demonstrated demand and operating readiness, not a long country list on a website.
4. Packages remain simple; complexity appears as explicit modules or custom enterprise scope.
5. Partner economics are disclosed and never override client fit.
6. Premium pricing funds preparation, communication, redundancy, and intervention.
7. Software eventually supports the operating model; it does not define the business before the service is proven.
