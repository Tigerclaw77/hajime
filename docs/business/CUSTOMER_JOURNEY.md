# Hajime Japan Customer Journey

## Experience promise

At every active stage, the customer should be able to answer:

- Where are we now?
- What happened most recently?
- What happens next?
- Who is responsible?
- When should I expect movement?
- Is anything at risk?

The customer should not need to interpret internal task volume or provider jargon. Hajime absorbs complexity and communicates the smallest truthful set of facts needed for confidence and action.

## 1. Visitor

### Customer mindset

“I am considering Japan, but I do not yet understand what launching there really involves.”

### Questions

- Is Hajime relevant to my kind of business?
- Is this a filing service, law firm, or consultancy?
- How much support can Hajime provide?
- What is a realistic timeline?
- Can Hajime help with banking, office, accounting, and professional introductions?

### Concerns

The visitor fears hidden complexity, unclear credibility, aggressive selling, and being routed into a generic form without understanding the service.

### Expected emotions

Curiosity mixed with uncertainty. The desired movement is from confusion to recognition: “This is the kind of coordination I need.”

### Hajime responsibilities

- State plainly that Hajime is a premium business launch concierge.
- Explain what coordination includes and what professional services remain external.
- Present packages simply.
- Avoid guaranteed timing, approval, or all-inclusive claims.
- Offer a clear route to a fit conversation or paid Blueprint.

### Partner responsibilities

None at the visitor stage. Partner logos, claims, or endorsements should be used only with permission and should not imply guaranteed access or outcomes.

### Automation opportunities

Basic fit routing, language preference, content relevance, appointment scheduling, and clear acknowledgment. Avoid gimmicky personalization or automated professional advice.

### Success signal

The right visitor understands the category and chooses to inquire. The wrong-fit visitor self-selects out without confusion.

## 2. Lead

### Customer mindset

“I have a real plan or problem. I need to know whether Hajime can help.”

### Questions

- Will someone understand my situation?
- How quickly will I hear back?
- What information is safe and necessary to share now?
- Am I too early, too late, or too complex?

### Concerns

The lead worries about being ignored, overexposing sensitive information, receiving a generic sales pitch, or learning that the desired date is unrealistic.

### Expected emotions

Hopeful but guarded. The desired movement is from uncertainty to a clear next conversation or respectful referral.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Acknowledge the inquiry and set a response expectation.
- Gather only useful initial facts.
- Protect sensitive information and avoid unnecessary document requests.
- Qualify respectfully and identify obvious professional or risk needs.
- Explain if Hajime is not the appropriate provider.

### Partner responsibilities

No partner contact without consent. A professional may support internal fit review only under appropriate confidentiality and data limits.

### Automation opportunities

Intake normalization, missing-fact prompts, response routing, duplicate detection, and follow-up scheduling. Adverse qualification remains reviewable.

### Success signal

The lead receives a timely, specific next step and does not have to repeat basic context.

## 3. Client Decision

### Customer mindset

“I believe Japan is important. I am deciding whether Hajime is worth the premium and whether I can trust it with the launch.”

### Questions

- What exactly will Hajime own?
- Which work belongs to me and which belongs to partners?
- What could change the price or timeline?
- Are external fees included?
- What outcomes are not guaranteed?
- Who will be my coordinator?

### Concerns

The prospect fears vague scope, surprise fees, being passed between providers, sales promises that operations cannot keep, and paying a premium for a glorified referral list.

### Expected emotions

Analytical and cautious. The desired movement is from evaluation to informed commitment.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Diagnose before proposing.
- Recommend the simplest adequate package.
- Show the coordination burden Hajime removes.
- State assumptions, exclusions, third-party costs, and no-guarantee boundaries.
- Answer commercial and process questions directly.
- Route legal, tax, accounting, immigration, or other professional questions.

### Partner responsibilities

Support scoped pre-engagement questions when necessary, disclose their own engagement requirements, and avoid bypassing Hajime's agreed client experience.

### Automation opportunities

Discovery summaries, scope drafting, package-fit suggestions, proposal preparation, and reminder scheduling. A person approves scope and pricing.

### Success signal

The client can explain what they are buying, what remains external, and why the package fits.

## 4. Onboarding

### Customer mindset

“I have committed. Show me that Hajime is organized and that I made the right decision.”

### Questions

- Who is my coordinator?
- What do you need from me first?
- Where will decisions and documents live?
- When will I see the plan?
- Will I have to repeat everything from sales?

### Concerns

The new client fears handoff friction, a flood of forms, silence after payment, unclear priorities, and repeated requests.

### Expected emotions

Excited, exposed, and impatient for evidence of progress. The desired movement is from commitment to control.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Introduce the named coordinator.
- Demonstrate knowledge of discovery and purchased scope.
- Confirm stakeholders, decision authority, communication preferences, and boundaries.
- Ask only for the minimum facts needed to plan.
- Present the immediate next action and onboarding timetable.
- Identify sensitive evidence that should wait for a secure, purpose-specific request.

### Partner responsibilities

Accept or decline proposed assignments promptly, state prerequisites, and coordinate first meetings through the agreed handoff.

### Automation opportunities

Welcome sequence, stakeholder invitations, intake prefill, coordinator routing, consent collection, and kickoff-agenda preparation.

### Success signal

Within the first interaction, the client knows their coordinator, next action, expected effort, and planning date.

## 5. Planning

### Customer mindset

“I need Hajime to turn my idea into a credible route and tell me which decisions matter now.”

### Questions

- Which entity and launch route fit my goals?
- Which questions require an attorney, accountant, or immigration professional?
- What can happen in parallel?
- Which office, capital, and ownership choices affect everything else?
- What is the realistic completion range?

### Concerns

The client fears making an irreversible decision with incomplete context, being trapped in meetings, or seeing the target date slip before work has begun.

### Expected emotions

Engaged but cognitively loaded. The desired movement is from many possibilities to an approved, understandable plan.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Prepare decision briefs and professional questions.
- Coordinate specialist input without presenting it as Hajime advice.
- Build the responsibility map, dependencies, and timing range.
- Explain uncertainty and identify the critical client decisions.
- Confirm package fit and surface added scope early.

### Partner responsibilities

Provide timely, scoped professional analysis; identify missing facts; state assumptions and limitations; and translate conclusions into usable next requirements.

### Automation opportunities

Requirement drafting, route comparison, missing-fact detection, timeline generation, partner matching, and risk flagging. Material route decisions remain human-approved.

### Success signal

The client approves a plan they understand and can see exactly what must happen next.

## 6. Execution

### Customer mindset

“I want the plan to move without having to manage every provider.”

### Questions

- What is happening now?
- What is waiting on me, Hajime, a partner, or government?
- Are we still on track?
- Why is this document needed again?
- When should I intervene?

### Concerns

The client fears silence, duplicated work, unexplained status changes, surprise urgency, and discovering delays too late.

### Expected emotions

Alternating confidence and anxiety as visible activity gives way to normal external waiting. The desired state is calm awareness with focused action only when needed.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Maintain one truthful current/next/owner/timeframe view.
- Consolidate requests and explain purpose.
- Prepare and monitor partner handoffs.
- Distinguish active work, waiting, and blocked status.
- Escalate before critical impact where possible.
- Send meaningful updates, not activity noise.
- Reset expectations promptly when assumptions change.

### Partner responsibilities

Accept clear scopes, meet response and delivery standards, protect client data, communicate blockers early, request only necessary information, and return usable deliverables.

### Automation opportunities

Task readiness, evidence reuse suggestions, reminders, timeline updates, SLA warnings, document quality assistance, and status drafting.

### Success signal

The client completes their actions on time, partners have what they need, and no critical delay appears as a surprise.

## 7. Launch

### Customer mindset

“The company exists. Can it actually operate, and what am I responsible for now?”

### Questions

- Which registrations and accounts are complete?
- What remains pending?
- Who owns accounting, banking, insurance, mail, payroll, and compliance?
- Where are the final records and credentials?
- What should I expect in the first 30, 60, and 90 days?

### Concerns

The client fears a ceremonial “done” that leaves operational gaps, inaccessible records, missing recurring deadlines, or providers who assume someone else owns the next step.

### Expected emotions

Relief and excitement, with a new need for practical confidence. The desired movement is from formation completion to operating readiness.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Verify package completion conditions.
- Coordinate operational provider handoffs.
- Assemble the operating packet and responsibility calendar.
- Identify unresolved items and accepted risks.
- Conduct a launch review and secure client acceptance.
- Offer appropriate ongoing support without implying it is mandatory.

### Partner responsibilities

Deliver final work, identifiers, instructions, and recurring obligations; confirm the future service owner; and close access and assignments appropriately.

### Automation opportunities

Readiness assessment, handoff packet preparation, recurring-date creation, credential inventory, and completion summary drafting.

### Success signal

Every launch-critical item is complete, waived, or assigned with a date and responsible party.

## 8. Ongoing Support

### Customer mindset

“I want to run the business without rebuilding the provider network every time something changes.”

### Questions

- Who do I contact when an operational issue arises?
- Which obligations are coming next?
- Does this change require an accountant, attorney, insurer, or filing specialist?
- Are my providers still performing well?
- Can Hajime support another entity or country?

### Concerns

The client fears forgotten deadlines, fragmented provider relationships returning, subscription fees without visible value, and unclear boundaries on support requests.

### Expected emotions

Stable and focused. The desired state is dependable background confidence with timely intervention.

### Hajime responsibilities

- Maintain current business, provider, and obligation context.
- Triage requests and route professional questions.
- Coordinate recurring obligations and provider handoffs.
- Provide periodic operating reviews and concise digests.
- Scope material projects transparently.
- Prepare clean handoff if the client does not renew.

### Partner responsibilities

Maintain professional relationships, meet recurring service standards, communicate changing requirements, and provide renewal or scope information early.

### Automation opportunities

Recurring reminders, request classification, provider routing, document-expiry monitoring, obligation calendars, and periodic summaries.

### Success signal

The client renews because Hajime continues removing real coordination burden, not because leaving feels dangerous.

## Service recovery journey

When something goes wrong, the customer experience follows:

1. **Acknowledge**: confirm the issue and the immediate owner.
2. **Stabilize**: protect deadlines, evidence, access, and alternate routes.
3. **Explain**: distinguish fact, uncertainty, responsibility, and impact without blame.
4. **Act**: present the remediation plan, next decision, and timeframe.
5. **Update**: communicate at the agreed cadence until stable.
6. **Review**: document root cause, prevention, and any commercial remedy.

Hajime should not disappear while waiting for a partner response. The coordinator remains accountable for communication even when another party owns the remedy.

## Customer-experience standards

- One named coordinator and a clear backup.
- No repeated request without explaining why prior evidence is insufficient or why another consumer must accept it.
- No unexplained status label.
- No external deadline or approval represented as guaranteed.
- No professional conclusion presented as Hajime's own.
- No client action without purpose, responsible deadline, and consequence.
- No major scope or timeline change buried in a routine update.
- No launch marked complete without an operational handoff.
- No renewal pressure based on fear.
- No customer should have to ask “what happens next?” twice.
