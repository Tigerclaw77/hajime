# Hajime Japan Disclaimers and Service Boundaries

## Status of this document

This is an internal product and operations policy. It is not customer terms, a legal opinion, or a substitute for Japanese counsel.

Before Hajime trades, Japanese counsel should review the actual business model, marketing, contracts, referral economics, partner relationships, staff scripts, document handling, and customer-facing copy. Boundary review must be repeated whenever Hajime adds a service, country, partner category, or automation.

The conservative operating rule is simple: when an activity could be reserved to a licensed or registered professional, Hajime coordinates the customer to that professional and does not perform or characterize the reserved service itself.

## Core identity

Hajime is a premium business launch concierge.

Hajime:

- Plans and coordinates cross-provider projects.
- Clarifies tasks, responsibilities, dependencies, and timing.
- Sources and introduces qualified partners.
- Prepares clients and partners for efficient handoffs.
- Consolidates administrative requests.
- Tracks documents, decisions, submissions, and external waiting.
- Communicates project status and risk.
- Coordinates launch and ongoing operational handoffs.

Hajime is not:

- A law firm.
- An immigration consultancy or authorized immigration representative unless a separate appropriately authorized entity and engagement are established.
- An accounting or tax advisory firm.
- A bank, payment service, financial adviser, or lender.
- An insurance broker, agent, or adviser.
- A real-estate brokerage or relocation company.
- A translator, notary, judicial scrivener, or government filing professional unless the work is separately delivered by an appropriately qualified partner.
- A government agency or a representative of one.

## Authoritative perimeter references

The following references support the need for a conservative partner-led model and should be revalidated by counsel against the Japanese text and Hajime's actual conduct:

- Japan's Attorneys Act restricts compensated handling of legal services by non-attorneys and restricts non-attorneys from using attorney or law-office descriptions. See the [Japanese Law Translation Attorneys Act, Article 72 and Article 74](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/4610/tb).
- Japan's National Tax Agency states that tax representation, preparation of tax documents, and tax consultation are services of certified public tax accountants and qualifying professional corporations, including that these services must not be performed by others even without charge. See the [National Tax Agency overview](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/01.htm).
- The Ministry of Justice describes judicial scriveners as professionals connected to registration and deposit application procedures. See the [Ministry of Justice overview of judicial scriveners](https://www.moj.go.jp/EN/MINJI/shihou.html).
- The Immigration Services Agency describes defined residence procedures and an application-intermediary system for persons recognized under that system. See the [Immigration Services Agency application procedures](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/index.html) and [application-intermediary overview](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/nyuukokukanri07_00262.html).
- Japan's Financial Services Agency describes insurance solicitation as agency or intermediation for concluding insurance contracts and warns about unlicensed insurance activity. See the [Insurance Business Act translation](https://www.fsa.go.jp/common/law/ins01.pdf) and [FSA insurance information](https://www.fsa.go.jp/user/hoken.html).

These citations do not establish Hajime's complete legal perimeter. Counsel must also assess company, administrative-scrivener, banking, payment, real-estate, employment-placement, privacy, consumer-protection, advertising, referral-fee, and other rules relevant to actual operations.

## What Hajime does

### Project planning

Hajime gathers operational facts, documents client goals, identifies decisions, builds a timeline, maps dependencies, and prepares questions for qualified professionals.

Hajime may describe common project phases and administrative requirements when the description is approved, current, and clearly informational. It does not make a legal determination about which entity, visa, election, license, or filing the client should choose.

### Partner introduction

Hajime identifies the type of professional or provider needed, evaluates fit and availability, introduces candidates, prepares context, and coordinates the engagement.

The client and partner establish any required direct professional relationship. The partner owns conflict clearance, advice, representation, professional deliverables, and duties to the client.

### Administrative coordination

Hajime may request information identified by a responsible partner, organize documents, schedule meetings, record status, maintain a responsibility map, and relay administrative communications accurately.

Hajime does not decide that evidence is legally sufficient, give substantive answers on behalf of a professional, sign for the client, or submit an application unless counsel has approved the activity and the submitting party is properly authorized.

### Status and timeline management

Hajime may report verified facts such as requested, received, under review, submitted, accepted, rejected, or completed. It may provide planning ranges with assumptions and confidence.

Hajime does not present an internal percentage as evidence of legal completion or guarantee a partner, agency, bank, visa, license, tax, or insurance outcome.

### Client preparation

Hajime may help the client organize questions, business facts, meeting agendas, document indexes, and operational narratives. It may explain what a partner requested in plain process language after confirming substantive meaning where necessary.

Hajime does not coach a client to misstate facts, conceal ownership or funds, manipulate eligibility, or provide an answer designed to produce a preferred regulated outcome.

## What Hajime does not do

### Legal services

Hajime does not:

- Give legal advice or legal opinions.
- Select a legal structure for the client as a legal conclusion.
- Draft, revise, or approve legal documents as legal work.
- Represent the client in a legal matter or dispute.
- Interpret laws for the client's specific facts.
- Negotiate or settle legal rights.
- Describe itself as an attorney, law office, or equivalent.

Hajime coordinates a qualified attorney, judicial scrivener, administrative scrivener, notary, or other appropriate professional as counsel determines.

### Immigration services

Hajime does not:

- Determine immigration eligibility.
- Recommend a residence status as professional advice.
- Prepare or submit immigration applications as an unauthorized intermediary.
- Represent the client before immigration authorities.
- Guarantee a visa, status, or processing time.

Hajime can collect operational context, schedule a qualified immigration professional, track client actions, and coordinate evidence requested by that professional.

### Tax and accounting services

Hajime does not:

- Provide tax consultation or tax planning.
- Calculate tax liability as advice.
- Prepare or submit tax returns or tax documents.
- Act as tax representative.
- Provide an accounting opinion, audit, certification, or bookkeeping unless separately and lawfully structured.
- Recommend an election or treatment as Hajime's conclusion.

Hajime can prepare an intake, coordinate a certified public tax accountant or accountant, track requested documents and deadlines, and record the provider's approved responsibility map.

### Banking and financial services

Hajime does not:

- Act as a bank or lender.
- Guarantee account opening, credit, payment access, or timing.
- Provide investment, financing, or regulated financial advice.
- Hold or transmit client funds except through a separately reviewed and lawful commercial process.
- Tell a client how to characterize facts to influence due diligence.
- Represent an introduction as bank endorsement or preferred treatment.

Hajime can compare operational banking paths using approved factual criteria, prepare the client, coordinate introductions, organize evidence, and route requests.

### Insurance

Hajime does not solicit, recommend, arrange, bind, or advise on insurance unless counsel confirms that the exact activity is permitted and appropriately licensed.

Hajime can identify that insurance coordination may be needed, gather business facts, introduce a licensed broker, agent, or insurer, schedule communication, and track quote and activation status. The licensed provider owns suitability and solicitation.

### Real estate, office, and relocation

Hajime does not broker real property, negotiate leases, provide property advice, or conduct relocation activity requiring a license.

Hajime can define operational needs, introduce qualified office or property providers, compare approved factual service terms, schedule viewings or calls, and track the client's selection. Legal or regulatory suitability of an address routes to the appropriate professional.

### Translation and certification

Hajime does not certify translation accuracy, notarize documents, issue apostilles, or claim a translation meets an agency's standard unless the qualified provider or receiving party confirms it.

Hajime can coordinate qualified translators and certification providers, control source versions, track deliverables, and route acceptance.

### Government and external outcomes

Hajime does not act as a government agency, influence an agency decision, or guarantee incorporation, registration, a license, tax treatment, visa, bank account, insurance, lease, or published timing.

Hajime reports the latest verified status, coordinates responses, and updates expectations.

## Partner responsibilities

Every partner is responsible for:

- Working within valid capability, license, authority, and engagement.
- Conducting required conflicts and client acceptance.
- Giving their own professional advice and decisions directly or in attributable form.
- Stating assumptions, limitations, fees, and timing.
- Requesting only information needed for the assigned purpose.
- Protecting client data and respecting access limits.
- Reporting blockers, errors, and changed expectations promptly.
- Maintaining their own records, insurance, and regulatory duties.
- Correcting partner-caused errors under the engagement.

Hajime coordination does not supervise or control independent professional judgment.

## Client responsibilities

The client is responsible for:

- Providing truthful, complete, current information.
- Disclosing ownership, control, business activity, source of funds, and other requested facts.
- Reviewing and approving their own decisions and attestations.
- Reading professional advice and asking the responsible professional questions.
- Meeting action and payment deadlines.
- Protecting credentials and notifying Hajime of changes.
- Obtaining internal corporate approval and signer authority.
- Following laws and professional instructions applicable to their business.
- Understanding that external outcomes and timing remain discretionary.

Hajime should not silently absorb a client responsibility. If the client cannot perform it, the coordinator identifies a lawful support path or pauses the work.

## Hajime responsibilities

Hajime is responsible for:

- Delivering the contracted coordination service with reasonable care.
- Maintaining a named coordinator and backup.
- Keeping responsibilities and next actions clear.
- Selecting partners under the stated process.
- Sharing information only for approved purposes.
- Escalating material blockers and boundary questions.
- Communicating known changes promptly.
- Correcting Hajime-caused coordination errors.
- Maintaining accurate records of status, decisions, and handoffs.
- Avoiding misleading marketing and unsupported claims.

Hajime remains accountable for its coordination even when another party owns the substantive outcome.

## No-guarantee framework

Customer copy should distinguish:

- **Hajime commitment**: an action or response Hajime controls.
- **Partner estimate**: a professional or provider range based on accepted scope and complete inputs.
- **External range**: government, bank, insurer, landlord, or other discretionary timing.
- **Target date**: a planning objective dependent on stated assumptions.
- **Outcome**: only complete after authoritative confirmation.

Avoid “guaranteed,” “approved,” “compliant,” “eligible,” “will qualify,” “bank-ready,” or “complete” unless the proper authority and context support the statement.

Preferred language:

- “We will coordinate the filing with your judicial scrivener.”
- “Your attorney will advise on the appropriate route.”
- “The bank makes the account-opening decision.”
- “The current government review range is an estimate, not a commitment.”
- “This document has been received; the named reviewer has not yet accepted it.”

## Liability-allocation principles

Actual contractual limitations must be drafted and validated by counsel. The business intent is to allocate responsibility fairly and transparently, not to disclaim all accountability.

Engagement terms should address:

- Defined Hajime service and standard of care.
- Client truthfulness, decisions, approvals, deadlines, and lawful conduct.
- Independent partner responsibility and direct professional engagements.
- External discretion and uncontrollable delay.
- No guarantee of legal, immigration, tax, bank, insurance, government, property, or commercial outcome.
- Reliance on client and partner information.
- Scope-change and pause consequences.
- Confidentiality, data handling, document retention, and approved sharing.
- Third-party fees, referral economics, and payment authority.
- Process for claims, remediation, limitation, indemnity, and dispute resolution where lawful.
- Exclusions that cannot lawfully be limited.

Operations must never use contractual limitation as a reason to ignore a preventable Hajime failure or avoid service recovery.

## Escalation to licensed professionals

### Mandatory escalation triggers

Escalate before answering when a client asks:

- Which legal entity, contract, filing, license, or legal position applies to their facts.
- Whether they qualify for a visa or residence status.
- How a transaction, salary, capital contribution, expense, or structure will be taxed.
- Whether a document is legally sufficient or compliant.
- How to prepare or submit a professional or government filing.
- Which insurance coverage is suitable.
- Whether an address, lease, or property arrangement is legally adequate.
- How to respond to a rejection, dispute, investigation, notice, or regulator.
- How to structure ownership, control, funds, or activity to obtain approval.

### Escalation response

1. Acknowledge the question and explain why a qualified professional should answer it.
2. Identify the appropriate professional capability.
3. Prepare the known facts and precise question with client consent.
4. Arrange the introduction or assignment.
5. Record the professional's attributable response.
6. Convert only the approved operational consequences into project tasks.

### When unsure

Do not improvise. Pause the affected action and escalate to the operations lead and counsel or relevant professional. “I need to confirm the right expert for that question” is an acceptable concierge response.

## Marketing and UI copy rules

### Approved identity language

- Premium business launch concierge.
- Launch coordinator.
- One accountable guide across your launch.
- We coordinate qualified local partners.
- Clear plan, responsibilities, documents, and handoffs.

### High-risk language requiring review

- We form your company.
- We handle your visa.
- We get your bank account.
- We ensure compliance.
- Legal or tax experts at Hajime.
- Guaranteed launch date.
- Pre-approved banking.
- Official partner of a government agency.
- All-inclusive.

### Status copy

Use responsibility-aware states:

- Waiting on you.
- Waiting on Hajime.
- Waiting on partner.
- Waiting on government or external review.
- Under professional review.
- Submitted by your filing partner.
- Accepted by the named recipient.

Do not use a green check to imply legal acceptance when the system only knows a file was uploaded.

### Calls to action

Client actions should state purpose and authority:

- “Review the office options prepared by Hajime.”
- “Upload the passport copy requested by your attorney.”
- “Confirm your capital decision with your accountant.”

Avoid language that suggests Hajime is making the professional decision.

## Complaints, claims, and incidents

Any threatened legal claim, regulator contact, professional complaint, suspected unauthorized practice, data incident, fraud concern, or serious misleading-sales allegation moves immediately to restricted escalation. Staff should preserve records, avoid speculative admissions, stop affected activity where needed, and follow counsel-led communication.

Routine service complaints remain owned by operations and receive fact-based remediation. The presence of a liability limitation does not replace respectful service recovery.

## Boundary governance

- Maintain an approved question-and-response library for staff.
- Train sales, coordinators, support, and partner managers on role-specific boundaries.
- Sample client communications and calls.
- Record and review boundary escalations.
- Require counsel review for new services, countries, referral models, marketing claims, and automation.
- Restrict or pause any service whose lawful delivery path is unclear.
- Test whether the real customer experience matches the written disclaimer; a footer cannot cure contradictory conduct.

## Non-negotiables

1. Hajime is always described as coordinator and concierge.
2. Professional advice is attributable to the qualified professional.
3. External decisions and timing are never guaranteed.
4. Client and partner responsibilities are stated before work begins.
5. Purpose-specific information sharing replaces broad access.
6. Sales cannot promise around operating boundaries.
7. Staff escalate when the right answer requires licensed judgment.
8. Customer terms and marketing receive Japanese counsel review before use.
9. Disclaimers do not excuse poor coordination or misleading conduct.
10. What Hajime actually does must remain inside the boundary its words describe.
