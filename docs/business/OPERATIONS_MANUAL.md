# Hajime Japan Operations Manual

## Operating objective

Hajime operations make a complex launch feel controlled without hiding uncertainty. The coordinator is accountable for project clarity, momentum, and handoffs. Specialists remain accountable for their professional work, clients for their decisions and evidence, banks and agencies for their outcomes.

The operating standard is not “everything went according to plan.” It is “the right person knew what to do, emerging risk was visible early, and the client was never left guessing.”

## Roles

### Coordinator

Owns the client relationship, integrated project plan, action clarity, partner handoffs, status communication, dependency management, and first-line escalation.

### Senior coordinator or operations lead

Supports complex planning, workload balancing, escalations, service recovery, scope interpretation, and coordinator coaching.

### Partner manager

Owns partner sourcing, onboarding, capability and capacity records, relationship health, performance review, incident follow-up, and replacement readiness.

### Quality reviewer

Checks defined project gates, client-facing deliverables, boundary compliance, evidence of completion, and consistency with service standards. The reviewer does not replace professional approval.

### Sales or account owner

Owns commercial qualification, proposal, engagement, scope changes, renewals, and enterprise relationship. Operations owns delivery truth and must approve material feasibility assumptions.

### Admin and risk owner

Owns workspace-level access, restricted exceptions, retention, serious incidents, partner suspension, and business-risk escalation.

## Coordinator capacity

Capacity is based on project complexity, active effort, deadlines, client responsiveness, partner count, language, and escalation load, not raw project count.

Every coordinator has:

- A defined active-work capacity range.
- A lower complex-project limit.
- Backup coverage.
- A visible upcoming-start forecast.
- Protected time for proactive review and escalation.

No coordinator should be scheduled to full theoretical capacity. Concierge quality depends on room for exceptions.

## Project activation

A project becomes active only when:

- Engagement and initial payment requirements are complete.
- Package, add-ons, assumptions, and exclusions are documented.
- Client decision-maker and communication preferences are known.
- Coordinator and backup are assigned.
- Sales handoff is complete and internally understood.
- Immediate risk or professional-review needs are identified.
- First client action and kickoff timing are ready.

The coordinator reviews the sales packet before contacting the client. The client should not repeat information because Hajime failed to hand it over.

## Coordinator workflow

### 1. Orient

Understand the desired outcome, purchased scope, people, business, timing, risks, existing providers, promises, and open professional questions.

### 2. Plan

Prepare the integrated launch plan, requirements, milestones, responsibility map, partner needs, target range, assumptions, and client decision sequence.

### 3. Align

Confirm the plan with the client and relevant partners. Resolve conflicting requirements, owner ambiguity, and package gaps before critical work begins.

### 4. Activate

Start only ready work. Assign partners with complete briefs. Give the client focused requests rather than the entire future checklist.

### 5. Monitor

Track active work, waiting party, expected response, critical path, document readiness, partner SLA, government range, and client action.

### 6. Intervene

Address missing input, stale ownership, conflicting advice, rejected evidence, capacity risk, and timing drift before downstream impact grows.

### 7. Communicate

Keep the client and partners informed at the appropriate level. Every update states current status, meaningful change, next action, owner, and timeframe.

### 8. Verify and hand off

Confirm completion evidence, record continuing obligations, deliver the operating packet, obtain client acceptance, and transition ongoing work cleanly.

### 9. Learn

Record actual durations, avoidable rework, partner performance, root causes, client feedback, and template improvements.

## Daily coordinator responsibilities

### Start-of-day review

Review, in order:

1. Critical deadlines and high-impact blockers.
2. Client actions due or overdue.
3. Partner SLA warnings and unanswered escalations.
4. Government or external events received.
5. Tasks newly ready for assignment.
6. Projects with changed completion prediction or health.
7. Meetings and commitments due that day.
8. Projects without a meaningful update inside the expected cadence.

The coordinator identifies the day's intervention list before responding to routine messages.

### During the day

- Prepare client and partner meetings.
- Route complete, purpose-specific briefs.
- Consolidate document and decision requests.
- Confirm ownership and deadlines after every material handoff.
- Record professional decisions with attribution.
- Update project facts and timelines when assumptions change.
- Escalate before a committed date is missed where possible.
- Protect focus time for planning and complex review.

### End-of-day review

- Confirm every urgent message has an owner and next action.
- Record material decisions and commitments.
- Update blocked or waiting states honestly.
- Set the next review date for external waits.
- Brief backup coverage on critical overnight or absence-sensitive items.
- Do not leave a client action marked urgent if the client has not been told.

## Weekly operating rhythm

### Project health review

Each active project is reviewed for:

- Current phase and critical path.
- Next client action and effort.
- Partner-owned work and SLA risk.
- Government waiting range.
- Document expiry or rejection risk.
- Target-date confidence.
- Scope or package mismatch.
- Upcoming professional decision.
- Last meaningful client update.

### Coordinator portfolio review

The operations lead reviews capacity, blocked work, overdue tasks, near-completion projects, new starts, absence coverage, and repeated exception patterns. Rebalance before overload becomes visible to clients.

### Partner bottleneck review

The partner manager and operations lead review affected projects, root cause, alternate capacity, escalation state, and reassignment threshold.

### Quality calibration

Review a sample of project plans, client updates, partner briefs, evidence handoffs, completion packets, and boundary decisions. Use examples to align judgment without turning the meeting into status reporting.

## Client updates

### Standard update structure

1. **Overall status**: On track, at risk, waiting on external review, or blocked.
2. **What changed**: Only meaningful progress or changed expectation.
3. **Your action**: One prioritized list with purpose, due date, and expected effort.
4. **Hajime and partner work**: What is active and who owns it.
5. **What happens next**: The event that unlocks the next step and expected range.

### Cadence

- Concierge clients receive the agreed proactive cadence, typically weekly during active execution and event-driven updates for material changes.
- Guided clients receive scheduled checkpoints and action-driven updates.
- Enterprise programs follow the governance cadence.
- External waiting does not justify silence. Send a short confirmation at the agreed review point even if nothing changed.

### Communication rules

- Do not forward a confusing partner email without context.
- Do not say “in progress” when work is waiting on someone.
- Do not provide a new exact completion date when only a range is defensible.
- Do not bury a blocker below routine completions.
- Do not expose internal partner performance or blame.
- Do not translate professional advice in a way that changes meaning; confirm material summaries with the partner.

## Partner communications

Every partner handoff includes:

- Client and project reference.
- Defined scope and desired outcome.
- Why the partner was selected.
- Known facts and assumptions.
- Questions requiring the partner's authority.
- Required inputs and approved document access.
- Deliverables and acceptance standard.
- Target and SLA.
- Communication audience and client-contact protocol.
- Escalation contacts.

Partners acknowledge scope, capacity, missing prerequisites, conflicts, and expected date. Hajime does not assign work by forwarding an unstructured email thread.

During delivery, communication should distinguish:

- Professional conclusion.
- Operational instruction.
- Client decision required.
- Missing evidence.
- External waiting.
- Risk or change request.

## Escalation procedures

### Escalation levels

#### Level 1: Owner correction

Use for a minor missed response, incomplete handoff, or emerging due-date risk. Coordinator confirms the issue, required response, and near-term deadline with the current owner.

#### Level 2: Coordinator intervention

Use when the owner cannot resolve promptly, the critical path is affected, evidence is rejected, or responsibilities conflict. Coordinator creates a remediation plan and informs affected parties.

#### Level 3: Operations or partner-manager intervention

Use for SLA breach, repeated quality issue, capacity failure, material scope conflict, or client confidence risk. Consider alternate resources, formal expectation reset, and service recovery.

#### Level 4: Executive, risk, or professional escalation

Use for legal or regulatory exposure, fraud concern, security incident, threatened claim, severe reputational risk, partner suspension, or material client harm. Restrict access and communication as directed by incident leadership.

### Required escalation record

- Facts and source.
- Impacted outcome, deadline, and parties.
- What remains uncertain.
- Current owner and escalation owner.
- Immediate containment.
- Options and recommendation.
- Client communication decision.
- Next review time.
- Resolution and prevention action.

### Escalation timing

Escalate based on potential impact, not just elapsed time. A one-day delay on a critical signing window can be more important than a week of normal government waiting.

## Blocked and waiting work

### Waiting

Waiting is normal when the party or event and expected window are known. Record who or what is expected, since when, next check, and which dates depend on it.

### Blocked

Blocked means no valid next action is available without intervention, an expected event has failed, ownership is unclear, or the delay now threatens the project.

Every blocked item requires an escalation owner and a remediation decision date. “Blocked” is not a place to store uncomfortable tasks indefinitely.

## Quality assurance

### Quality gates

Perform defined review at:

- Sales-to-operations handoff for scope and promises.
- Planning approval for route, ownership, dependencies, and boundaries.
- Partner assignment for capability, scope, and access.
- Filing or submission readiness for complete accepted inputs and responsible approval.
- Material timeline reset for assumptions and client communication.
- Project completion for deliverables, obligations, and handoff.

### QA checks

- Outcome and owner are clear.
- Required professional decisions are attributed.
- Evidence is accepted for the intended use, not merely uploaded.
- Client-facing language respects boundaries.
- Dates distinguish estimates, commitments, and external ranges.
- Internal notes and restricted information are not exposed.
- Package scope and add-ons are current.
- Partner access remains necessary and limited.
- Completion has evidence and continuing work has an owner.

### Sampling

All critical and high-risk gates receive review. Standard projects use risk-based and representative sampling. New coordinators, partners, services, and country variants receive increased review until performance is demonstrated.

## Internal review and decision authority

Coordinators can make routine operational decisions within package and workflow policy. They must seek review for:

- New or unclear professional boundary.
- Critical requirement waiver.
- Material scope or fee dispute.
- Substantial target-date reset.
- High-risk partner assignment or replacement.
- Suspected fraud, sanctions, or identity issue.
- Client threat, claim, or serious complaint.
- Unsupported activity or country variation.
- Data-sharing or access exception.
- Cancellation with unresolved professional or government work.

The person preparing a high-risk exception should not be its sole approver.

## Project handoffs

### Sales to coordinator

Transfer client outcome, scope, assumptions, exclusions, stakeholders, dates, risks, professional questions, promises, and payment status.

### Coordinator to partner

Transfer a bounded scope, facts, questions, accepted inputs, deliverables, timing, audience, and escalation route.

### Partner to partner

Hajime coordinates purpose-limited evidence and conclusions with client authority. One partner does not gain broad access to another's work or internal notes.

### Coordinator coverage handoff

For absence or reassignment, document current phase, critical path, urgent commitments, sensitive context, client style, partner issues, next actions, and communication due. Introduce the new coordinator when the change is material.

### Launch to ongoing support

Transfer business records, provider relationships, recurring obligations, renewal dates, open risks, credentials references, client roles, and agreed support scope. Close formation work rather than leaving both projects ambiguously active.

### Project closure

Confirm client acceptance, open-item disposition, partner completion, access review, final update, commercial closure, retention, performance data, and lessons learned.

## Service recovery

When Hajime or a partner fails the expected standard:

1. Stabilize the project and protect critical dates.
2. Give the client a named owner and response time.
3. Establish facts before assigning blame.
4. Offer a remediation path and alternatives.
5. Determine whether a commercial remedy is appropriate.
6. Review root cause across scope, capacity, handoff, quality, and monitoring.
7. Implement prevention and confirm closure with the client.

Do not make the client manage Hajime's internal investigation.

## Success metrics

### Client outcomes

- Client confidence and referral.
- Client action clarity and turnaround.
- Percentage of projects without surprise critical delays.
- Launch-readiness and clean-handoff rate.
- Complaint and service-recovery outcome.
- Ongoing-support attachment and renewal.

### Delivery

- Actual versus predicted completion range.
- Critical-path delay by responsible party.
- Preventable rework and repeated evidence requests.
- Blocker age and escalation effectiveness.
- Quality-gate exception rate.
- Scope-change frequency and source.

### Partner network

- Acceptance speed, turnaround, SLA risk, rework, and communication reliability.
- Capacity accuracy and bottleneck concentration.
- Replacement and incident rates.

### Team health

- Coordinator complexity-adjusted load.
- After-hours and urgent-intervention frequency.
- Coverage quality and handoff errors.
- Training and calibration findings.
- Employee retention and sustainable utilization.

### Commercial health

- Gross margin by package and service.
- Unpriced exception effort.
- Third-party cost variance.
- Upsell quality, renewal, and referral.

No single metric should reward closing tasks, overloading coordinators, hiding blockers, or pressuring clients at the expense of valid outcomes.

## Operating non-negotiables

1. One accountable coordinator and visible backup for every active project.
2. One owner and next review time for every critical action, wait, or blocker.
3. No professional advice presented as Hajime's own.
4. No partner handoff without a bounded scope and accepted responsibility.
5. No repeated evidence request without purpose and explanation.
6. No silent material change to scope, date, owner, or risk.
7. No project completion without an operational handoff.
8. No coordinator overload disguised as efficiency.
9. No internal blame or performance detail passed to the client.
10. No client left wondering what happens next.
