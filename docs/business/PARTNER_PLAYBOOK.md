# Hajime Japan Partner Playbook

## Partnership promise

Hajime brings qualified, prepared clients and coordinates the work surrounding a partner's expertise. Partners bring the licensed authority, specialist capability, service delivery, and professional judgment Hajime does not provide.

The relationship succeeds when:

- Scope and responsibility are clear.
- The partner receives complete, relevant inputs.
- The client understands whose decision or advice they are receiving.
- Status and blockers are communicated early.
- Hajime protects the partner from avoidable coordination noise.
- The partner protects the client from jargon, ambiguity, and preventable rework.

Partners are not interchangeable vendors, and Hajime is not a lead broker that disappears after introduction.

## Partner categories

### Attorneys and immigration professionals

Own legal or immigration advice, representation, eligibility analysis, application strategy, and professional conclusions within their licensed scope. Hajime prepares facts, coordinates questions and evidence, schedules decisions, and translates approved conclusions into project actions without changing meaning.

### Accountants and tax professionals

Own accounting setup, tax advice, registrations, elections, filings, and continuing professional obligations. Hajime coordinates intake, evidence, decisions, calendars, and operating handoff.

### Banks and banking partners

Banks own eligibility, due diligence, account decisions, terms, and requests. Introduction partners own the specific service they contract to provide. Hajime prepares the client and evidence, coordinates meetings and follow-up, and never guarantees approval.

### Translators and certification providers

Own accurate translation, certification, notarization, legalization, or related deliverables within their qualifications. Hajime defines the receiving purpose, source version, target language, certification standard, deadline, and acceptance path.

### Insurance providers

Licensed brokers, agents, and insurers own suitability, recommendations, underwriting, terms, and coverage. Hajime prepares business facts, coordinates quotes and decisions, and tracks activation and renewal.

### Registered offices, serviced offices, and mail providers

Own availability, property or service terms, operational standards, mail handling, and evidence they issue. Hajime clarifies client needs, compares qualified options, coordinates selection, and tracks activation and renewal. Hajime does not perform regulated real-estate activity without appropriate authority.

### Government filing specialists

Own the filing or submission services stated in their scope, communication with agencies where authorized, and interpretation of substantive agency requests within competence. Hajime manages readiness, status, response coordination, and client communication.

## Partner selection criteria

### Mandatory eligibility

- Valid legal entity or professional identity.
- Verified licenses, credentials, registrations, and insurance where required.
- Clear service capability and geographic scope.
- No unresolved conflict, sanction, or material conduct concern.
- Willingness to follow privacy, security, confidentiality, and document-handling standards.
- Written commercial and operating terms.
- Named service and escalation contacts.
- Ability to distinguish professional advice from coordination.
- Capacity to meet the proposed service level.

### Fit criteria

- Experience with international founders and relevant business types.
- Language and cross-cultural communication ability.
- Responsiveness and clarity.
- Realistic turnaround and transparent prerequisites.
- Quality and first-pass completeness.
- Comfort working inside a coordinated multi-partner project.
- Transparent pricing and change requests.
- Client empathy without making unsupported promises.
- Willingness to report capacity and blockers early.
- Geographic, entity, and complexity specialization.

### Portfolio criteria

Hajime should avoid dependency on one provider. Each critical capability should have:

- A primary partner path.
- At least one credible contingency where the market allows.
- Known capacity and escalation contacts.
- A documented replacement and handoff approach.
- Coverage for language, region, and common complexity variations.

## Due diligence and onboarding

### Review

Collect and verify the partner's organization, ownership where relevant, licenses, professional status, insurance, service categories, countries and regions, languages, pricing, data-handling practices, conflicts process, subcontractors, complaints or disciplinary context where lawfully available, and references.

### Service test

New partners begin with limited assignments or a pilot. Assess acknowledgment, questions, scope discipline, communication, deliverable quality, turnaround, and client experience.

### Operating alignment

Before active status, agree:

- Capability and exclusions.
- Assignment and acceptance process.
- Required input standard.
- Response and delivery expectations.
- Client-contact protocol.
- Status and blocker language.
- Document access and retention.
- Fee, quote, and scope-change process.
- Escalation and incident contacts.
- Performance review and offboarding.

### Client-visible profile

Only approved factual claims appear in Hajime materials. Do not imply exclusivity, endorsement, government affiliation, or guaranteed availability without a valid basis.

## Assignment standard

Every assignment contains:

- Client and project reference.
- Specific service and desired outcome.
- Facts and assumptions relevant to the partner.
- Questions requiring partner authority.
- Deliverables and acceptance standard.
- Required client decisions or evidence.
- Target date and service-level expectations.
- Approved document and information access.
- Client communication arrangement.
- Fees or quote process.
- Escalation contacts.

The partner confirms acceptance, responsible professional or team, capacity, missing prerequisites, expected range, and conflicts. Silence is not acceptance.

## Quality standards

All partners are expected to:

1. Work only within verified capability and authority.
2. State assumptions, exclusions, and uncertainty.
3. Request only information needed for the assigned purpose.
4. Protect client data and avoid unapproved onward sharing.
5. Confirm receipt and identify incomplete inputs promptly.
6. Communicate blockers before the delivery date is missed.
7. Deliver work that is complete, attributable, and usable downstream.
8. Separate professional conclusions from administrative status.
9. Correct partner-caused errors promptly and transparently.
10. Avoid guaranteed outcomes or misleading claims.
11. Respect Hajime's client communication and escalation structure.
12. Return or close access and records appropriately after assignment.

## Service-level framework

Specific targets vary by capability and contract, but every active partner arrangement defines:

- **Assignment acknowledgment**: time to accept, decline, or request missing scope.
- **Initial response**: time to address a complete client or coordinator question.
- **Input review**: time to confirm completeness after receiving documents.
- **Delivery range**: expected working time after complete inputs.
- **Status cadence**: frequency during active work and external waiting.
- **Blocker notice**: how quickly material delay or rejection is reported.
- **Correction response**: time to assess and plan remediation.
- **Escalation response**: time for a senior contact to engage.

SLA clocks distinguish partner working time from waiting on client, Hajime, government, or another partner. A pause requires the missing input and responsible party to be named.

Never promise the client a tighter external SLA than the partner has accepted and demonstrated.

## Communication protocol

### Routine work

Use the agreed project channel and reference. Keep messages attached to the relevant scope, decision, task, or evidence. Confirm decisions and deadlines in writing.

### Client contact

Partners may communicate directly with clients when the assignment calls for it. Hajime remains included on operational commitments and dependencies unless confidentiality or professional-duty rules require a separate channel.

Partners should not solicit unrelated services from the client during an active Hajime assignment without discussing fit and boundary with Hajime, subject to professional independence and client choice.

### Professional advice

Advice is issued by the professional in their name. Hajime may prepare questions and summarize approved operational consequences. If a summary could change substantive meaning, the partner confirms it before client reliance.

### Status language

Use clear states: awaiting input, under partner review, submitted, waiting on external response, changes requested, accepted, rejected, or completed. Avoid vague “processing” when a more precise status is known.

## Category-specific operating standards

### Attorneys and immigration professionals

- Conflict clearance before substantive facts are broadly shared.
- Written scope identifies client, responsible professional, jurisdiction, and advice boundaries.
- Advice and filings are attributable and retained under professional rules.
- Material assumptions, deadlines, and client decisions are explicit.
- Hajime does not edit conclusions without approval.
- Urgent adverse events route directly to the professional and Hajime escalation owner.

### Accountants and tax professionals

- Intake distinguishes bookkeeping, tax advice, registrations, payroll, and recurring filings.
- Applicable obligations and elections are professionally confirmed.
- Evidence and deadlines are tied to a named business and fiscal period.
- Continuing responsibility after launch is accepted in writing.
- Hajime does not state tax treatment or filing completeness independently.

### Banks and banking partners

- No approval or timing guarantee.
- Customer eligibility and due diligence remain the bank's decision.
- Introduction does not imply preferred treatment.
- Additional-information requests are captured accurately and routed promptly.
- Banking evidence receives the strictest purpose-limited access.
- Rejection is communicated factually, with alternate paths only after review.

### Translators and certification providers

- Source version, target language, recipient, and certification standard are fixed before work.
- Names, dates, numbers, stamps, and omitted text receive explicit quality attention.
- Deliverable is linked to the source and provider qualifications.
- Corrections create a revised version and preserve prior history.
- Confidential material is not used for training, samples, or subcontracting without authority.

### Insurance providers

- Licensed provider owns recommendations, suitability, underwriting, terms, and disclosure.
- Hajime passes business facts accurately and does not compare coverage as advice.
- Quote validity, exclusions, binding status, and effective date are explicit.
- Renewal ownership is established at activation.

### Registered office and mail providers

- Availability and permitted use are confirmed for the client's activity.
- Contract, renewal, cancellation, mail handling, forwarding, identification, and access terms are clear.
- Address evidence requirements and issue timing are known.
- Operational incidents involving mail or address continuity are escalated promptly.
- Professional partners confirm legal or filing suitability where needed.

### Government filing specialists

- Filing authority, submission channel, responsibility, and evidence standard are explicit.
- Submission receipt or acknowledgment is provided.
- Agency requests and deadlines are transmitted without paraphrase that changes meaning.
- Substantive interpretation routes to the qualified professional.
- Government waiting is distinguished from partner delay.

## Performance review

### Continuous measurement

Track by comparable capability and assignment:

- Assignment acceptance and response speed.
- Complete-input to delivery turnaround.
- On-time rate and SLA risk.
- First-pass acceptance and rework.
- Document or filing correction rate.
- Communication clarity and status reliability.
- Client and coordinator feedback.
- Capacity forecast accuracy.
- Escalation, complaint, and reassignment.

Do not penalize partners for government, client, or Hajime waiting when their SLA is legitimately paused. Metrics include sample size and complexity.

### Review cadence

- New partners: review after each initial assignment and before broader use.
- Active core partners: structured quarterly review or more often at high volume.
- Low-volume partners: review after material assignments and at least annually.
- Restricted or improving partners: defined remediation checkpoints.

### Review agenda

- Volume and service mix.
- What worked and where handoffs failed.
- Turnaround and quality evidence.
- Client communication and complaints.
- Capacity outlook and planned constraints.
- Pricing or scope changes.
- Improvement commitments, owners, and dates.
- Capability expansion or restriction.

## Quality incident management

A quality incident includes material error, missed deadline, unauthorized data sharing, misleading advice claim, credential concern, conflict failure, repeated unresponsiveness, client mistreatment, or deliverable unusable for its agreed purpose.

### Response

1. Protect the client and critical deadline.
2. Restrict access or new assignments when needed.
3. Establish facts and preserve records.
4. Assign Hajime and partner incident owners.
5. Notify the client at the appropriate level and time.
6. Correct or replace the work.
7. Determine commercial, contractual, reporting, or insurance steps.
8. Record root cause and prevention.
9. Decide active, restricted, suspended, or offboarded status.

Serious incidents are not handled solely through a routine performance score.

## Replacement procedure

Replacement may be triggered by capacity failure, SLA breach, quality issue, conflict, credential change, client request, partner request, scope mismatch, or relationship termination.

### Decision factors

- Immediate client and deadline risk.
- Professional independence and client choice.
- Work already completed and reliance risk.
- Alternate partner eligibility and capacity.
- New engagement, conflict, and fee requirements.
- Document access and confidentiality.
- Need for original records or transfer consent.
- Client communication and cost responsibility.

### Handoff

- Close or limit the prior assignment.
- Confirm transfer authority.
- Identify accepted work, open questions, deadlines, and disputed items.
- Share only approved records with the replacement.
- Obtain replacement acceptance and timeline.
- Revoke unnecessary old access.
- Update the client and project plan.
- Measure the prior assignment fairly and separately from the replacement.

Hajime must not imply that replacement guarantees a better external outcome.

## Conflict resolution

### Scope or responsibility conflict

Return to the written assignment and responsibility map. Identify the required outcome, missing owner, and whether the issue is included work, new scope, or professional interpretation.

### Conflicting professional views

Hajime does not choose which legal, tax, accounting, immigration, or insurance opinion is correct. The coordinator frames the conflict, confirms assumptions, obtains written positions, and helps the client seek clarification or an independent qualified opinion.

### Fee conflict

Separate approved scope, additional request, partner-caused correction, and client-driven change. No added work begins without client authorization when timing allows. Urgent protective work is escalated commercially.

### Service-quality conflict

Establish the accepted standard and evidence. Agree remediation, owner, and date. If trust or risk cannot be restored, replace or suspend.

### Client-partner relationship conflict

Protect respectful communication and professional independence. Hajime may mediate process and expectations but does not interfere with duties owed directly to the client.

## Commercial integrity

- Partner selection prioritizes fit, capability, availability, quality, and client need.
- Referral fees, markups, discounts, or preferred arrangements are documented and disclosed where required.
- Partners do not change scope or fee without a clear client approval path.
- Hajime does not demand professional conclusions favorable to closing or retaining business.
- Client choice is respected, including the right to use a qualified outside partner where the package permits.

## Offboarding

An offboarded partner receives no new assignments. Hajime reviews every active and future assignment, protects critical work, transfers records lawfully, resolves fees and deliverables, revokes access, updates client-visible information, and records the business reason.

Professional record-retention duties may continue after access ends. Offboarding does not erase assignment history or performance evidence.

## Partner non-negotiables

1. Verified capability before assignment.
2. Explicit acceptance before ownership.
3. Purpose-limited client data access.
4. Early blocker communication.
5. No guaranteed external outcomes.
6. Attributable professional advice.
7. Transparent scope and fees.
8. Measurable delivery standards.
9. Respectful client treatment.
10. Clean replacement and offboarding when standards cannot be restored.
