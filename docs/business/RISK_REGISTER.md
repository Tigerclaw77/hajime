# Hajime Japan Business Risk Register

## Purpose

This register identifies risks that could prevent Hajime from delivering a trustworthy, sustainable premium concierge service. It is an operating tool, not a one-time compliance exercise.

Every material risk has an owner, indicators, preventive controls, a recovery path, and a review cadence. “Low likelihood” never means “no preparation” when impact is severe.

## Rating method

### Likelihood

- **Low**: uncommon under normal controls.
- **Medium**: plausible during ordinary operations.
- **High**: expected to occur repeatedly across a growing portfolio.

### Impact

- **Moderate**: localized delay, cost, or dissatisfaction that normal operations can resolve.
- **High**: material client, financial, partner, or reputation harm requiring leadership intervention.
- **Critical**: potential illegality, severe client harm, major data loss, business interruption, or threat to Hajime's ability to operate.

Ratings describe inherent business concern and should be reassessed after actual controls, incidents, and country expansion.

## 1. Unauthorized or mischaracterized professional activity

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Sales, coordinators, marketing, or product behavior crosses from coordination into legal, immigration, tax, accounting, insurance, real-estate, financial, or other reserved activity, or creates the appearance that Hajime is the professional provider.

**Detection**: Communication sampling; customer complaints; partner feedback; staff boundary escalations; use of high-risk phrases; staff answering client-specific professional questions; missing attribution; regulator or association inquiry.

**Mitigation**: Counsel-validated service perimeter; approved scripts and copy; mandatory role training; qualified partner routing; partner-attributed advice; restricted claims; review of new services, countries, referral economics, and automation; separation of Hajime and professional engagements.

**Recovery**: Stop the affected activity; preserve records; obtain counsel review; correct client communication; route work to a qualified professional; notify insurers or authorities where required; remediate client harm; revise process and training; discipline or restrict repeated violations.

**Owner**: Executive risk owner with Japanese counsel and operations lead.

## 2. Regulatory or country-rule change

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High to Critical.

**Risk**: Formation, immigration, tax, banking, filing, data, consumer, or provider rules change and Hajime continues using outdated requirements, claims, or workflows.

**Detection**: Partner advisories; government publications; professional review cadence; rejected filings; changed agency forms; inconsistent partner instructions; unusual delay or correction pattern.

**Mitigation**: Named country owner; authoritative-source register; partner advisory obligations; effective-dated service manuals; periodic professional validation; versioned templates; impact search across active projects; no unsupported extrapolation.

**Recovery**: Validate the change; identify affected clients and work; pause risky actions; obtain professional remediation; update plans and expectations; migrate active processes through controlled review; document cost and responsibility; conduct post-change audit.

**Owner**: Country operations owner and relevant professional partner.

## 3. Partner capacity or service failure

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: A key attorney, accountant, translator, office provider, bank liaison, filing specialist, or other partner cannot accept or complete work at the promised standard.

**Detection**: Slow assignment acceptance; rising workload; missed acknowledgments; SLA warnings; repeated waiting; decline rate; quality defects; partner-reported constraint; multiple affected critical paths.

**Mitigation**: Capability-specific capacity tracking; backup partners; conservative commitments; accepted assignments before ownership; bottleneck reviews; partner SLAs; concentration limits; early input-completeness checks.

**Recovery**: Escalate to partner leadership; protect deadlines; communicate with affected clients; triage critical work; reassign where appropriate; transfer records securely; provide service recovery; restrict or suspend new assignments; review capacity assumptions.

**Owner**: Partner manager and operations lead.

## 4. Partner quality or professional error

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Partner advice, filing, translation, certification, calculation, or deliverable is materially wrong or unsuitable, causing rejection, delay, cost, or client harm.

**Detection**: Downstream rejection; conflicting professional review; correction requests; first-pass failure; client complaint; quality sampling; unusual rework; partner self-report.

**Mitigation**: Credential verification; scoped assignments; defined acceptance standard; professional attribution; risk-based second review; new-partner pilots; version control; complete inputs; professional insurance requirements where appropriate.

**Recovery**: Contain reliance; notify responsible professional; obtain correction or independent review; preserve versions and advice; protect deadlines; inform client factually; consider alternate provider and commercial remedy; notify insurers or authorities where required; suspend capability pending review.

**Owner**: Partner manager, operations lead, and responsible professional.

## 5. Client abandonment or chronic non-response

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: Moderate to High.

**Risk**: Client stops responding, misses decisions, fails to provide evidence, or disengages while partner costs, deadlines, and sensitive records remain active.

**Detection**: Missed actions; bounced communications; repeated reminders; unpaid milestone; meeting absence; expiring documents; partner waiting; approaching hard deadline.

**Mitigation**: Qualification for responsiveness; named decision-maker; clear client duties; action requests with effort and consequence; escalation cadence; pause and abandonment terms; no uncontrolled third-party spend; backup contact.

**Recovery**: Escalate through approved contacts; state impact and final response date; pause affected work and SLA appropriately; protect or withdraw filings under professional guidance; close partner assignments; settle fees; revoke access; deliver a record and responsibility handoff; archive under retention policy.

**Owner**: Coordinator and account owner.

## 6. Misrepresentation, fraud, or prohibited client activity

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Client provides false identity, ownership, source-of-funds, business-activity, document, or intent information, or uses Hajime to facilitate unlawful or deceptive activity.

**Detection**: Inconsistent facts; altered documents; unexplained ownership; pressure to omit information; unusual payment; partner or bank concern; refusal to identify beneficial owners; impossible business claims; sanctions or adverse-risk signal.

**Mitigation**: Transparent intake; identity and authority checks appropriate to risk; partner due diligence; restricted industries; staff red-flag training; no coaching around disclosure; escalation and refusal policy; controlled access to sensitive evidence.

**Recovery**: Pause work and restrict access; preserve evidence; consult counsel and relevant professionals; stop submissions or payments where lawful; terminate if appropriate; fulfill reporting or record-preservation duties; communicate carefully; review exposure and affected partners.

**Owner**: Risk owner and counsel.

## 7. Bank rejection or account-opening delay

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: A bank declines the client, requests extensive additional information, or takes longer than the operating plan allows.

**Detection**: Readiness gaps; weak local activity; repeated information request; bank silence; interview concern; missed expected range; rejection; mismatch between evidence and narrative.

**Mitigation**: No-guarantee expectation; qualified route selection; complete business narrative and evidence; realistic ranges; alternate operational plans; early banking preparation; transparent ownership; client interview preparation.

**Recovery**: Obtain factual outcome and permitted reason; route substantive questions to qualified partners; correct evidence if appropriate; evaluate alternate bank or payment path; reset launch dependencies; communicate impact; never encourage misstatement or application shopping that creates risk.

**Owner**: Coordinator and banking partner.

## 8. Government delay, outage, rejection, or request

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Government review exceeds the expected range, systems or offices become unavailable, a filing is rejected, or additional information is requested.

**Detection**: Missing acknowledgment; published outage; elapsed external range; agency correspondence; partner notice; cohort-wide delay; seasonal backlog.

**Mitigation**: Honest timing ranges; authoritative submission receipt; agency calendars; early readiness checks; buffer before hard commitments; responsible filing partner; scheduled status checks; no promise based on best case.

**Recovery**: Verify status through the authorized filer; classify request; protect response deadline; assemble remediation with qualified professional; reset forecast and downstream work; update client; escalate systemic delays across the portfolio.

**Owner**: Filing partner and coordinator.

## 9. Translation, certification, or document-version error

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Wrong source version, mistranslation, missing page, incorrect name or number, inadequate certification, or stale document is routed to a partner or agency.

**Detection**: Version mismatch; quality checklist failure; recipient rejection; conflicting extracted data; missing source-output linkage; expired evidence; client or partner correction.

**Mitigation**: Immutable versions; named receiving purpose; approved source manifest; qualified provider; names/dates/numbers review; certification requirement confirmed in advance; recipient-specific acceptance; expiry monitoring.

**Recovery**: Stop further use; identify every affected submission and requirement; notify translator and responsible professional; issue corrected version with preserved history; request deadline relief where available; communicate client impact; review root cause and provider performance.

**Owner**: Document coordinator and translation partner.

## 10. Privacy, confidentiality, or unauthorized access incident

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Identity, ownership, financial, immigration, legal, or business information is exposed, sent to the wrong party, retained unnecessarily, or accessed beyond assignment scope.

**Detection**: Access logs; recipient report; unusual download; bounced or misaddressed message; stale partner access; missing device; support report; security alert; document discovered in an unapproved channel.

**Mitigation**: Data minimization; purpose-bound sharing; role and assignment scope; secure approved channels; access expiry; partner handling terms; staff training; least privilege; incident plan; limited exports; retention policy.

**Recovery**: Contain access; preserve evidence; activate incident leadership; assess content and affected parties; obtain counsel and security review; notify as required; rotate credentials; recover or revoke copies where possible; support affected clients; remediate controls and partner status.

**Owner**: Security/privacy owner and executive incident lead.

## 11. Cybersecurity or technology outage

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High to Critical.

**Risk**: Systems, devices, communication tools, document stores, or future Hajime software become unavailable, compromised, corrupted, or unreliable.

**Detection**: Monitoring; failed access; integrity mismatch; unusual authentication or export; provider alert; staff or client report; backup failure; inconsistent project status.

**Mitigation**: Approved tools; least privilege; strong access controls; device management; backups and restore tests; vendor review; business-continuity procedures; offline critical-contact and deadline register; no single communication channel for emergencies.

**Recovery**: Isolate incident; invoke continuity plan; shift to controlled manual operations; protect hard deadlines; notify affected stakeholders appropriately; restore verified records; reconcile changes made during outage; conduct incident and vendor review.

**Owner**: Technology/security owner and operations lead.

## 12. Operational scaling and coordinator overload

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Project growth outpaces coordinator capacity, causing slow responses, hidden blockers, weak handoffs, poor judgment, burnout, and inconsistent client experience.

**Detection**: Complexity-adjusted load; after-hours work; overdue reviews; rising stale tasks; update gaps; missed client commitments; coordinator absence risk; complaints; growing exception age; turnover.

**Mitigation**: Capacity model with operating slack; staged intake; workload review; backup coverage; standardized services; coordinator training; support roles; quality sampling; limits on custom scope; hiring ahead of committed volume.

**Recovery**: Freeze or pace new starts; rebalance portfolios; add senior intervention; reset client expectations; triage critical work; engage contingency partners or contractors within controls; review sales commitments; provide team recovery time; correct package economics.

**Owner**: Operations lead and executive team.

## 13. Single-person knowledge or continuity failure

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: A coordinator, founder, or partner contact becomes unavailable and critical project context exists only in their memory or personal messages.

**Detection**: Unrecorded decisions; inaccessible channels; no backup; projects with one active contact; overdue updates during absence; incomplete handoff notes.

**Mitigation**: Named backup; project decision and commitment records; approved communication channels; coverage handoff; cross-training; partner escalation contacts; absence planning; no personal-only document custody.

**Recovery**: Activate backup; reconstruct current state from records; confirm facts with client and partners; prioritize deadlines; disclose necessary coordinator change; complete knowledge capture; address access or employment separation.

**Owner**: Operations lead.

## 14. Scope creep and unpriced exception work

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Coordinators absorb additional entities, stakeholders, bank paths, translations, licensing, remediation, or post-launch work without scope and fee adjustment.

**Detection**: Effort above package assumption; repeated “small” requests; additional partners or workstreams; margin decline; extended service window; coordinator concern; client expectation mismatch.

**Mitigation**: Clear service catalog and packages; written assumptions; change triggers; coordinator authority to pause new scope; simple add-ons; commercial review; margin by service; sales-handoff quality.

**Recovery**: Separate original promise from new need; complete urgent protective action if necessary; present scope options; obtain approval; reassign capacity; waive fee only through authorized service recovery; update package learning.

**Owner**: Coordinator and account owner.

## 15. Poor unit economics or cash-flow pressure

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Fees do not cover coordinator effort, partner management, quality, acquisition, and exceptions, or Hajime fronts external costs before receiving client funds.

**Detection**: Gross margin by package; unpaid third-party costs; effort variance; discount rate; extended projects; high acquisition cost; refund or dispute rate; low recurring attachment; cash forecast.

**Mitigation**: Premium pricing; deposits; milestone billing; no uncontrolled pass-through exposure; effort measurement; discount discipline; scope controls; partner terms; coordinator capacity economics; cash reserve.

**Recovery**: Stop unprofitable selling pattern; reprice or narrow scope; collect outstanding payments; renegotiate partner terms responsibly; pace starts; reduce nonessential cost without degrading active service; secure appropriate financing only with clear plan.

**Owner**: Finance and executive team.

## 16. Reputation damage and misleading public claims

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Client complaint, partner conduct, public copy, referral claim, missed launch, or online discussion creates the view that Hajime is misleading, unqualified, careless, or unresponsive.

**Detection**: Complaints; review sites; social monitoring; partner feedback; sales-call sampling; high-risk claim audit; press or regulator inquiry; referral-source concern; low recommendation score.

**Mitigation**: Honest positioning; boundary-reviewed copy; no guarantees; service recovery; client consent for testimonials; partner standards; complaint process; accurate status; strong handoffs; no suppression or manipulation of valid criticism.

**Recovery**: Establish facts; prioritize affected client; correct public misinformation without exposing confidential details; remove misleading claims; involve counsel or communications support; remediate root cause; issue appropriate apology or correction; monitor recurrence.

**Owner**: Executive team and operations lead.

## 17. Conflict of interest or distorted partner selection

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Referral fees, volume terms, personal relationships, or commercial pressure cause Hajime to recommend a partner that is not the best fit, or the relationship is not disclosed appropriately.

**Detection**: Selection inconsistent with capability or capacity; unusual concentration; undisclosed payment; partner complaints; client question; repeated selection despite poor performance; staff relationship disclosure.

**Mitigation**: Written matching criteria; economic disclosure; conflict policy; multiple eligible options where appropriate; partner performance review; approval for preferred arrangements; separation of professional independence from sales targets.

**Recovery**: Disclose and investigate; reassess affected assignments; offer alternate provider; return or correct improper compensation where required; revise selection and approval; discipline misconduct; obtain counsel review.

**Owner**: Partner manager and risk owner.

## 18. Client-provider or interprofessional conflict

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Client and partner lose trust, or qualified professionals give conflicting conclusions that Hajime is not competent to resolve.

**Detection**: Contradictory instructions; hostile communication; refusal to proceed; repeated scope dispute; client challenge; partner withdrawal; unresolved professional question.

**Mitigation**: Clear scopes; responsibility map; attributable advice; written assumptions; meeting preparation; respectful communication standard; independent-review path; no Hajime reinterpretation of professional conclusions.

**Recovery**: Separate process conflict from substantive disagreement; preserve written views; facilitate clarification; engage independent qualified opinion if the client chooses; replace partner where relationship cannot continue; reset plan, fees, and timing transparently.

**Owner**: Coordinator with operations lead and relevant professional.

## 19. Demand concentration or acquisition-channel failure

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Revenue depends heavily on one partner, referrer, founder network, customer segment, country route, or seasonal source.

**Detection**: Revenue and pipeline by source; referral concentration; falling qualified leads; one segment's conversion change; partner policy change; seasonality; acquisition cost increase.

**Mitigation**: Diverse trusted channels; client referrals; content that clarifies category; enterprise and ongoing services; source-quality tracking; no exclusive dependency; reserve and variable cost discipline.

**Recovery**: Reduce spend on failing channels; activate alternate referral and customer segments; deepen existing-client services without pressure; adjust capacity; test new channels deliberately; avoid lowering qualification standards to fill volume.

**Owner**: Commercial lead and executive team.

## 20. Country expansion before operating readiness

**Likelihood**: Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Hajime markets or sells a new country before validating legal boundaries, services, partners, duration, pricing, escalation, and coordinator knowledge.

**Detection**: Sales request outside supported scope; one-off partner dependency; missing country manual; unvalidated claims; unknown government process; high custom effort; inconsistent advice; no backup capability.

**Mitigation**: Country readiness gate; counsel and professional review; limited pilot; service and boundary manual; partner redundancy; package economics; trained coordinators; clear supported-status labels; executive approval.

**Recovery**: Stop general sale; identify active commitments; assign senior country owner and professionals; narrow or terminate unsupported scope respectfully; communicate uncertainty; remediate clients; complete readiness or withdraw market claim.

**Owner**: Executive team and country operations owner.

## 21. Inaccurate timeline or status representation

**Likelihood**: High.

**Impact**: High.

**Risk**: Internal optimism, stale data, vague statuses, or pressure to please clients causes Hajime to show misleading progress or completion dates.

**Detection**: Repeated forecast misses; “in progress” without owner activity; exact dates for external work; client surprise; task state inconsistent with evidence; overdue update; completion without acceptance.

**Mitigation**: Responsibility-aware states; date provenance; external ranges; critical-path review; evidence-based completion; forecast-confidence language; coordinator calibration; no sales override of delivery truth.

**Recovery**: Correct status promptly; explain changed fact and impact; give a new range; identify intervention; review affected commitments; perform root-cause analysis on data, partner, process, or incentive.

**Owner**: Coordinator and operations lead.

## 22. Business continuity disaster

**Likelihood**: Low to Medium.

**Impact**: Critical.

**Risk**: Natural disaster, pandemic, office closure, major vendor outage, leadership incapacity, financial institution issue, or geopolitical event interrupts Hajime or partner operations.

**Detection**: Government and emergency alerts; vendor or partner notice; staff safety reports; widespread service failure; inaccessible office or systems; banking or communication disruption.

**Mitigation**: Distributed work capability; emergency contacts; backup coordinators; critical-deadline register; partner geographic redundancy; secure backups; alternate communication; cash reserve; decision-authority succession; continuity exercises.

**Recovery**: Protect people first; activate incident command; identify critical client deadlines and external dependencies; shift to alternate channels and partners; communicate service status; pause noncritical starts; reconcile records after restoration; review continuity gaps.

**Owner**: Executive incident lead and operations lead.

## Risk review cadence

### Continuous

Critical incidents, fraud signals, privacy events, professional-boundary concerns, and hard-deadline risks are escalated immediately.

### Weekly

Operations reviews blocked projects, partner bottlenecks, overdue client actions, coordinator load, timeline confidence, and near-completion risk.

### Monthly

Leadership reviews complaints, service recovery, scope and margin variance, access incidents, sales quality, partner concentration, and risk trends.

### Quarterly

Review the complete register, control effectiveness, owners, residual ratings, country advisories, insurance, continuity, and planned service expansion.

### Before material change

Run a dedicated risk review before a new country, package, partner category, referral model, regulated-adjacent service, major automation, or enterprise commitment.

## Incident learning

Every material incident should answer:

- What happened and when?
- Who and what were affected?
- Which control should have prevented or detected it?
- Why did that control fail or not exist?
- What reduced harm?
- What client, partner, contractual, insurance, or regulatory action followed?
- Who owns prevention and by when?
- How will Hajime verify the fix?

The purpose is accountable learning, not blame avoidance. Repeated incidents with the same cause require leadership action, not another reminder.

## Risk appetite

Hajime has low appetite for:

- Professional-boundary ambiguity.
- Misleading sales or guaranteed outcomes.
- Hidden ownership, fraud, or prohibited activity.
- Unnecessary sensitive-data access.
- One-partner dependency in a critical service.
- Coordinator overload.
- Unsupported country expansion.
- Work without a named owner or lawful delivery path.

Hajime can accept managed uncertainty in government timing, bank discretion, partner availability, and client decision-making only when the uncertainty is explicit, monitored, and communicated honestly.
