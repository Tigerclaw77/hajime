# Hajime Japan Sales Process

## Sales philosophy

Hajime sells confidence through clarity. The sales process should feel like the beginning of excellent coordination: prepared, concise, honest about uncertainty, and respectful of the founder's time.

Sales must not use legal fear, manufactured urgency, guaranteed outcomes, or vague “all-inclusive” claims. The goal is mutual qualification. A prospect who needs only a filing, cannot provide transparent information, expects guaranteed bank or visa approval, or does not value coordination should be declined or referred appropriately.

## Funnel stages

**Lead -> Qualified -> Discovery -> Solution fit -> Proposal -> Engagement -> Deposit -> Onboarding**

Alternate outcomes are nurture, referral to a professional, not currently supported, disqualified, lost, or no decision. Every closed outcome records a useful reason.

## 1. Lead

### Objective

Capture enough context to respond intelligently and determine the next appropriate step.

### Minimum information

- Person and organization.
- Contact route, language, and time zone.
- Target country and target timing.
- New company, branch, or existing business need.
- High-level business activity.
- Source or referrer.
- Immediate question or reason for contacting Hajime.

### Hajime action

Acknowledge promptly, set a realistic response expectation, and avoid answering professional questions during intake. Route clear professional-only inquiries to an appropriate path.

### Exit

Move to qualification, request missing basics, refer, nurture, or close as unsupported.

## 2. Qualification

### Objective

Determine whether Hajime can responsibly serve the prospect and whether concierge coordination is likely to create enough value.

### Qualification dimensions

- Credible business purpose.
- Supported jurisdiction, entity, and activity.
- Ownership transparency and decision authority.
- Realistic target timing.
- Expected need for multiple coordinated services.
- Willingness to engage qualified partners.
- Budget orientation and preference for premium support.
- Language and communication fit.
- Partner capacity and any obvious conflicts.
- Risk indicators requiring review.

### Strong fit

A prepared founder or expansion team with a clear Japan rationale, several interdependent launch needs, responsive leadership, and a preference for one accountable coordinator.

### Poor fit or disqualification

- Requests to conceal ownership, funds, activities, or identity.
- Demand for guaranteed government, bank, visa, tax, or legal outcomes.
- Prohibited or unsupported activity.
- Deadline impossible even under best-case assumptions.
- Need for professional representation without willingness to engage a professional.
- Primary motivation is obtaining the cheapest filing.
- Abusive, discriminatory, or threatening behavior.
- Unresolved conflict or sanctions concern.

### Exit

Invite to discovery, offer paid Blueprint planning, refer to a professional, place in nurture, or decline with respectful boundary language.

## 3. Discovery

### Objective

Understand the desired business outcome, operating reality, stakeholders, timing, risks, and the coordination burden the prospect wants Hajime to own.

### Preparation

The sales owner reviews submitted facts, referrer context, likely services, unanswered questions, and any boundary concern. The prospect receives a short agenda and a request for the actual decision-maker to attend.

### Discovery topics

- Why Japan and why now.
- Business model, customers, activities, and local operations.
- Entity and ownership assumptions.
- Founders, directors, beneficial owners, and signers.
- Capital and source context at a high level.
- City, office, staffing, and relocation needs.
- Banking, accounting, tax, insurance, and launch expectations.
- Existing professional relationships.
- Desired opening date and immovable commitments.
- Internal capacity to manage the project.
- Decision process and commercial expectations.

### What sales may say

Sales can explain Hajime's coordination model, typical phases, planning ranges, partner roles, package boundaries, and known dependencies.

### What sales must not say

Sales must not recommend a legal structure, interpret immigration eligibility, promise bank acceptance, give tax treatment, declare evidence sufficient, or guarantee a deadline. These questions are recorded for qualified review.

### Exit

Produce a discovery summary, fit decision, unanswered professional questions, initial service map, and proposed next commercial step.

## 4. Solution fit

### Objective

Translate discovery into the simplest package that can responsibly deliver the desired level of support.

### Package guidance

- Recommend DIY Blueprint when the client primarily needs a route and can manage execution.
- Recommend Guided Launch when the client will execute actions but needs structure, introductions, and checkpoints.
- Recommend Concierge when Hajime must actively manage multiple parties and protect founder time.
- Recommend Enterprise when governance, multiple projects or countries, procurement, or dedicated capacity is required.

Do not undersell a package that cannot absorb known complexity. Do not oversell Concierge when the prospect has a simple need and prefers self-management.

### Scope assumptions

Record entity count, founders and owners, business activity, target location, banking paths, language and certification needs, partner categories, client response expectations, service window, and excluded complexity.

## 5. Proposal

### Objective

Give the prospect a decision-ready commercial offer with no hidden boundary ambiguity.

### Proposal contents

- Desired outcome and project summary.
- Recommended package and why it fits.
- Included Hajime services and deliverables.
- Key assumptions and client responsibilities.
- Known partner roles.
- Typical timeline range and uncertainty drivers.
- Exclusions and likely add-ons.
- Hajime fee and payment schedule.
- Treatment of professional, government, and external fees.
- Scope-change rules.
- No-guarantee and professional-boundary language.
- Validity period and next step.

### Proposal review

Standard proposals require sales-owner review. High-risk, custom, discounted, enterprise, regulated, or unusually urgent scopes require operations and boundary review before release.

### Presentation

Walk the prospect through the proposal when the fee or complexity warrants it. Focus on ownership and outcome, not a long feature recital. Ask the prospect to restate any deadline or approval assumption that could cause future misunderstanding.

## 6. Engagement

### Objective

Establish clear contractual responsibility and consent before operational work begins.

### Requirements

- Correct contracting party and authorized signer.
- Accepted service scope and package version.
- Professional and external-fee treatment.
- Client duties and response expectations.
- Communications and document-handling consent.
- Referral and partner-disclosure terms where applicable.
- Pause, cancellation, refund, and scope-change terms.
- Boundary and no-guarantee acknowledgment.
- Required risk or identity checks.

Sales cannot alter standard boundary language or promise unapproved exceptions in side messages.

## 7. Deposit and payment readiness

### Objective

Reserve delivery capacity and confirm the client is commercially ready before project activation.

### Standard practice

- Collect the required initial payment before coordinator assignment and substantive planning.
- Confirm cleared funds before committing substantial partner or third-party spend.
- Identify payer and contracting-party mismatch for review.
- Issue payment milestones independently from external outcome promises.

### Exceptions

Enterprise terms, approved referral programs, or defined institutional clients may use alternate billing. Exceptions require authorized approval and do not change operational scope.

## 8. Sales-to-onboarding handoff

### Objective

Transfer context without making the client repeat the sale.

### Handoff packet

- Discovery summary in the client's words.
- Purchased package, add-ons, and exceptions.
- Key assumptions and explicit exclusions.
- Stakeholders and decision authority.
- Target outcome and dates.
- Known professional questions and risks.
- Existing providers and commitments.
- Communication preferences.
- Promises made and proposal version.
- Payment and contracting status.

### Handoff meeting

The sales owner and coordinator review the packet before the welcome call. Any ambiguity is resolved internally first. The coordinator then confirms scope and next actions with the client.

## Follow-up cadence

Cadence responds to buying stage and stated timing rather than sending endless generic messages.

### New qualified lead

- Initial response within the stated service standard.
- One useful follow-up after the prospect has had reasonable time to respond.
- A final close-the-loop note with a clear way to return.

### After discovery

- Send or confirm the written recap promptly.
- State when the proposal or paid-planning option will arrive.
- Follow up once before proposal expiry with any meaningful clarification.
- Close or move to nurture rather than maintaining an artificial active opportunity.

### Proposal outstanding

- Confirm receipt and answer scope questions.
- Follow up based on the prospect's decision date.
- Provide a concise deadline reminder only when price, capacity, or assumptions genuinely expire.
- Do not manufacture a closing deadline.

### Nurture

Use low-frequency, relevant contact tied to the prospect's target quarter, readiness milestone, or new country availability. Stop when requested. Educational material should clarify launch planning, not create anxiety.

## Referral program

### Referral sources

Founders, investors, accelerators, chambers, attorneys, accountants, banks, office providers, relocation firms, and existing clients can refer prospects.

### Principles

- Referred prospects receive the same qualification and pricing discipline.
- Referral compensation is lawful, documented, disclosed where required, and never paid for regulated referrals where prohibited.
- Partners do not receive confidential opportunity details without prospect permission.
- Referral quality is reviewed by fit, conversion, client outcome, and conduct, not volume alone.

### Client referrals

Thank clients thoughtfully. Prefer service credit, charitable recognition, or a modest transparent benefit over aggressive affiliate behavior. Never incentivize public claims that imply guaranteed outcomes.

## Upsells and scope expansion

An upsell is appropriate when a real need emerges:

- The client wants Hajime to assume more coordination.
- A second entity, founder, country, bank path, or location is added.
- Translation, certification, licensing, immigration, or remediation scope expands.
- The client needs operational launch or ongoing support.

The coordinator identifies the need; an authorized commercial owner presents the scope. Do not hold necessary information hostage to force an upgrade. If work is essential to the original promised outcome, resolve whether it was omitted from scope before charging more.

## Renewals and recurring services

### Renewal preparation

Begin before the current term ends with:

- Services used and outcomes coordinated.
- Upcoming obligations and risks.
- Provider and business changes.
- Expected support demand.
- Recommended scope and service level.
- Any price or boundary change.

### Renewal conversation

Connect the service to future coordination value. Do not imply the client will become noncompliant merely because they do not renew. Identify responsibilities that will return to the client or partner after service ends.

### Non-renewal

Provide a clean handoff of open responsibilities, dates, provider contacts, and client records included in scope. Revoke unnecessary access and close recurring tasks under policy.

## Sales metrics

Monitor:

- Lead source and qualified-lead rate.
- Discovery attendance and decision-maker participation.
- Qualified-to-proposal and proposal-to-engagement conversion.
- Time in stage and no-decision rate.
- Package mix and average coordination fee.
- Discount and exception rate.
- Lost reasons and poor-fit patterns.
- Sales-to-onboarding scope errors.
- Gross margin and coordinator effort by source and package.
- Referral, expansion, renewal, and ongoing-support attachment.
- Client complaints tied to expectation setting.

Do not reward closed revenue without considering fit, margin, delivery success, and boundary compliance.

## Sales quality checklist

Before marking an engagement won, confirm:

1. The customer and desired outcome are understood.
2. Hajime can support the country, activity, timing, and complexity.
3. The package matches the coordination burden.
4. Professional questions are routed, not answered by sales.
5. External uncertainty and no-guarantee boundaries are understood.
6. Scope assumptions and exclusions are written.
7. External and partner fees are transparent.
8. Decision authority, contract, payment, and consent are complete.
9. The coordinator receives a usable handoff.
10. No side promise contradicts the proposal or operating manual.
