# Hajime Experiment Playbooks

## Operating rules

- A qualified prospect has a credible company, concrete Japan trigger within 12 months, at least three interdependent needs, decision access, budget for coordination plus external costs, and no obvious boundary conflict.
- A customer counts only after written acceptance, cleared payment or deposit, and scheduled delivery.
- Every playbook is shorter than 30 days and should stop as soon as its decision threshold is reached.
- Interviews without a real paid ask do not validate willingness to pay.

## E01: Six Qualified Paid Offers

### Objective

Determine whether any qualified buyer will pay Hajime for Japan Launch Readiness.

### Hypothesis

At least one of six qualified prospects will pay $1,500 for a fixed-scope readiness engagement.

### Materials required

- Qualification checklist.
- Discovery script.
- One-page readiness scope.
- Invoice/payment method.
- Prospect and outcome tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Request warm introductions and contact triggered prospects until six qualify.
2. Hold a 30-minute discovery focused on current behavior and consequences.
3. Reflect the problem and make the same $1,500 offer.
4. Send written scope within one business day with a seven-day expiry.
5. Follow up once on the agreed decision date.
6. Record yes, no, not now, and the primary reason.

### Metrics

- Qualified discoveries.
- Offers sent.
- Cleared payments.
- Offer-to-paid conversion.
- Days to decision.
- Founder sales hours.
- Primary objections.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** At least one cleared payment from six qualified offers.
- **Pivot:** No payment, but at least three buyers identify the same narrower paid problem.
- **Fail:** No payment and no coherent paid alternative.

### Expected outcomes

One payment, a short objection list, and a clearer view of trust and price friction.

### Failure signals

- Buyers request only free information.
- Specialist-only needs dominate.
- No decision-maker attends.
- Prospects will not fund external costs.

### Lessons to capture

Exact trigger, source, customer language, alternative chosen, trust concern, price objection, and whether the offer removed a real workload.

## E02: Problem Interviews with a Paid Ask

### Objective

Determine whether coordination pain is frequent, consequential, and purchasable rather than merely annoying.

### Hypothesis

At least three of ten triggered prospects report material cross-provider burden, and at least one accepts a paid next step.

### Materials required

- Interview guide.
- Neutral note template.
- Qualification rubric.
- Approved paid offer.

### Execution steps

1. Recruit ten prospects with active or recently completed Japan launches.
2. Ask about the most recent process, providers, repeated work, internal hours, delays, and alternatives.
3. Do not describe Hajime until the behavioral interview is complete.
4. Qualify current prospects.
5. Make the paid offer to qualified active prospects.
6. Ask recent launchers what they actually paid to remove and would change.

### Metrics

- Interviews completed.
- Prospects with three or more interdependent needs.
- Internal hours and consequences reported.
- Existing spend.
- Qualified paid asks.
- Payments.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** Three material coordination cases and one payment.
- **Pivot:** Pain exists but concentrates in a different segment or narrower service.
- **Fail:** Pain is low-consequence, information-only, or never purchased.

### Expected outcomes

A behavioral problem map and direct evidence distinguishing a service problem from an educational-content problem.

### Failure signals

- Hypothetical answers dominate.
- Interviewees lack a dated project.
- Participants praise the concept but decline a concrete offer.

### Lessons to capture

Tasks customers delegated, tasks kept internally, actual spend, decision-makers, cost of delay, and language used for the pain.

## E03: Manual Paid Readiness Pilot

### Objective

Test whether Hajime can deliver valuable readiness work manually at viable labor cost.

### Hypothesis

Three paid customers will rate the work at least 8/10, identify a changed decision or workload, and produce positive contribution margin.

### Materials required

- Signed scope and payment.
- Structured intake.
- Readiness report template.
- Responsibility/dependency map.
- Time and direct-cost log.
- Post-delivery interview.

### Execution steps

1. Begin only after payment and complete intake.
2. Record time by intake, analysis, partner coordination, production, review, and rework.
3. Produce only contracted deliverables.
4. Route professional questions rather than answering them.
5. Review the output with the customer.
6. Ask what changed, what was unnecessary, and what they would pay to continue.
7. Calculate contribution using a realistic founder labor rate.

### Metrics

- Delivery hours and elapsed days.
- Direct cost.
- Scope changes and repeated requests.
- On-time delivery.
- Value rating.
- Changed decision, sequence, provider, or workload.
- Contribution margin.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** Median rating >=8/10, positive contribution, and concrete behavioral value across three customers.
- **Pivot:** Value is high but one deliverable or customer type drives excessive labor.
- **Fail:** Median rating <6/10 or margin remains non-positive after one correction.

### Expected outcomes

Actual delivery cost, a reduced standard scope, and evidence about what customers value.

### Failure signals

- Report restates public information.
- Founder cannot separate coordination from professional advice.
- Open-ended communication dominates labor.
- Customer cannot name an action changed by the work.

### Lessons to capture

High-value artifact, low-value artifact, bespoke tasks, rework cause, boundary questions, and repeatable workflow percentage.

## E04: Four-Partner Referral Sprint

### Objective

Test whether professional partners see Hajime as useful and will refer qualified business.

### Hypothesis

Four active pilots will generate two qualified referrals and one payment within 28 days.

### Materials required

- Ideal partner criteria.
- One-page Hajime boundary and customer profile.
- Referral/handoff template.
- Partner interview questions.
- Referral tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Contact at least ten potential partners across accounting, judicial scrivener, immigration/administrative scrivener, and business law.
2. Ask about recent foreign-founder matters and unpaid coordination burden.
3. Explain what Hajime owns and does not own.
4. Invite four suitable partners into a no-fee, non-exclusive pilot.
5. Agree on qualification, consent, response, and escalation expectations.
6. Follow up weekly with useful context, not repeated referral requests.
7. Stop and decide at Day 28. A second sprint is a separate experiment testing recurrence.

### Metrics

- Partners contacted and met.
- Pilots accepted.
- Qualified referrals.
- Referral-to-discovery and referral-to-paid conversion.
- Outbound partner introductions.
- Partner response and second-referral rate.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** Two qualified referrals and one paid customer by Day 28.
- **Pivot:** Partners value delivery help but require a different handoff or customer profile.
- **Fail:** No qualified referrals from four genuinely active partners.

### Expected outcomes

Identification of the highest-leverage partner type and whether Hajime reduces or adds professional workload.

### Failure signals

- Partners view Hajime as competition.
- They demand commissions or exclusivity before evidence.
- Referred prospects are unqualified.
- Partner scopes or fees are unclear.

### Lessons to capture

Partner motivation, trust requirement, ideal referral trigger, objections, handoff friction, and reciprocal value.

## E05: $1,500 Pricing Cohort

### Objective

Test premium readiness pricing without confounding scope or segment.

### Hypothesis

At least two of five comparable qualified prospects will accept the $1,500 fixed-fee readiness offer.

### Materials required

- Stable readiness scope.
- Qualification rubric.
- Standard proposal.
- Pricing and loss-reason tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Select five prospects from the same broad segment and stage.
2. Use the same discovery process, scope, price, and payment terms.
3. Do not discount.
4. Record objections and alternatives.
5. Deliver accepted work and calculate contribution.
6. Test $2,500 only after two $1,500 acceptances; test a lower price only with genuinely reduced scope.

### Metrics

- Acceptance rate.
- Days to payment.
- Price objections.
- Delivery hours and margin.
- Value rating.
- Upgrade interest.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** >=30% acceptance, positive contribution, and median value rating >=8/10.
- **Pivot:** Buyers value the work but consistently require narrower scope or different payment terms.
- **Fail:** Zero of five qualified prospects accept at $1,500.

### Expected outcomes

A defensible starting price or evidence that the entry offer is economically invalid.

### Failure signals

- Price compared only with filing fees.
- Founder discounts to close.
- Accepted work is unprofitable.

### Lessons to capture

Price anchor, budget authority, perceived risk, payment-term concern, and whether trust or price actually caused the loss.

## E06: Trigger-Based LinkedIn Outreach

### Objective

Test whether publicly visible Japan triggers can produce qualified discovery efficiently.

### Hypothesis

Seventy-five tailored contacts will produce at least nine positive replies, five qualified discoveries, and one payment.

### Materials required

- Trigger definitions.
- Named prospect list.
- Two short message variants.
- Outreach and outcome tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Identify companies with Japanese customers, partners, hiring, accelerator participation, fundraising, or relocation signals.
2. Verify company stage and decision-maker.
3. Send a short message tied to the observed trigger.
4. Ask for a 20-minute comparison of the current launch plan.
5. Send one follow-up after five business days.
6. Qualify replies and make paid offers normally.

### Metrics

- Messages sent.
- Positive reply rate.
- Discoveries and qualification rate.
- Offers and payments.
- Founder hours per discovery.
- Results by trigger and message.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** >=12% positive replies, five qualified discoveries, and one payment.
- **Pivot:** Replies occur but one trigger or segment dominates.
- **Fail:** <5% positive replies or zero qualified discoveries after 75 contacts.

### Expected outcomes

A repeatable prospecting signal or evidence that direct outreach is too expensive in founder time.

### Failure signals

- Generic messages outperform only by attracting unqualified curiosity.
- Trigger evidence is stale.
- Replies come from non-decision-makers without introductions.

### Lessons to capture

Best trigger, title, message language, objection, source quality, and hours per qualified opportunity.

## E07: Trust-Source Comparison

### Objective

Measure whether warm, partner-referred, and direct prospects differ materially in conversion and sales effort.

### Hypothesis

Warm or professional referrals will close at least 15 percentage points better or require at least 30% fewer founder sales hours than direct outreach.

### Materials required

- Common qualification and sales process.
- Accurate lead-source field.
- Sales-hour and outcome tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Tag every prospect as warm founder, professional partner, or direct.
2. Use the same offer and qualification rules.
3. Track sales hours, days, objections, proposals, and payments.
4. Review after at least five qualified prospects in two source groups.

### Metrics

- Qualification, proposal, and payment conversion by source.
- Founder hours per payment.
- Days to close.
- Average selling price.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** One source materially outperforms and receives 60% of acquisition time next cycle.
- **Inconclusive:** Sample is insufficient or differences are small.
- **Fail:** No source produces payment; return to offer/segment assumptions.

### Expected outcomes

An evidence-based acquisition allocation rather than a channel preference.

### Failure signals

- Source attribution is missing.
- Different offers are used by source.
- One unusually large opportunity distorts results.

### Lessons to capture

Trust transfer, education required, objection mix, and source-specific customer quality.

## E08: One-Page Offer with Targeted Traffic

### Objective

Test whether validated sales language can convert targeted visitors into qualified discovery.

### Hypothesis

One hundred targeted visitors will produce at least eight bookings and four qualified discoveries.

### Materials required

- One-page readiness offer.
- Qualification form and booking link.
- Basic analytics.
- Known targeted sources.

### Execution steps

1. Use language from paid customers and discovery, not invented marketing copy.
2. State target customer, trigger, deliverable, price, boundaries, and next step.
3. Send traffic from outreach, partners, and qualified communities.
4. Exclude team and irrelevant traffic.
5. Track booking, attendance, qualification, proposal, and payment.

### Metrics

- Targeted visitors.
- Visitor-to-booking conversion.
- Booking completion and qualification.
- Paid conversion by source.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** >=8% booking conversion, four qualified discoveries, and at least one paid outcome or active proposal.
- **Pivot:** Visitors qualify but do not book, indicating trust or call-to-action friction.
- **Fail:** <3% booking after 100 targeted visitors and two message variants.

### Expected outcomes

Evidence about message clarity, not proof of market size.

### Failure signals

- Traffic is broad or untraceable.
- Email signups are counted as commercial validation.
- Page changes occur before sample completion.

### Lessons to capture

Source quality, message, objections, form abandonment, and qualified conversion.

## E09: Founder-Community Office Hours

### Objective

Test whether a concentrated founder community contains triggered buyers and can transfer trust.

### Hypothesis

One host-curated session will produce eight suitable attendees, three qualified follow-ups, and one paid offer acceptance.

### Materials required

- Qualified host or accelerator.
- Attendee criteria.
- 45-minute working-session outline.
- Paid follow-up offer.

### Execution steps

1. Select a host that can nominate companies with active Japan plans.
2. Avoid a general incorporation lecture.
3. Work through a responsibility/dependency scenario.
4. Clearly separate professional questions.
5. Offer private qualification and paid readiness follow-up.
6. Contact qualified attendees within one business day.

### Metrics

- Registrants and suitable attendees.
- Follow-up requests.
- Qualified discoveries.
- Offers and payments.
- Founder hours and host willingness to repeat.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** Three qualified discoveries and one payment.
- **Pivot:** Host provides qualified introductions but the group event adds little.
- **Fail:** Audience is aspiration-stage or produces no qualified follow-up.

### Expected outcomes

A repeatable community channel or a decision to use only direct host introductions.

### Failure signals

- High attendance with no active projects.
- Session becomes free consulting.
- Host cannot screen participants.

### Lessons to capture

Host quality, trigger distribution, questions asked, paid follow-up interest, and event preparation cost.

## E10: High-Intent Google Ads Kill Test

### Objective

Determine whether high-intent search can produce qualified opportunities at a tolerable acquisition cost.

### Hypothesis

A $300-$500 exact/phrase-match test will produce at least three qualified discoveries or one payment.

### Materials required

- Validated one-page offer.
- Conversion tracking.
- Exact/phrase keywords tied to subsidiary, office, hire, or company-launch intent.
- Negative keyword list.
- Daily budget cap.

### Execution steps

1. Run only after two manual sales.
2. Use validated message and price.
3. Exclude free, jobs, tourism, school, template, and visa-only terms.
4. Review search terms daily and add negatives.
5. Stop at $500 or 14 days.
6. Qualify every booking and attribute payment.

### Metrics

- Spend, clicks, and targeted bookings.
- Qualified discoveries.
- Cost per qualified discovery.
- Proposals, payments, and customer acquisition cost.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** Three qualified discoveries at <=$170 each or one paid customer with acquisition cost below first-project contribution.
- **Pivot:** Search intent exists but keywords attract the wrong segment.
- **Fail:** $500 spent with no qualified proposal.

### Expected outcomes

A quick channel rejection or a small scalable search path.

### Failure signals

- Cheap clicks but no qualified intent.
- Visa-only demand dominates.
- Sales labor makes total acquisition cost uneconomic.

### Lessons to capture

Search terms, intent, qualification, acquisition cost including founder time, and message mismatch.

## E11: Post-Delivery Referral Ask

### Objective

Test whether demonstrated value generates named customer introductions.

### Hypothesis

At least 30% of satisfied completed customers will introduce one similar prospect within 14 days.

### Materials required

- Customer value rating.
- Specific ideal-customer description.
- Short introduction language.
- Referral tracker.

### Execution steps

1. Ask what changed because of the engagement.
2. If rating is >=8/10, describe one specific customer profile.
3. Ask for one named introduction, not names to contact without consent.
4. Provide a two-sentence forwardable note.
5. Follow up once after the next meaningful launch milestone.

### Metrics

- Eligible customers.
- Asks made.
- Named introductions.
- Qualified referrals and payments.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** >=30% introduction rate and at least one qualified referral from five customers.
- **Pivot:** Customers agree but need a different timing or profile.
- **Fail:** Zero introductions after five explicit asks from satisfied customers.

### Expected outcomes

Evidence of organic trust compounding or proof that purchase frequency/referral density is weak.

### Failure signals

- Asking before value is demonstrated.
- General "keep us in mind" language.
- Introductions are unqualified.

### Lessons to capture

Best ask timing, customer language, referral profile, and referral-to-paid conversion.

## E12: Upgrade and Stabilization Offer

### Objective

Test whether readiness produces larger or recurring revenue.

### Hypothesis

At least 30% of five satisfied eligible readiness customers will buy a launch upgrade or scoped three-month stabilization service.

### Materials required

- Completed readiness engagement.
- Clear open-work map.
- Fixed upgrade or stabilization scope.
- Price, limits, and payment terms.

### Execution steps

1. Identify remaining coordination work from the readiness output.
2. Offer only the smallest useful next scope.
3. Keep professional and external fees separate.
4. Require payment before continued work.
5. Track delivery hours, margin, usage, and customer outcome.

### Metrics

- Eligible customers.
- Offers and acceptances.
- Upgrade value and attachment rate.
- Delivery hours and contribution.
- Stabilization completion/retention.

### Decision rules

- **Pass:** >=30% acceptance and positive contribution.
- **Pivot:** Customers want a narrower project rather than recurring service.
- **Fail:** Zero of five satisfied eligible customers buy more.

### Expected outcomes

Evidence for LTV beyond the first engagement or a decision to operate as a one-time service.

### Failure signals

- Offer invents work the customer does not need.
- Stabilization becomes unlimited support.
- Customer prefers the accountant or secretary to own all follow-on work.

### Lessons to capture

Purchased next outcome, rejected work, willingness to delegate, support burden, and margin.

## Common experiment record

For every run, record:

- Experiment ID and version.
- Assumption tested.
- Start/end date.
- Target and actual sample.
- Cash and founder hours.
- Raw funnel and delivery results.
- Deviations from the playbook.
- Pass, pivot, fail, or inconclusive.
- Decision changed.
- Next experiment, if any.

If the result does not change a decision, the experiment was poorly designed and should not be repeated unchanged.
