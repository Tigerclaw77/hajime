# Hajime Japan Customer Entry Points

## Entry-point architecture

Entry points are acquisition and qualification lenses, not separate products. Every qualified customer enters the same shared coordination system:

1. Commercial trigger and readiness.
2. Business, ownership, activity, and city facts.
3. Professional questions and responsibility assignment.
4. Entity/branch/other route decided by qualified advisers and the customer.
5. Address, capital, evidence, and filing dependencies.
6. Formation coordination.
7. Tax/accounting, bank readiness, insurance, employment, and operating handoffs as applicable.
8. Launch closeout and ongoing-obligation ownership.

Every entry page should promise the same outcome: one accountable coordinator, a clear plan, visible dependencies, and the next action. It must also state that licensed professionals provide regulated advice and services.

## 1. Start a SaaS Company in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Japanese customer contracting and local credibility.
- Entity timing versus pilot or distributor activity.
- Founder/director location, capital, office, banking, tax, IP, and first hires.
- Existing overseas company ownership and headquarters documents.

### Messaging

> Turn a real Japan customer, partner, or hiring opportunity into an operating company without making the founder manage every local provider.

Avoid promising faster incorporation or a bank account. Lead with preserving founder time and protecting the commercial trigger.

### Expected partners

- Judicial scrivener and business attorney.
- Accountant/tax adviser.
- Administrative scrivener if immigration is involved.
- Office/address provider.
- Banking-readiness and payroll/employment partners.

### Unique requirements

- IP ownership and intercompany agreements routed to counsel.
- Stock option, funding, and governance questions.
- Enterprise customer procurement and data/privacy dependencies.
- First-hire sequencing.

### Shared workflow components

Readiness, formation, address, capital, professional coordination, bank readiness, accounting/tax handoff, and launch closeout.

## 2. Expand Your Existing Business into Japan

### Primary concerns

- Subsidiary, branch, distributor, or other operating route.
- Headquarters approvals and documents.
- Local management, reporting, office, finance, employment, and recurring compliance.
- Commercial launch date and customer obligations.

### Messaging

> Give headquarters one coordinated Japan launch plan across local professionals, documents, operational setup, and reporting.

### Expected partners

- International business attorney.
- Judicial scrivener.
- Accountant/tax and transfer-pricing advisers.
- Payroll/labor provider.
- Office provider or licensed broker.
- Bank, insurer, and translator.

### Unique requirements

- Foreign-parent certificates, resolutions, notarization, apostille, translation, and signatory coordination.
- Intercompany agreements and accounting/reporting alignment.
- More stakeholders and formal approvals.

### Shared workflow components

Readiness, route questions, formation, evidence, address, capital, bank readiness, accounting, employment, and closeout.

## 3. Open a Japanese Subsidiary

### Primary concerns

- Corporate route and governance decisions owned by advisers and headquarters.
- Parent-company evidence and beneficial ownership.
- Director/signatory arrangements.
- Capital transfer, office, bank, tax, payroll, and recurring governance.

### Messaging

> Coordinate the work between headquarters and Japan so the new subsidiary is operational, not merely registered.

### Expected partners

- Corporate attorney and judicial scrivener.
- International and Japanese tax/accounting firms.
- Bank-readiness, payroll, labor, and corporate-secretarial partners.
- Translation/notarization providers.

### Unique requirements

- Board and shareholder approvals.
- Parent-company documents and certifications.
- Intercompany service, IP, funding, and transfer-pricing questions.
- Headquarters reporting and procurement.

### Shared workflow components

The complete formation core plus multi-stakeholder governance and post-launch operating handoff.

## 4. Launch an E-commerce Business in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Whether a local entity is needed for the operating model.
- Consumption tax, customs, importer roles, product requirements, labeling, marketplace, logistics, returns, and payments.
- Warehousing, address, banking, and customer support.

### Messaging

> Coordinate the entity, tax, logistics, marketplace, and provider decisions behind a credible Japan commerce launch.

Do not market the size of Japan's e-commerce economy as proof that a specific product will succeed. METI reported a JPY 26.1 trillion domestic B2C e-commerce market in 2024, but product demand and formation need remain customer-specific ([METI](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2025/0826_003.html)).

### Expected partners

- E-commerce tax and customs advisers.
- Customs broker/importer and freight/fulfillment providers.
- Product/labeling specialist.
- Marketplace specialist.
- Attorney, judicial scrivener, accountant, bank-readiness, and insurer.

### Unique requirements

- Product-category acceptance gate.
- Importer-of-record and customs responsibilities.
- Marketplace account eligibility and ownership.
- Inventory, returns, consumer rules, and indirect tax.

### Shared workflow components

Readiness, formation, address, capital, professional coordination, bank readiness, accounting, and closeout, with a commerce workstream added.

## 5. Start a Consulting or Agency Business in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Customer contracts, invoicing, entity choice questions, tax/accounting, address, banking, and founder status.
- Whether the customer truly needs a company now.
- Separating personal and business administration.

### Messaging

> Get a clear, professionally supported route from active client work to an operating Japan business.

### Expected partners

- Accountant/tax adviser.
- Judicial scrivener and attorney as needed.
- Administrative scrivener if immigration questions exist.
- Address and banking-readiness providers.

### Unique requirements

- Tight qualification for customer evidence and budget.
- Clear boundary between business coordination and personal tax/immigration advice.
- Paid Blueprint as the default entry product.

### Shared workflow components

Readiness, formation, address, professional questions, accounting, bank readiness, and closeout.

## 6. Establish a Japan Employer Entity

### Primary concerns

- Entity versus EOR or other options, determined with advisers.
- First-hire date, payroll, labor/social insurance, benefits, employment terms, and management.
- Bank and funding sequence.

### Messaging

> Coordinate the company and employment workstreams behind your first Japan team, with each professional decision owned by the right specialist.

### Expected partners

- Employment attorney.
- Labor and social-insurance consultant.
- Payroll and benefits provider.
- Accountant/tax adviser.
- Judicial scrivener, office, bank-readiness, and immigration professional.

### Unique requirements

- Candidate and payroll deadlines.
- Employment classification and agreement questions.
- Social-insurance and withholding setup.
- EOR transition plan where applicable.

### Shared workflow components

Formation core plus employment readiness, payroll handoff, and recurring provider coordination.

## 7. Import, Export, or Distribute Products in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Product admissibility, importer/exporter roles, customs, tax, contracts, logistics, insurance, and local sales.
- Distributor versus owned-operation route.
- Warehouse and inventory control.

### Messaging

> Put the corporate, trade, tax, logistics, and distribution work in one coordinated launch plan.

### Expected partners

- Trade/customs adviser and customs broker.
- Product compliance specialist.
- Freight, warehouse, and distribution partners.
- Attorney, tax/accounting, insurance, bank-readiness, and formation professionals.

### Unique requirements

- Category-specific licenses or notifications.
- Incoterms and commercial contract questions.
- Customs valuation, duties, consumption tax, and evidence.
- Product liability and cargo insurance.

### Shared workflow components

Formation core with trade, logistics, and product workstreams.

## 8. Open a Restaurant or Franchise in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Site, lease, franchise rights, entity, capital, permits, construction, food/alcohol requirements, employment, tax, insurance, and opening date.
- The order in which site and regulatory commitments can safely be made.

### Messaging

> Coordinate the company, site, permits, professionals, and opening dependencies behind your first Japan location.

### Expected partners

- Franchise/corporate attorney.
- Licensed commercial broker and office/site professionals.
- Administrative scrivener and local permit specialists.
- Architect, contractor, food/alcohol specialists.
- Accountant, payroll/labor provider, insurer, and bank-readiness contacts.

### Unique requirements

- Physical site and construction critical path.
- Local permits and inspections.
- Franchise, trademark, supply, and food-safety questions.
- Staffing and operating readiness.

### Shared workflow components

Readiness and formation core, expanded into a location-launch program. Do not promote this entry point before the dedicated bench exists.

## 9. Establish Manufacturing, R&D, or a Service Center

### Primary concerns

- Entity, site, incentives, equipment, customs, environment/safety, employment, IP, tax, and headquarters governance.
- Phased launch from representative sales or R&D to larger operations.

### Messaging

> Give headquarters one Japan program owner across corporate setup, site, specialists, staffing, and operational handoff.

### Expected partners

- Corporate and sector counsel.
- Tax/accounting and incentives advisers.
- Commercial/industrial real-estate professionals.
- Environmental, safety, permitting, customs, and engineering specialists.
- Payroll/labor, recruitment, bank, and insurance providers.

### Unique requirements

- Site due diligence and incentives.
- Equipment/import and sector permissions.
- IP, R&D, data, and employment requirements.
- Enterprise reporting and procurement.

### Shared workflow components

Formation core plus physical-site, sector, and program-governance workstreams.

## 10. Launch Portfolio Companies in Japan

### Primary concerns

- Repeatable readiness, consistent provider quality, portfolio reporting, founder autonomy, and cost predictability.
- Identifying which portfolio companies are actually ready.

### Messaging

> Give portfolio founders a consistent Japan launch route without forcing every company to rebuild the local provider network.

### Expected partners

- Investor/accelerator program owner.
- Panel of attorneys, scriveners, accountants, immigration, employment, and office providers.
- Sector specialists by portfolio composition.

### Unique requirements

- Program qualification rules.
- Per-company consent and direct professional relationships.
- Portfolio reporting that protects confidential information.
- Volume capacity and conflicts.

### Shared workflow components

The same per-company readiness and formation workflow, with portfolio governance layered above it.

## Entry-point release gate

Publish or actively promote a dedicated entry point only when:

- At least three qualified prospects independently express the need.
- At least one customer has paid for the workflow or a partner has brought a qualified live case.
- The common core covers at least 70% of the work.
- Unique requirements are named and scoped.
- Two viable qualified partner routes exist for critical work where possible.
- Pricing supports expected coordination effort.
- Marketing has boundary and factual review.

If these conditions fail, serve the case selectively through the general "Expand into Japan" path rather than creating another market promise.
