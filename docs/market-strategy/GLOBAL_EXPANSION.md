# Hajime Global Expansion Strategy

## Global thesis

Hajime should export its coordination operating model, not its Japan provider network or assumptions. The reusable platform is:

- Commercial-trigger qualification.
- Readiness planning.
- Responsibility and dependency mapping.
- Provider selection and handoff.
- Consolidated evidence and decision requests.
- Status, escalation, and next-action communication.
- Operational closeout and recurring-obligation handoff.

Entity types, professional boundaries, ownership evidence, tax, immigration, licensing, banking, employment, and filing workflows remain country-specific and must be owned locally.

## Expansion prerequisites

Do not launch another country until:

- Japan has at least 50 completed paid launches.
- A non-founder team reliably delivers Japan work.
- At least three existing customers or channel partners present qualified demand for the same next country.
- The country has a named local operating lead.
- Two viable qualified partners exist for each critical professional capability where possible.
- Local counsel approves service boundaries, contracts, referral economics, marketing, data handling, and complaint routes.
- A manual pilot proves price, margin, and customer satisfaction before software localization.

## Evaluation model

Scores are 1-5 and directional as of 2026-07-12.

| Factor | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Cross-border demand | 20% | Foreign founders and businesses repeatedly establish local operations |
| Coordination pain | 20% | Launch involves fragmented providers and meaningful dependencies |
| Premium willingness to pay | 15% | Target customers can fund coordination beyond filing fees |
| Platform reuse | 15% | Hajime's core workflow transfers with limited structural change |
| Partner accessibility | 15% | Strong bilingual professional ecosystem can be governed |
| Localization/risk | 15% | Higher score means lower localization, regulatory, and operating risk |

The United States is attractive but placed last because state/federal variation, professional regulation, tax, banking, immigration, and litigation exposure make a broad launch incompatible with controlled early expansion. Estonia scores well operationally but its digital ecosystem reduces the coordination pain Hajime is paid to solve.

## 1. Singapore

### Market attractiveness

High. Singapore is an international business hub with a large, active formation ecosystem. ACRA reported 632,478 active business entities and roughly 5,400-7,400 monthly formations in the first half of 2026 ([ACRA business registry statistics](https://www.acra.gov.sg/resources/business-registry-statistics/)). Those totals include domestic and simple formations, so Hajime's premium serviceable segment is a small subset.

### Platform reuse

Very high. English-language service, clear corporate administration, and strong professional infrastructure allow the Japan coordination model to transfer.

### Partner differences

- Corporate secretarial provider is central.
- Local director, registered office, tax, employment, banking, immigration, and licensing capabilities require Singapore-specific partners.
- Competition includes mature incorporation and corporate-services platforms.

### Workflow differences

- More digitally standardized formation.
- Corporate secretary and recurring statutory administration are central from inception.
- Differentiation must be operational expansion coordination, not filing access.

### Localization effort

Low-medium: legal, tax, corporate-secretarial, privacy, and employment localization; English customer experience largely reusable.

### Expansion role

First country pilot because it is the cleanest test of portability. Launch through one channel or existing-customer cohort, not a broad public campaign.

## 2. UAE

### Market attractiveness

Very high for premium coordination. The country combines substantial inward investment with mainland/free-zone choices, visas, offices, banking, and activity-specific licensing. The UAE's Ministry of Investment publishes an active foreign-direct-investment strategy and ecosystem materials ([UAE Foreign Direct Investment Report 2025](https://www.investuae.gov.ae/assets/UAE_Foreign_Direct_Investment_Report_2025_EN.pdf)).

### Platform reuse

High at the responsibility, dependency, evidence, provider, and status layers.

### Partner differences

- Local corporate-service, free-zone/mainland, legal, tax, immigration, office, banking, and beneficial-ownership partners.
- Emirate and zone capability must be explicit.
- Provider incentives and package commissions require strict governance.

### Workflow differences

- Jurisdiction/zone and licensed-activity decisions create an early critical path.
- Residence, establishment cards, immigration, office, and banking may be tightly connected.
- Provider-bundled offers are common; Hajime must remain transparent and independent.

### Localization effort

Medium-high: emirate/zone variation, Arabic documents where applicable, professional and commercial licensing, tax, immigration, and referral practices.

### Expansion role

Second country and first major premium scale market after Singapore proves portability.

## 3. United Kingdom

### Market attractiveness

High volume and excellent partner access. Companies House reported 801,871 incorporations and a 5.43 million-company register for 2024-25 ([Companies House annual report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-to-2025/companies-house-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-to-2025)). Most incorporations do not need premium coordination.

### Platform reuse

Very high for English-language customer experience, corporate workflow, partner handoff, and recurring-obligation coordination.

### Partner differences

- Company formation agents, accountants, solicitors, registered-office, payroll, banking, immigration, and sector advisers.
- Identity verification and Companies House reform must be tracked.

### Workflow differences

- Basic formation is fast and inexpensive; Hajime must target international operations, subsidiaries, employment, banking, and cross-border handoffs.
- The premium problem begins after or around formation, not in filing forms.

### Localization effort

Low-medium, with UK legal, tax, AML, data, employment, and corporate-registry controls.

### Expansion role

Third, after the Asian operating model works in two markets. Strong base for European and North American channel relationships.

## 4. Australia

### Market attractiveness

High. ASIC reported more than 3.5 million registered companies by April 2025, while the government states a standard company application can take about 15 minutes and confirmation generally arrives within two business days when complete ([ASIC statistics](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/statistics/company-registration-statistics/), [business.gov.au registration guide](https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-a-company)). Volume is large, but filing-only value is low.

### Platform reuse

Very high for English-language coordination and professional handoffs.

### Partner differences

- ASIC/company registration, tax/ABN/GST, resident-director, foreign-company, employment, immigration, banking, and foreign-investment specialists.
- Registered foreign companies need a local agent and certified documents ([Australian Government foreign-company registration](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/print/ag/registration-as-a-foreign-company/165)).

### Workflow differences

- Federal corporate registration with separate tax and business registrations.
- Foreign investment and state licensing may add layers.
- Target foreign subsidiaries, hiring, and operating launches rather than ordinary domestic formations.

### Localization effort

Medium.

### Expansion role

Fourth-wave English common-law market; can follow UK or run as a shared English-market program after local partner readiness.

## 5. Canada

### Market attractiveness

High for cross-border SMBs, technology companies, and U.S.-adjacent expansion, but basic federal formation is commoditized. Corporations Canada states online basic incorporation costs CAD 200 and can be completed within one business day ([Corporations Canada](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/start-business)).

### Platform reuse

Very high for core coordination.

### Partner differences

- Federal and provincial corporate, tax, legal, registered-office, employment, immigration, banking, and language capabilities.
- Quebec and bilingual service require deliberate localization.

### Workflow differences

- Choice between federal and provincial incorporation.
- Provincial registrations and operating obligations can multiply.
- Annual return and significant-control information are recurring corporate duties ([Corporations Canada annual returns](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/annual-return-business-corporations)).

### Localization effort

Medium; higher for multi-province or Quebec work.

### Expansion role

Pair with the UK/Australia English-market capability, targeting foreign operations rather than basic domestic startups.

## 6. South Korea

### Market attractiveness

Medium-high. Geographic proximity and similar foreign-entry coordination pain create strategic fit. Invest Korea describes foreign-invested corporations, branches, and liaison offices, with a foreign-invested-company threshold that includes KRW 100 million and at least 10% voting ownership under the cited framework ([Invest Korea](https://www.investkorea.org/ik-en/cntnts/i-341/web.do)).

### Platform reuse

Medium. The coordination concepts transfer; provider, document, language, banking, and government workflows must be rebuilt.

### Partner differences

- Korean corporate legal, tax/accounting, labor, immigration, banking, address, and translation partners.
- Strong local-language operating lead is essential.

### Workflow differences

- Foreign investment reporting and capital remittance are central.
- Corporate seals, translations, bank and registration dependencies need local sequencing.
- Customer communication may need Korean and English.

### Localization effort

High.

### Expansion role

Strategic Northeast Asia expansion after English-language portability is proven. It strengthens Hajime's regional identity more than an early Estonia or Portugal launch.

## 7. Portugal

### Market attractiveness

Medium. International founders and relocation-linked entrepreneurs create demand, but immigration and tax can dominate the service. Portugal generally applies non-discrimination to foreign investment and provides online/on-the-spot incorporation routes; non-EU/EEA non-residents may require a Portuguese tax representative ([AICEP incorporation guide](https://investinportugal.portugalglobal.pt/doing-business/incorporate-a-company/)).

### Platform reuse

Medium.

### Partner differences

- Portuguese lawyer/solicitor, accountant, tax representative, immigration, banking, address, and translation partners.

### Workflow differences

- Tax numbers and representation can precede incorporation.
- Apostille, translation, immigration, and EU/non-EU distinctions matter.
- Simple formation routes reduce value unless broader relocation or operations are involved.

### Localization effort

High due to Portuguese language and immigration/tax boundaries.

### Expansion role

Selective later market, ideally through existing customers or a strong local operating partner.

## 8. Estonia

### Market attractiveness

Moderate as a formation market, strong as a digital partner ecosystem. Estonia reports more than 139,000 e-residents and 41,000 companies created by e-residents; e-residents formed 5,556 companies in 2025 ([e-Residency dashboard](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/dashboard/), [2025 results](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/es/blog/posts/e-residents-generated-record-state-revenue-2025/)).

### Platform reuse

Very high technically and operationally.

### Partner differences

- E-residency, contact-person/address, accounting/tax, banking/payment, legal, and marketplace providers.

### Workflow differences

- Highly digital and already served by more than 100 marketplace providers.
- Low coordination pain for straightforward remote companies.

### Localization effort

Low-medium.

### Expansion role

Do not build a full Estonia country operation early. Use Estonia as a partner/channel integration or as one option in later multi-country readiness work when a customer has genuine EU operating needs.

## 9. United States

### Market attractiveness

Enormous, with continuous business-application volume tracked by the U.S. Census Bureau ([Business Formation Statistics](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/index.html)). Market size does not make it an appropriate early country.

### Platform reuse

Low-medium because entity, tax, licensing, employment, and professional rules vary by state and customer activity.

### Partner differences

- State-specific corporate counsel/registered agents, federal and state tax, banking, employment, immigration, licensing, insurance, and beneficial-ownership specialists.
- Professional and referral rules can differ by state.

### Workflow differences

- State formation plus federal, state, and local registrations.
- Multi-state nexus, employment, sales tax, licensing, and litigation exposure.
- Broad "United States formation" is not one stable workflow.

### Localization effort

Very high.

### Expansion role

Last among the listed countries. Begin only with one state, one customer segment, and one partner-led route supported by existing demand. Do not advertise nationwide coverage until state expansion is individually governed.

## Recommended expansion sequence

1. **Singapore portability pilot:** 3-5 existing or partner-referred customers, manual delivery, one local lead.
2. **UAE premium pilot:** one emirate/zone route and a tightly defined founder/SMB segment.
3. **United Kingdom:** international subsidiaries and operating launches, not basic filing.
4. **Australia and Canada:** add through a shared English-market operating team with country-specific panels.
5. **South Korea:** build a bilingual Northeast Asia capability.
6. **Portugal:** selective relocation/operating launches with strong local ownership.
7. **Estonia:** partner/channel offering rather than a full operating country.
8. **United States:** one-state pilot after mature multi-country governance.

## Global brand architecture

- **Corporate brand:** Hajime.
- **Country promise:** "Establish and operate in [country] with one accountable coordinator."
- **Entry path:** SaaS, subsidiary, commerce, employer, franchise, or other proven customer context.
- **Local ownership:** named country coordinator and visible licensed professionals.
- **Shared standard:** current step, next step, responsible party, timeframe, assumptions, and boundaries.

Do not create separate brands for industries or countries. The customer entry point changes; the trusted coordination identity does not.

## Global kill criteria

Pause or close a country pilot when:

- Fewer than three of 15 qualified prospects pay.
- No existing customer or repeatable channel produces demand.
- Local professional work cannot be separated clearly from Hajime coordination.
- Contribution margin remains non-positive after one scope/price correction.
- One provider controls a critical capability without backup.
- Customer satisfaction is below 8/10.
- Country operations require founder effort that damages Japan delivery.
- Marketing must rely on cheap filing, guaranteed outcomes, or partner commissions to convert.
