# Hajime Japan Partner Ecosystem

## Ecosystem design

Hajime should build a governed network, not a public directory. Partners perform work, refer customers, receive qualified opportunities, and provide capability redundancy. No partner buys placement or controls Hajime's recommendation.

Referral direction uses:

- **Inbound:** partner refers customers to Hajime.
- **Outbound:** Hajime introduces customers to partner.
- **Two-way:** both, based on fit and consent.

Relationship priority:

- **P1:** required for the core promise; executive-level relationship and backup provider required.
- **P2:** required for promoted entry paths; managed capability and performance review.
- **P3:** optional customer problem-solvers; use when needed.
- **P4:** future geography or vertical capability; research before activation.

### Core relationship standard

- Two viable providers per critical role where possible.
- Credentials and scope verified.
- Direct professional engagement for regulated work.
- Written handoff, consent, response, and escalation process.
- No guaranteed outcomes or undisclosed compensation.
- Matter-level performance review.
- Immediate suspension for serious conduct, confidentiality, or boundary concerns.

### Expansion activation rule

## Mutual value exchange

### What Hajime gives partners

- Qualified, transparent customers with a real trigger.
- Structured business, ownership, timing, and open-question context.
- Consolidated requests and one coordination contact.
- Clear attribution of professional advice.
- Reduced unpaid project management.
- Feedback on handoff quality and customer readiness.
- Repeat work without demanding exclusivity.

### What partners give Hajime

- Qualified professional delivery and honest scope limits.
- Clear evidence requirements, fees, assumptions, and response ranges.
- Early warning of changed facts or professional concerns.
- Capacity visibility and escalation contacts.
- Appropriate referrals when clients need cross-provider coordination.
- Participation in reviewed playbooks and readiness clinics.

## Revenue policy

Revenue priority is:

1. Customer-paid Hajime coordination.
2. Customer-paid partner professional or operating services under separate scopes.
3. Reciprocal referrals based on fit.
4. Disclosed customer-benefiting commercial arrangements only after legal, professional-rule, and conflict review.

Prohibited:

- Paid ranking.
- Hidden markups.
- Commission tied to approval.
- Personal coordinator incentives.
- Lead resale.
- Customer data sharing without consent.
- Degraded Hajime service when a customer chooses its own qualified provider.

## Partner lifecycle

1. **Discover:** capability and boundary interview.
2. **Verify:** credentials, experience, scope, pricing model, references, security, and conflicts.
3. **Pilot:** one or two non-exclusive matters with no paid referral required.
4. **Review:** customer feedback, accuracy, communication, rework, timing, and conduct.
5. **Preferred:** repeat performance and documented backup coverage.
6. **Suspend/remove:** capacity, quality, disclosure, confidentiality, complaint, or boundary failure.

## Ecosystem metrics

- Qualified referrals per active partner.
- Second-referral rate.
- Referral-to-paid conversion.
- Response and handoff time.
- Repeated-request and rework rate.
- Scope and fee variance.
- Customer rating by provider.
- Capacity failures and escalations.
- Revenue concentration.
- Conflicts, complaints, and boundary incidents.

The network is strong when partners repeatedly send appropriate customers and customers receive reliable options. Partner count alone is not a success metric.
