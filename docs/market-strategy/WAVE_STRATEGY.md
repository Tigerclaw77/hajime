# Hajime Japan Wave Strategy

## Sequencing principle

Each wave expands only after the previous wave produces paid demand, repeatable delivery, positive contribution margin, and reliable partner behavior. Calendar dates are planning windows, not automatic launch dates.

## Wave 1: 0-12 months - prove the coordination core

### Target segments

- Revenue-backed B2B SaaS and technology founders.
- Professional-services firms, consultancies, and agencies with existing customers.
- Venture-backed startups with a concrete Japan milestone.
- Profitable foreign SMBs opening a subsidiary or branch.
- Established foreign residents with real customers, a dated trigger, and willingness to pay for coordination.

### Why now

- Decision-makers are reachable without enterprise procurement.
- The segments share the core readiness, formation, address, capital, professional, banking, and accounting handoffs.
- Commercial triggers support faster sales and premium fees.
- Investors, accountants, attorneys, and founder networks can create recurring referrals.
- Manual delivery is sufficient to learn the workflow.

### Why not narrower

Wave 1 has one operating beachhead but several closely related customer descriptions. Hajime should not lock itself to SaaS forever; it should test whether the same coordination core works for founder-led services and owner-led SMBs without opening materially different delivery systems.

### Required capabilities

- Paid launch-readiness assessment.
- Standard qualification and professional-boundary process.
- Formation, address, capital, accounting/tax handoff, and bank-readiness coordination.
- Manual responsibility, dependency, status, and document tracking.
- Transparent proposals and third-party fee separation.
- Customer consent and secure information-handling process.
- Delivery economics and partner performance measurement.

### Likely partners

- Bilingual accountant/tax firm.
- Judicial scrivener.
- Administrative scrivener/immigration professional.
- International business attorney.
- Registered or serviced-office provider.
- Translation provider.
- Banking-readiness contacts without approval promises.

### Expected revenue profile

- Paid readiness: USD 1,500-2,500.
- Guided Launch: approximately USD 5,000-8,000.
- Concierge: approximately USD 10,000-18,000.
- Owner-led SMB/custom scope: USD 10,000-25,000+.
- Revenue is project-heavy and founder-delivered; adjacent commissions should be negligible.

These are validation hypotheses, not permanent list prices.

### Operational risks

- Over-customizing for the first customer.
- Mistaking visa or professional demand for coordination demand.
- Underpricing founder time.
- Accepting customers without a concrete trigger.
- Relying on one partner per capability.
- Building software before repeated paid work identifies a bottleneck.

### Exit gate

Move to Wave 2 only when:

- At least 10 customers have paid.
- Six or more share one coherent operating pattern.
- One channel has produced at least three customers.
- One partner has made a second qualified referral.
- Median customer value rating is at least 8/10.
- Contribution margin is positive with founder labor included.
- At least two qualified providers exist for each critical professional role.

## Wave 2: 12-24 months - add operational depth

### Target segments

- Established e-commerce brands.
- Import/export and distribution businesses.
- Remote-first companies creating a Japan employer entity.
- Higher-revenue Amazon or marketplace sellers requiring a local operating presence.
- Creators and media companies only when they operate credible revenue-producing businesses.
- Wave 1 customers purchasing post-launch coordination.

### Why now

- Wave 1 should have proven the common formation core and basic partner governance.
- These segments add repeatable operational work: customs, logistics, indirect tax, marketplace, payroll, labor, benefits, and ongoing provider coordination.
- Recurring post-launch revenue can begin without changing Hajime's identity.
- Commerce and employment partners can become both referral sources and delivery partners.

### Why not earlier

- Product, customs, logistics, payroll, and employment requirements introduce more failure points than standard founder launches.
- Marketplace sellers and creators require strict revenue and trigger qualification to avoid a low-budget support funnel.
- Remote-first customers compare Hajime with EOR and global entity-management platforms.
- The partner bench must support category-specific questions without Hajime giving advice.

### Required capabilities

- Product/category intake and unsupported-activity routing.
- Customs, importer, logistics, marketplace, and indirect-tax handoffs.
- Labor, payroll, benefits, and employment-professional coordination.
- Post-launch obligation and provider handoff process.
- Segment-specific entry pages using the same qualification and project core.
- Margin tracking by add-on and workstream.
- Stronger security, consent, and document-retention controls as data volume grows.

### Likely partners

- Customs broker and trade adviser.
- Freight forwarder, warehouse, and fulfillment provider.
- E-commerce/marketplace specialist.
- Indirect-tax and product-compliance professionals.
- Labor and social-insurance consultant.
- Payroll provider and employment attorney.
- Insurance broker.
- Telecom and office-operations providers.

### Expected revenue profile

- E-commerce/import launch: USD 8,000-25,000.
- Employer-entity launch: USD 7,500-20,000.
- Post-launch stabilization: USD 1,000-2,500 per month for a defined period.
- Optional coordination add-ons increase project value; customer-paid fees remain primary.
- Repeat and referral revenue should begin lowering acquisition cost.

### Operational risks

- Product categories creating hidden licensing or safety obligations.
- Open-ended support disguised as a project add-on.
- Referral commissions weakening neutral recommendations.
- Customers assuming Hajime controls customs, marketplace, bank, or payroll decisions.
- Too many entry pages creating different promises and inconsistent qualification.

### Exit gate

Move to Wave 3 only when:

- At least 25 cumulative customers have paid.
- Two or more Wave 2 entry paths each have at least five completed customers.
- Each entry path has positive contribution margin.
- At least 30% of eligible customers purchase a post-launch or repeat service.
- No critical partner category depends on one provider.
- Segment-specific incidents and scope changes are documented and controlled.
- A non-founder coordinator can deliver the common workflow at target quality.

## Wave 3: 24-36 months - institutional and physical expansion

### Target segments

- Manufacturers opening sales, service, R&D, or production operations.
- Restaurant and hospitality franchises.
- Enterprise subsidiaries and regional operating programs.
- Investor, accelerator, and portfolio launch programs.
- Selected deep-tech, research, education, or regulated verticals with dedicated partner benches.
- International holding structures only when counsel-defined and tied to genuine operations.

### Why now

- Hajime should have delivery evidence, management capacity, partner redundancy, and recurring revenue.
- Larger customers reward program governance, portfolio reporting, and long-term provider coordination.
- Physical and institutional launches create substantial coordination value and defensible relationships.
- A mature Japan operating model becomes the basis for country expansion.

### Why not earlier

- Physical sites, licenses, construction, labor, procurement, security, and governance materially increase risk.
- Enterprise customers expect references, controls, service levels, and continuity beyond a founder.
- Portfolio programs can amplify poor processes as easily as good ones.
- Regulated verticals require specialized legal and operational expertise that cannot be generalized.

### Required capabilities

- Multi-stakeholder program governance.
- Service-level, escalation, and quality-control processes.
- Procurement, confidentiality, security, and vendor-governance readiness.
- Physical-site dependency and milestone management.
- Segment acceptance policies and reviewed playbooks.
- Dedicated capability owners and partner panels for each promoted vertical.
- Portfolio and multi-entity reporting.
- Business-continuity and coordinator handoff processes.

### Likely partners

- Larger law, tax, accounting, labor, and corporate-secretarial firms.
- Commercial real-estate and licensed brokerage partners.
- Construction, permitting, environmental, and safety specialists.
- Sector regulators and trade specialists through qualified advisers.
- Banks, insurers, payroll, recruitment, and relocation providers.
- Chambers, trade agencies, economic-development organizations, investors, and accelerators.

### Expected revenue profile

- Manufacturing or franchise launch: USD 15,000-40,000+.
- Enterprise subsidiary: USD 25,000-75,000+.
- Portfolio/program engagements: USD 25,000-100,000+ plus per-launch fees.
- Recurring governance and post-launch coordination become a meaningful revenue share.
- Sales cycles lengthen and cost of sale rises, but LTV and referral leverage increase.

### Operational risks

- Enterprise concentration and slow receivables.
- Contractual liability exceeding coordination control.
- Partner capacity failure across several simultaneous launches.
- Vertical-specific mistakes hidden by generic workflow labels.
- Brand dilution into broad "market-entry consulting" or unlicensed professional services.
- Premature global expansion while Japan delivery still depends on the founder.

### Exit gate to global expansion

Begin a second-country pilot only when:

- Japan has at least 50 completed paid launches.
- At least half of new business comes from repeatable channels or referrals.
- A non-founder operating team reliably delivers the core workflow.
- Customer satisfaction and contribution margin remain on target across at least three entry paths.
- Japan partner governance survives coordinator turnover and capacity changes.
- At least three existing customers or channel partners have a real need in the same next country.
- The next country has a named local lead and redundant professional partners before marketing begins.

## What remains constant across all waves

- Hajime sells coordination, not approval or advice.
- The customer sees current step, next step, responsible party, and timeframe.
- Professional fees and responsibilities remain explicit.
- New segments use the shared readiness and formation core.
- Entry pages qualify; they do not create separate businesses.
- Customer payment and delivery evidence, not calendar ambition, unlock expansion.
