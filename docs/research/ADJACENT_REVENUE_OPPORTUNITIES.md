# Hajime Japan Adjacent Revenue Opportunities

## Principle

Hajime is paid first for independent coordination. Adjacent revenue is acceptable only when a recommendation solves a real customer problem, the customer can decline it, alternatives remain visible, and any economic relationship is disclosed.

Hajime must never resemble an advertising marketplace. No provider should buy ranking, "preferred" status, urgency, or access to client data. Japanese counsel should review referral economics, disclosures, and professional-rule implications before launch.

## Priority definitions

- **Core:** Directly supports a reliable Japan launch and may be offered early.
- **Secondary:** Useful after the core concierge service and partner governance are proven.
- **Future:** Potentially valuable but operationally distracting, conflict-prone, or too remote from launch.

## Recommended revenue sequence

### Stage 1: Customer-paid only

For the first 10 customers, earn revenue from:

- Paid launch readiness.
- Guided Launch or Concierge coordination.
- Clearly scoped post-launch coordination.
- Transparent project fees for translation or office-search coordination where Hajime does real work.

Avoid third-party commissions. This reveals whether customers value Hajime itself and removes attribution ambiguity.

### Stage 2: Selective disclosed economics

After ten customers and partner review, test no more than two adjacent models:

1. A disclosed office/coworking referral that offers the customer a real benefit.
2. A fixed accounting-software onboarding coordination fee paid by the customer.

Do not optimize commission revenue. Measure customer suitability, complaint rate, alternatives shown, and opt-out behavior.

### Stage 3: Durable post-launch revenue

The most strategically aligned adjacent revenue is a post-launch coordination retainer. It extends the core promise without turning Hajime into a broker. The retainer may cover obligation calendars, provider coordination, annual readiness, changes of address or officers, and escalation routing, while regulated work remains separately owned.

## Recommendation policy

Every recommended provider should have a written record containing:

- Customer need and selection criteria.
- At least one reasonable alternative where available.
- Why the recommended option fits.
- Known exclusions and material limitations.
- All fees payable by the customer.
- Any compensation, benefit, ownership, or reciprocal relationship affecting Hajime.
- The customer's explicit choice.

Do not label a provider "best." Use factual fit language such as "matches the stated need for English payroll support and a five-employee minimum."

## Conflict guardrails

- Provider ranking cannot depend on payment.
- Coordinators cannot receive personal incentives from partners.
- A commission cannot increase the customer's price without disclosure.
- Hajime must not share client data before informed consent.
- Regulated recommendations must be made by appropriately qualified parties.
- Clients may use their own provider without losing normal Hajime service.
- Material complaints suspend referrals pending review.
- Referral income should be reported separately from core service revenue.

## Opportunity gates

Approve an adjacent opportunity only when all are true:

1. At least three customers independently report the underlying problem.
2. The offer saves measurable time, cost, or risk.
3. Hajime can explain the recommendation without marketing language.
4. A customer can decline without penalty or degraded core service.
5. Conflicts can be disclosed in one clear sentence.
6. Delivery does not require Hajime to cross a professional boundary.
7. The opportunity does not consume capacity needed for the first 20 core customers.

## Explicit depriorities

- Airlines, hotels, and travel affiliate catalogs.
- Generic "member benefits" marketplaces.
- Sponsored provider placement.
- Lead resale.
- White-labeled legal, immigration, tax, insurance, banking, or real-estate advice.
- Commission structures tied to bank, visa, license, or government approval.

These may produce revenue but weaken the trusted-coordinator identity. Trust compounds more reliably than affiliate inventory.
