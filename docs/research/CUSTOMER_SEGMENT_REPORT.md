# Hajime Japan Customer Segment Report

## Beachhead customer

The first customer archetype is a **commercially committed international founder**, not a Japan-curious entrepreneur.

The company is typically an English-speaking B2B software or professional-services business with 2-50 employees, existing revenue or institutional funding, and one of five observable triggers:

1. A Japanese customer contract is signed or in late-stage negotiation.
2. A Japanese distributor or strategic partner is committed.
3. A first Japan-based hire has been selected.
4. A founder or executive has a dated relocation plan.
5. The board or owners approved a Japan launch date and budget.

The customer lacks a dedicated Japan operations leader. The founder can hire individual specialists but does not want to discover and manage the full sequence personally.

## Profile 1: Revenue-backed founder with a Japan trigger

### Goals

- Become operational in Japan by a commercially meaningful date.
- Protect the Japanese customer or partner relationship.
- Avoid preventable rework across entity, office, tax, banking, and hiring decisions.
- Keep executive time focused on customers and product.
- Receive one accurate answer to "what happens next?" even when several providers are involved.

### Decision process

The founder or COO normally owns the decision and budget. A co-founder, finance lead, existing accountant, or investor may influence provider selection. The buyer first decides whether Japan is real, then compares self-management, a professional firm, and a coordinated service. They buy when the cost of delay and executive distraction exceeds Hajime's fee.

The likely buying sequence is:

1. Trigger event creates a deadline.
2. Founder searches, asks investors, or contacts an existing advisor.
3. Founder receives fragmented or provider-specific answers.
4. Hajime maps the whole launch, responsibilities, dependencies, and realistic ranges.
5. Founder buys a paid readiness engagement, Guided Launch, or Concierge.

### Objections

- "Why not hire a lawyer or accountant directly?"
- "JETRO and Tokyo offer free support."
- "Can my team manage this with a checklist?"
- "Are you legally qualified to advise us?"
- "Why pay a coordination fee in addition to professional fees?"
- "Can you guarantee the bank account or launch date?"

The answer is not that alternatives are inadequate. Hajime should explain the distinct job: licensed professionals own their advice and filings; Hajime owns preparation, sequence, handoffs, consolidated requests, progress visibility, and escalation. Government support is a useful input, not a dedicated project owner. Tokyo explicitly offers one-stop consultation and procedures, so Hajime must never imply that public guidance is unavailable ([Invest Tokyo](https://www.startup-support.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/)).

### Trigger events

- Signed Japanese customer or procurement request for a local entity.
- Distributor requiring a Japanese contracting counterparty.
- Candidate offer or transfer date.
- Funding round containing a Japan milestone.
- Founder relocation, lease, or school decision.
- Existing provider warns that another specialist or prerequisite is needed.

### Buying signals

- Uses a specific date rather than "someday."
- Names a customer, partner, hire, or board commitment.
- Asks who owns each task and what can happen in parallel.
- Has already spoken with two or more providers and received inconsistent scopes.
- Shares an approved launch budget.
- Values executive time and asks Hajime to attend or coordinate provider meetings.
- Accepts that professional and government fees are separate.

### Typical timeline

- Discovery to paid readiness: 3-10 business days.
- Readiness to full engagement: 1-2 weeks.
- Straightforward Guided Launch: commonly 6-14 weeks under Hajime's package assumptions, with external decisions capable of extending it.

JETRO's cost model shows that incorporation sits inside a broader sequence involving office, professional filings, and operating expenses; that broader dependency set is the economic basis for coordination ([JETRO cost model](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/setting_up/modelcase.html)).

### Preferred communication channels

- Warm email introduction from an accountant, attorney, investor, or founder.
- Concise LinkedIn message tied to the visible trigger.
- Video discovery call with a written one-page follow-up.
- Shared project summary and scheduled milestone updates after purchase.
- Email for decisions and records; messaging only for urgent coordination.

## Profile 2: VC-backed startup entering Japan

### Goals

- Demonstrate credible market-entry execution to the board and investors.
- Reach a customer, partnership, or hiring milestone without building an internal operations function.
- Preserve runway by avoiding premature enterprise infrastructure.
- Establish a repeatable reporting rhythm for launch risks and progress.

### Decision process

The CEO or COO sponsors the decision. Finance validates cost, counsel reviews boundaries, and an investor or accelerator may nominate providers. A trusted portfolio referral can compress a six-week evaluation into days.

### Objections

- "Our investor already has a law firm."
- "Can an EOR postpone entity formation?"
- "Will this scale after launch?"
- "Do you have references from companies at our stage?"
- "Can you work with our existing counsel and accountant?"

Hajime should be provider-compatible. It should coordinate the buyer's existing specialists instead of forcing replacement.

### Trigger events

- Seed or Series A/B financing.
- Acceptance into a Japan accelerator or market-entry program.
- Enterprise pilot requiring local procurement.
- First Japan general manager or salesperson.
- Investor or strategic partner sets a launch milestone.

### Buying signals

- Board deck includes Japan milestones.
- Founder requests a budget range before selecting entity details.
- Company has customer evidence but no operations hire.
- Investor or accelerator makes the introduction.
- Buyer asks for weekly risk and dependency reporting.

### Typical timeline

- Sales cycle: 2-6 weeks.
- Engagement: 6-16 weeks for a straightforward launch; longer with hiring, licensing, or immigration dependencies.

### Preferred communication channels

- Investor and accelerator introductions.
- Founder communities and private portfolio channels.
- LinkedIn and email with evidence of a real Japan trigger.
- Partner-led workshops or office hours, not broad webinars.

Accelerators are attractive as referral concentrations rather than as mass advertising. Alchemist Japan, for example, focuses on enterprise technology startups and links Japanese and Silicon Valley networks ([Alchemist Japan](https://www.alchemistaccelerator.com/japan)).

## Profile 3: Profitable owner-led SMB

### Goals

- Create a compliant, controlled subsidiary or branch.
- Keep headquarters reporting and local operations aligned.
- Avoid placing the entire launch burden on one finance or sales executive.
- Select durable accounting, payroll, office, and governance arrangements.

### Decision process

The owner, managing director, or CFO approves. Existing counsel and accountants carry significant influence. Procurement is light compared with an enterprise, but the buyer expects formal proposals, references, confidentiality, and clear responsibility boundaries.

### Objections

- "Why not use our global accounting firm?"
- "Can you meet our headquarters reporting requirements?"
- "Who is liable for professional errors?"
- "Will you remain involved after incorporation?"
- "Can you coordinate in our home-country time zone?"

### Trigger events

- Board approval or annual expansion plan.
- Japanese distributor relationship reaches scale.
- Need to invoice locally or hire a team.
- Acquisition, joint venture, or customer service requirement.
- Existing Japan revenue makes the current structure inefficient.

### Buying signals

- Has a named executive sponsor and budget.
- Requests entity, branch, or operating-model comparisons from qualified advisors.
- Needs headquarters documents and several providers coordinated.
- Asks for post-launch compliance handoff.

### Typical timeline

- Sales cycle: 4-12 weeks.
- Launch: 2-6 months depending on governance, hiring, office, and bank dependencies.

### Preferred communication channels

- International accountant, attorney, chamber, trade agency, or banker introduction.
- Formal email and video meeting.
- Written proposal with scope boundaries, assumptions, and third-party costs.

Global providers already serve this segment broadly. Vistra offers formation, registered address, resident-director, accounting, payroll, and banking support; TMF emphasizes bundled corporate secretarial, payroll, and accounting services ([Vistra Japan](https://www.vistra.com/locations/asia-pacific/japan), [TMF Japan](https://www.tmf-group.com/en/locations/asia-pacific/japan/)). Hajime should pursue the owner-led portion where responsiveness and a named coordinator matter more than a global master-service agreement.

## Jobs to be done

Prospects are not primarily buying "a Japanese company." They are hiring Hajime to:

- Tell them whether they are ready and what decisions are irreversible or time-sensitive.
- Convert separate professional scopes into one executable plan.
- Reduce repeated document requests and translation of context.
- Identify the next action, owner, dependency, and timeframe.
- Surface delays before a customer, hire, or board commitment is missed.
- Prepare productive conversations with licensed specialists and institutions.

## Disqualification rules

Do not advance a prospect to proposal when:

- There is no concrete Japan trigger in the next 12 months.
- The buyer seeks a guaranteed visa, bank account, tax result, license, or government date.
- The primary need is legal, immigration, tax, accounting, real-estate, or financial advice rather than coordination.
- The buyer cannot fund both third-party costs and Hajime's fee.
- The business model or source of funds cannot be explained clearly.
- The buyer expects Hajime to act as nominee, conceal beneficial ownership, or bypass institutional checks.

## Message to test

> Launch your Japan business without becoming its project manager. Hajime gives your company one accountable coordinator across the plan, providers, documents, dependencies, and next actions. Licensed specialists remain responsible for their professional work.

Test this against two alternatives:

- "A clear Japan launch plan in ten business days."
- "One launch owner for your first Japan customer, hire, or office."

The winning message is the one that produces paid readiness calls from qualified, triggered buyers, not the one with the highest click-through rate.
