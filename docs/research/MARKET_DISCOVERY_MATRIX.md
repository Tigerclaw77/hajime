# Hajime Japan Market Discovery Matrix

## Decision

Hajime should first pursue **English-speaking, founder-led B2B technology and professional-services companies with 2-50 employees, existing revenue or institutional funding, a concrete Japan trigger, and no internal Japan operations lead**.

A concrete trigger means a signed or late-stage Japanese customer, a committed channel partner, a first local hire, a founder relocation, or a board-approved market-entry date. This segment has enough urgency and budget to buy coordination, remains reachable through founder and professional networks, and needs more than low-cost incorporation without bringing enterprise procurement overhead.

The initial offer should be a paid launch-readiness engagement that can convert into Guided Launch or Concierge. Hajime should not lead with incorporation alone. Public alternatives already range from free government guidance and do-it-yourself software to fixed-fee filing specialists and global corporate-service firms. Hajime wins by owning the plan, handoffs, dependencies, and communication across providers.

## Method

This is a hypothesis-ranking model, not measured demand. Scores must be replaced with interview, proposal, and payment evidence. Each factor is scored from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).

| Factor | Weight | What a 5 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Willingness to pay | 20% | Can comfortably buy a premium coordination engagement in addition to third-party fees |
| Trigger urgency | 15% | A dated commercial or operational event makes delay expensive |
| Reachability | 15% | Founders can be identified and reached directly or through concentrated networks |
| Coordination pain | 15% | Several interdependent providers and decisions create meaningful founder burden |
| Sales-cycle speed | 10% | An owner can approve within days or a few weeks |
| Referral density | 10% | The segment repeatedly appears in trusted partner or portfolio networks |
| Strategic fit | 15% | Hajime can create value while staying inside its coordinator boundaries |

**Weighted score = sum of (rating / 5 x weight).** Scores are directional estimates as of 2026-07-12.

The Visa-dependent segment's numeric score overstates its practical priority because urgency and reachability are high. It should not be an initial focus: immigration questions create regulated-service dependence, policy sensitivity, and a crowded field of licensed specialists. Hajime may serve these customers only with clear professional ownership and after the core model is validated.

## Segment comparison

### 1. Revenue-backed B2B technology or professional-services founder

- **Typical customer profile:** Founder or COO at a 2-50 person SaaS, software, consulting, design, research, or specialist-services company. English-speaking; already selling internationally.
- **Primary motivation:** Fulfill a Japan customer, partner, hiring, or relocation commitment without personally assembling the local operating stack.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Unclear sequence; conflicting provider instructions; English/Japanese handoffs; no owner across incorporation, office, bank readiness, tax setup, and optional immigration.
- **Urgency:** High when a contract, hire, or board date exists.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 4,000-12,000 for Hajime coordination, excluding government and professional fees.
- **Ability to reach:** High through founder LinkedIn, investors, accelerators, international accountants, attorneys, and Japan-focused communities.
- **Existing alternatives:** Self-management; local filing professionals; JETRO/Tokyo support; bundled corporate-service firms.
- **Why Hajime would win:** Founder-level accountability and operational translation without the overhead of a global provider.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 1-4 weeks when the trigger and budget owner are present.
- **Referral potential:** Very high; advisors and investors see the same pattern repeatedly.

### 2. VC-backed startup entering after validation

- **Typical customer profile:** Seed to Series B company with Japan customer interest, investor pressure, an accelerator placement, or a strategic partnership.
- **Primary motivation:** Preserve speed and executive attention while creating a credible local presence.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Board expectations, uncertain costs, limited internal bandwidth, and multiple stakeholders.
- **Urgency:** Medium-high; tied to milestones and runway.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 5,000-15,000 for coordination.
- **Ability to reach:** High through portfolio managers, accelerator operators, venture studios, and founders.
- **Existing alternatives:** Investor introductions, law/accounting firms, EOR providers, global entity-management firms.
- **Why Hajime would win:** A founder-friendly launch owner who can coordinate specialists without selling a large ongoing outsourcing contract.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 2-6 weeks.
- **Referral potential:** Exceptional if one investor or accelerator relationship works.

### 3. Profitable foreign SMB opening a subsidiary or branch

- **Typical customer profile:** Owner-led or family-owned 20-250 person company expanding sales, distribution, or service delivery.
- **Primary motivation:** Establish a controlled, compliant Japan presence with predictable reporting.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Headquarters documentation, entity choice, local governance, accounting, payroll, bank readiness, and vendor coordination.
- **Urgency:** High after board approval; moderate before it.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 10,000-25,000+ for coordination and launch management.
- **Ability to reach:** Moderate; chambers, trade bodies, international accountants, and attorneys are more effective than cold founder outreach.
- **Existing alternatives:** TMF Group, Vistra, major accounting firms, market-entry consultancies, and local professional networks.
- **Why Hajime would win:** More personal and focused than a multinational provider for an owner-led buyer.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 1-3 months.
- **Referral potential:** High, but relationships take longer to establish.

### 4. Foreign resident launching a services company

- **Typical customer profile:** Experienced professional already in Japan with stable residence status and initial clients.
- **Primary motivation:** Formalize a business while avoiding Japanese-language administrative burden.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Provider selection, filings, tax setup, banking, and separating business from personal administration.
- **Urgency:** High when client contracts or employment transitions are dated.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 1,500-5,000 for coordination.
- **Ability to reach:** Very high through local communities, coworking spaces, and referrals.
- **Existing alternatives:** Freee, TOSBEC, a judicial scrivener, or an accountant. Community discussions explicitly compare software and legal-office routes ([JapanFinance discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/JapanFinance/comments/1mjy8mo/register_kk_via_freee_or_regular_legal_office/)).
- **Why Hajime would win:** Plain-English sequencing and introductions for people willing to pay for time savings.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 1-3 weeks.
- **Referral potential:** High within local expatriate networks, but revenue per engagement is lower.

### 5. Visa-dependent entrepreneur

- **Typical customer profile:** Founder outside Japan or on another status who needs a startup or Business Manager visa path.
- **Primary motivation:** Establish the business and lawful residence together.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Capital, office, business-plan, incorporation, and immigration dependencies.
- **Urgency:** Very high.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 3,000-10,000 across coordination and licensed work.
- **Ability to reach:** High via search and relocation communities.
- **Existing alternatives:** Licensed administrative scriveners with integrated formation support. Public examples include Mayuha's English formation/visa offer and Royal Office's Business Manager support from JPY 330,000 ([Mayuha](https://www.mayuha.net/), [Royal Office](https://www.royaloffice.jp/en/visa-application)).
- **Why Hajime would win:** Broader operating coordination after licensed advice, not immigration expertise itself.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 2-8 weeks.
- **Referral potential:** Moderate.

### 6. Overseas e-commerce or consumer brand

- **Typical customer profile:** Revenue-generating brand seeking localized sales, import, warehouse, or marketplace operations.
- **Primary motivation:** Capture Japan demand and improve local commercial credibility.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Entity setup plus licensing, tax, logistics, labeling, payments, and distribution.
- **Urgency:** Medium-high around launch seasons.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 8,000-25,000.
- **Ability to reach:** Moderate through agencies, trade shows, logistics firms, and marketplaces.
- **Existing alternatives:** Specialized market-entry agencies, distributors, importers of record, and corporate-service firms.
- **Why Hajime would win:** Neutral coordination, but only after a strong commerce partner bench exists.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 1-3 months.
- **Referral potential:** Moderate.

### 7. Remote-first company considering a Japan entity

- **Typical customer profile:** International technology company with one or more Japan-based employees or candidates.
- **Primary motivation:** Move beyond contractors or EOR while supporting local growth.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Entity-versus-EOR decision, payroll, labor, tax, benefits, and ongoing compliance.
- **Urgency:** High when a hire or EOR cost threshold is reached.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 7,500-20,000.
- **Ability to reach:** Moderate through global mobility, HR, and EOR ecosystems.
- **Existing alternatives:** EOR platforms and global entity-management providers such as TMF and Vistra ([TMF Japan](https://www.tmf-group.com/en/locations/asia-pacific/japan/), [Vistra Japan](https://www.vistra.com/locations/asia-pacific/japan)).
- **Why Hajime would win:** High-touch local coordination, but the customer may expect capabilities outside Hajime's initial scope.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** 1-3 months.
- **Referral potential:** High through employment advisors, though channel conflict is possible.

### 8. Aspiration-led solo founder

- **Typical customer profile:** Individual attracted to living or doing business in Japan but without customers, capital, or a dated plan.
- **Primary motivation:** Explore possibility and reduce uncertainty.
- **Biggest frustrations:** Information overload and unclear eligibility.
- **Urgency:** Low.
- **Estimated budget hypothesis:** USD 250-1,500 for education or planning.
- **Ability to reach:** Very high through content and communities.
- **Existing alternatives:** Free public guidance, online content, software, and peer forums. Tokyo and JETRO both operate one-stop support channels ([Invest Tokyo](https://www.startup-support.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/), [JETRO Invest Japan Offices](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/support_programs/incentive/offices/)).
- **Why Hajime would win:** A paid readiness product can qualify serious buyers, but this audience should not dominate acquisition.
- **Estimated sales cycle:** Fast for a low-price product; poor conversion to Concierge.
- **Referral potential:** Low.

## Market evidence and implications

- JETRO's model case estimates establishment applications and filings at roughly USD 6,200 for a subsidiary and USD 4,500 for a branch, before broader launch costs. This supports separating Hajime's coordination fee from professional and government costs ([JETRO cost model](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/setting_up/modelcase.html)).
- Low-cost local specialists demonstrate that filings alone are commoditized. Public examples include Interr at JPY 250,000 for a GK and JPY 400,000 for a KK, and Japan International Immigration Law Office listing JPY 77,000 as its company-establishment service fee before other costs ([Interr](https://interr.app/), [Japan International Immigration Law Office](https://japan-internationalvisa.com/eng/business-manager-visa-price-information/)).
- At the high end, Healy Consultants lists average Japan engagement fees of USD 20,325 for a tax-resident GK and USD 14,500 for a joint-stock company, including wider setup components ([Healy Consultants](https://www.healyconsultants.com/japan-company-registration/fees-timelines/)).
- Free public support is substantial. Hajime cannot charge merely for access to general information; it must charge for preparation, orchestration, judgment about sequence, and reduced founder workload ([Invest Tokyo](https://www.startup-support.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/), [JETRO](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/support_programs/incentive/offices/)).

## Validation thresholds

Keep the beachhead only if, within 30 qualified conversations:

- At least 12 prospects report a dated Japan trigger and three or more interdependent launch needs.
- At least 8 request a proposal or paid readiness engagement.
- At least 3 pay a deposit or readiness fee without a software product being required.
- At least 2 of the first 5 customers originate from a repeatable partner source.
- Median expected sales cycle is 30 days or less for founder-led companies.

Re-segment if fewer than 20% of qualified prospects will pay at least USD 4,000 for coordination, or if most demand is actually for regulated advice Hajime cannot provide.
