# Hajime Japan Partner Acquisition Plan

## Partner thesis

The strongest recurring-referral network is a small group of **bilingual accountants, judicial scriveners, administrative scriveners, and international business attorneys** who serve foreign companies but do not want to own every non-professional dependency.

These partners encounter buyers at the moment of intent, hold high trust, and benefit when clients arrive prepared. Hajime should make specialists more effective, not insert itself between them and their professional judgment.

Start with 8-12 excellent relationships in Tokyo and one secondary hub rather than a large directory. Referral economics are optional and should not be introduced until client value, professional rules, disclosure, and Japanese counsel review are established.

## Partner value proposition

### For licensed professionals

- Clients arrive with a clear business description, ownership facts, timeline, and known open questions.
- Hajime separates professional questions from operational tasks.
- Requests are consolidated and chased by one coordinator.
- The professional's advice is attributed accurately and not paraphrased as Hajime advice.
- Hajime does not promise outcomes, select a professional based on commission, or interfere with professional independence.

### For ecosystem partners

- Their founders receive practical triage without the partner building an internal Japan desk.
- Unready prospects are redirected to public resources or a paid Blueprint rather than consuming partner time.
- The partner receives anonymized trend feedback with client consent and appropriate confidentiality.

## Ideal partner profile

Select for:

- Appropriate Japanese licensing and good standing where required.
- Substantial experience with foreign-owned or internationally managed companies.
- Clear English communication and written scope boundaries.
- Transparent fees, exclusions, turnaround expectations, and capacity.
- Willingness to contract directly with clients for regulated services.
- Respect for consent, privacy, and conflict disclosure.
- No guarantee language around visas, banking, licensing, registration, or tax outcomes.
- A collaborative response to errors, delays, and scope changes.

Reject partners who demand exclusivity before evidence, conceal fees, pay for biased placement, blur professional responsibility, or pressure Hajime to make approval claims.

## Acquisition sequence

### Phase 1: Ten partner interviews

Ask about their last five foreign-founder matters:

- Where did prospects arrive unprepared?
- Which adjacent tasks consumed unpaid time?
- Which client profiles became good long-term customers?
- Which referrals created poor outcomes?
- What would a coordinator need to do to earn trust?
- Which information may be shared, with whom, and under what consent?

Do not lead with referral commissions.

### Phase 2: Sixty-day pilot

Select one accountant, one judicial scrivener, one immigration professional, and one attorney. Agree in writing on:

- Qualification criteria.
- Scope ownership.
- Introduction and consent language.
- Response-time expectations.
- Document and status handling.
- Conflict escalation.
- Feedback after each matter.
- No exclusivity and no undisclosed economics.

### Phase 3: Repeatability

A partner becomes preferred only after:

- Two completed or materially progressed matters.
- Accurate scopes and invoices.
- Reliable communication.
- Positive client feedback.
- No material boundary or conduct concern.

Maintain at least two viable providers for each core capability to preserve choice and capacity.

## Referral incentives

Use incentives in this order:

1. **Better clients:** preparation, qualification, and realistic expectations.
2. **Lower servicing burden:** consolidated requests and one operational contact.
3. **Reciprocal referrals:** only when each party is genuinely best suited.
4. **Co-marketing:** practical sessions and educational materials with clear authorship.
5. **Financial referral compensation:** only if lawful, permitted by professional rules, reviewed by Japanese counsel, disclosed to the client, and irrelevant to ranking.

The safest initial policy is no paid referral fees. Revenue should come from the client for Hajime's coordination. This protects the recommendation's credibility while the partner model is unproven.

## Specific ecosystem targets

- **JETRO and Invest Tokyo:** Treat as authoritative public resources and potential ecosystem relationships, not competitors to disparage. JETRO operates investment support centers and Invest Japan offices; Tokyo provides one-stop business establishment support ([JETRO](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/support_programs/incentive/offices/), [Invest Tokyo](https://www.startup-support.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/)).
- **Foreign chambers:** Begin with member interviews and committee contribution. ACCJ and BCCJ aggregate internationally active companies; BCCJ explicitly aims to strengthen UK-Japan business links ([ACCJ](https://www.accj.or.jp/about-j), [BCCJ](https://bccjapan.com/japanese)).
- **B2B accelerators:** Offer private readiness clinics to programs with companies entering Japan, not generic startup audiences. Alchemist Japan is one current enterprise-technology example ([Alchemist Japan](https://www.alchemistaccelerator.com/japan)).
- **Corporate-service firms:** Some may be competitors for a whole engagement but partners when a founder needs independent preparation or when Hajime hands off ongoing compliance.

## Partner metrics

Track monthly:

- Qualified referrals per active partner.
- Referral-to-discovery and referral-to-paid conversion.
- Time from introduction to first response.
- Client preparedness and repeated-request count.
- Matters delayed by capacity or handoff failure.
- Client satisfaction by provider.
- Revenue concentration by partner.
- Conflicts, complaints, and boundary escalations.
- Second-referral rate.

The key proof is **a second qualified referral from the same partner**. A large partner list without repeats is not a network.

## First 90-day target

- Interview 10 professionals and 5 ecosystem operators.
- Sign four non-exclusive pilot protocols.
- Receive six qualified referrals.
- Convert at least two to paid readiness or launch engagements.
- Earn at least two repeat referrals.
- Remove any partner relationship that weakens transparency or customer choice.
