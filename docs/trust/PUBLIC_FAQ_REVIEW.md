# Public FAQ Review

**Status:** Reviewed replacement copy; publish after Japanese counsel and operations sign-off  
**Source reviewed:** Current eight-question website FAQ, July 12, 2026

## Review finding

The current FAQ explains the basic service and professional boundary well. It does not yet answer several questions a cautious buyer is likely to ask before entrusting Hajime with a high-stakes project:

- How partners are selected and whether recommendations are paid
- What happens when a partner fails or timing changes
- What Hajime is accountable for
- How sensitive information is handled
- What happens when the customer is dissatisfied
- Whether Hajime has customers, case studies, or a mature operating history
- Whether the displayed prices include external fees and what happens if scope changes

The replacement FAQ below keeps the current plain language and adds those missing trust questions.

## Approved public FAQ copy

### What does Hajime do?

Hajime coordinates the people, information, decisions, and next steps involved in forming and launching a business in Japan. You receive one integrated plan, one named coordinator, and clear ownership across each workstream.

### What is Hajime accountable for?

We are accountable for the coordination work we control: a clear plan, accurate responsibility mapping, prepared handoffs, consolidated requests, evidence-based status, timely communication, and escalation when work stalls or assumptions change.

### Is Hajime a law firm, accounting firm, or immigration adviser?

No. Hajime does not provide legal, tax, accounting, immigration, insurance, banking, or other regulated professional advice. Appropriately licensed professionals perform regulated work and remain responsible for their advice, filings, and deliverables.

### Who performs company registration and legal work?

The appropriate Japanese licensed professional performs regulated filings and legal work. Depending on the matter, this may include an attorney, judicial scrivener, administrative scrivener, certified public tax accountant, or another qualified provider. You will be told who owns each professional decision and whether a separate engagement is required.

### How do you choose partners?

Before assignment, Hajime checks the capability required, identity or registration where applicable, relevant experience, current capacity, communication fit, fees, conflicts, and data-handling expectations. No provider can buy ranking or access to a customer. Any material referral payment or commercial relationship is disclosed before the customer decides.

### Can I choose or replace a partner?

Yes. A recommendation is not a forced assignment. If fit, independence, capacity, communication, or quality becomes a material concern, Hajime will explain the options and coordinate a replacement where reasonably available. A change may affect cost or timing, which will be stated before action.

### How long does formation take?

A straightforward engagement often spans 8-16 weeks from planning through launch readiness, but this is a planning range rather than a promise. Immigration, banking, office requirements, overseas documents, professional capacity, and government review can extend the schedule. Updates identify the current assumption, responsible party, and next review point.

### Do you guarantee company registration, a visa, a bank account, or a completion date?

No. Those decisions belong to licensed professionals, banks, government agencies, and other independent bodies. Hajime can improve preparation, ownership, visibility, and escalation, but cannot guarantee an external decision or timing.

### What documents will I need?

Requirements vary by entity, owners, directors, residence status, banking route, and business activity. Common items include identity and address evidence, corporate records, signatures, source-of-funds context, and business-plan materials. The responsible professional confirms the applicable list.

### How do you protect sensitive information?

Hajime aims to collect only what is necessary for a stated purpose, restrict access to people who need it, and share information with partners only for an approved task. Do not send identity, financial, or formation documents through the public consultation form. Your project instructions will identify the approved transfer method and retention approach.

### Are government and professional fees included?

No, unless a written proposal explicitly says otherwise. Package prices cover Hajime's coordination scope. Government charges, licensed professional fees, translations, office services, banking costs, insurance, payroll, and other third-party expenses are shown separately where known before commitment.

### What happens if scope or costs change?

Hajime will explain the changed fact, the impact on scope, price, and timing, and the available options before performing material additional work. Urgent protective action is handled separately and documented.

### What happens if a project is delayed?

You receive the verified status, the reason for the delay, who owns the next action, the revised range or review date, and the available intervention. ???Waiting??? is not treated as permission to go silent.

### What if I am dissatisfied?

Raise the concern with your coordinator or mark a message ???Complaint.??? Hajime acknowledges complaints within one business day, assigns an owner, preserves the relevant record, and provides the next review date. Serious or unresolved matters move to an operations or independent professional review path.

### What is your refund approach?

The written proposal controls the actual payment and cancellation terms. Hajime's philosophy is to charge for work performed and authorized non-recoverable costs, not undelivered coordination. When Hajime materially fails in work it controls, we prioritize correction and consider a proportionate credit or refund. An external rejection or delay alone is not a Hajime service failure.

### Is Hajime an established provider?

Hajime is in early commercial validation. We publish only case studies, testimonials, partner claims, and performance figures that are real, permissioned, and supported by records. We will not invent scale to appear safer than we are.
