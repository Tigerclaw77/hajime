# Refund Philosophy

**Status:** Public philosophy only; not payment terms or a contract  
**Required review:** Japanese counsel and commercial owner must convert this philosophy into engagement-specific terms before payment is accepted

## Public statement

Hajime believes a customer should pay for coordination that was performed, value that was delivered, and authorized costs that cannot reasonably be recovered. A customer should not be charged as though undelivered work were complete.

The written proposal or engagement terms state the actual deposit, milestone, cancellation, and refund rules for each service. Those terms control.

## How Hajime thinks about common situations

### Before work begins

If the customer cancels before Hajime begins the agreed work, the default philosophy is to return Hajime fees not yet earned, less any clearly disclosed, authorized, and non-recoverable costs. Any non-refundable readiness fee or reservation deposit must be conspicuous before payment and connected to a real commitment or work allocation.

### After work begins

If the engagement stops after delivery has begun, Hajime should provide an itemized closeout showing:

- Work completed
- Deliverables transferred
- Work not performed
- Authorized third-party costs incurred
- Non-recoverable commitments
- Open professional or external matters
- Proposed refund, credit, or remaining balance

### When Hajime materially fails

If Hajime misses a commitment it controls or causes material coordination harm, the first response is correction where correction still protects the customer. A proportionate fee credit or refund should be considered when the missed value cannot be restored. Serious failures move to independent operations or professional review.

### When an external party delays or rejects

A government, bank, insurer, landlord, or professional decision is not controlled by Hajime. An external delay or rejection alone does not mean Hajime failed. Hajime's performance is assessed on preparation, accuracy, communication, ownership, and escalation around that event.

### When the customer changes direction

A changed entity, owner, city, timeline, capital plan, business activity, provider, or immigration assumption may invalidate work already performed. Hajime should explain what remains reusable, what must be redone, and the commercial options before continuing.

### Third-party fees

Government and independent-provider fees follow their own terms. Hajime should never imply it can refund money it does not control. Where Hajime collects or passes through third-party amounts, the written proposal must explain custody, authorization, reconciliation, and refund treatment.

## Principles for deciding a remedy

- Fairness over defensiveness
- Evidence over blame
- Separate Hajime performance from external outcomes
- No double recovery or hidden deductions
- No waiver of a valid complaint in exchange for a refund
- No confidentiality demand merely to suppress truthful criticism
- Written rationale and a review owner for every material decision

## Publication gates

- [ ] Counsel confirms applicable cancellation and consumer rules for the operating model and buyer type
- [ ] Each package has a defined work-start event and milestone evidence
- [ ] Proposal clearly separates Hajime fees, professional fees, government fees, and pass-through costs
- [ ] Refund authority and payment method are operationally available
- [ ] Complaint and appeal route is staffed

## Evidence basis

Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency explains that transaction rules can require important disclosures, prohibit false or exaggerated representations, and provide cancellation or damages protections for covered transaction types. Applicability varies by transaction and customer. See the [CAA overview](https://www.caa.go.jp/about_us/about/caa_pamphlet/jp_2026_011.html). Counsel must determine the rules applicable to Hajime; this philosophy does not attempt to do so.
