# Review Acquisition Process

**Status:** Immediately usable operating process  
**Goal:** Earn honest public reviews without manipulating sentiment, timing, or visibility

## Eligibility

A review request may be sent when:

- A real customer has paid for and received a meaningful deliverable.
- The customer has had enough experience to comment fairly.
- No material complaint, refund, or escalation is unresolved.
- The request is not tied to a discount, gift, referral payment, or service advantage.

## Process

### 1. Record the milestone

Note the delivered milestone, date, customer owner, satisfaction signal, open issues, and whether a review request is appropriate. Do not request a public review solely because the customer gave a high score.

### 2. Ask for private feedback first, without gating

> Now that [milestone] is complete, I would value your candid feedback. What worked, what created friction, and what should we improve?

This is a service-quality question, not a screening mechanism. Customers with critical feedback retain the same ability to post publicly.

### 3. Resolve open service issues

Address the issue because the service requires it, not in exchange for silence or a changed review. Do not make a refund conditional on removal, nondisclosure, or positive wording.

### 4. Make one neutral public-review request

> If you would be comfortable sharing an honest public review of your experience, you can do so at [approved review destination]. There is no obligation, and your review will not affect current or future service. Please describe only your own experience and avoid including sensitive project or partner information.

Send the same destination and neutral wording regardless of expected sentiment.

### 5. One reminder at most

If no review appears, send one reminder after 7-14 days. Do not continue chasing. Silence is not consent and not a service problem.

### 6. Record provenance

Record request date, wording, destination, relationship, incentive status, review URL if voluntarily provided, response, and any subsequent correction or complaint. Do not store platform credentials or post on the customer's behalf.

### 7. Respond publicly with restraint

For positive reviews:

> Thank you for trusting Hajime with your coordination. We appreciate the specific feedback and wish you well with the next stage.

For critical reviews:

> Thank you for raising this. We take the concern seriously and would like to review the facts through our complaint process. To protect your information, we will not discuss project details publicly. Please contact [verified channel] and reference [neutral case identifier if available].

Do not expose confidential facts, argue about the customer's motives, or pressure removal.

## Monthly review-quality check

- Requests sent and response rate
- Positive, neutral, and critical reviews
- Requests made while issues were open
- Any incentive or relationship disclosure
- Reviews needing privacy or accuracy follow-up
- Repeated service themes
- Whether website excerpts still match the source review

## Stop conditions

Pause review requests if Hajime cannot respond to complaints, if incentives are being offered, if staff are selectively routing only happy customers, or if public excerpts cannot be traced to permissioned source material.

## Evidence basis

Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency includes reviews and social posts in its explanation of advertising that may fall within the stealth-marketing rule when business involvement is not recognizable. See the [official guidance](https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/representation/fair_labeling/stealth_marketing/). This process deliberately avoids hidden sponsorship and review gating; counsel should review actual platform and jurisdiction requirements.
