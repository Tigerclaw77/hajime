# Trust Architecture

**Audience:** Hajime leadership and anyone approving public claims  
**Status:** Internal control document; not customer terms  
**Review cadence:** Before launch, monthly during the first ten customers, and after any material complaint or incident

## The trust question

The strongest reason not to trust Hajime is structural: a customer is being asked to place an important, expensive, multi-party project in the hands of a new coordinator that is not the law firm, tax adviser, bank, government agency, or filing professional responsible for many outcomes.

Hajime can overcome that objection only by proving five things:

1. It never blurs coordination with regulated professional work.
2. It is accountable for the work it does control.
3. It selects and monitors partners through evidence, not branding.
4. It protects sensitive information and limits access.
5. It tells customers bad news early and offers a fair path to remediation.

## Trust pillars

| Pillar | Customer fear | Public promise | Required proof | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | ???I do not know who is actually responsible.??? | One named coordinator and a responsibility map | Coordinator assignment; backup; current owner per task | Operations lead |
| Boundaries | ???Hajime may be giving unqualified advice.??? | Regulated work is attributed to the licensed professional | Counsel-approved boundary copy; engagement map; communication sample | Risk owner |
| Competence | ???A polished intermediary may add no real value.??? | Every engagement has a written plan, next action, owner, and timeframe | Delivered plan; update log; completion evidence | Coordinator |
| Partner quality | ???I may be handed to an unknown provider.??? | Identity, registration, capability, capacity, fit, and conflicts are checked before assignment | Dated partner record and official-register check | Partner manager |
| Independence | ???Recommendations may be paid placements.??? | No partner can buy ranking or access; commercial relationships are disclosed | Conflict and economics record; disclosure copy | Founder |
| Information protection | ???My identity and business records could leak.??? | Collect less, restrict access, share for a stated purpose, and respond to incidents | Data inventory; MFA; access review; retention schedule; incident procedure | Security owner |

## Customer-visible proof sequence

### Before booking

- Clear identity and service boundary
- Transparent package structure and external-cost exclusions
- Public FAQ, privacy commitments, security overview, and roadmap
- No invented client logos, case studies, partner badges, or outcome claims

### Before payment

- Written scope, exclusions, assumptions, deliverables, and fee schedule
- Named Hajime owner and backup route
- Responsibility map showing customer, Hajime, partner, and external ownership
- Partner identity and engagement structure
- Cancellation, refund, complaint, privacy, and escalation routes

### During delivery

- Current step, next step, responsible party, expected range, and changed assumptions
- Evidence-based status language
- Prompt disclosure of material delay, error, conflict, or partner issue
- Purpose-limited information sharing

### At closeout

- Delivered versus not delivered
- Open obligations and named owner
- Final cost reconciliation
- Access and retention review
- Unpressured satisfaction and review request

## Claim-control rule

Every public trust claim must be classified as one of:

- **Fact:** true now and supported by dated evidence.
- **Commitment:** a behavior Hajime controls and has capacity to perform.
- **Target:** a measurable objective, clearly labeled as such.
- **External estimate:** a range supplied by a partner, institution, or published source.
- **Future intention:** not available today and shown only on the roadmap.

No intention may be written as a fact. No target may be written as a guarantee. No partner or agency estimate may be written as a Hajime commitment.

## Launch blockers

Do not accept a paying customer until all are true:

- Japanese counsel has reviewed professional boundaries, marketing, referral economics, privacy notice, and proposed customer terms.
- A business-controlled document channel exists with MFA, least-privilege access, and a tested recovery path.
- The founder can produce a data inventory, retention rule, incident contact, and current vendor list.
- Every assigned regulated partner has a verified identity and registration record.
- The first engagement has a named coordinator, backup, complaint owner, and escalation owner.
- Scope, external fees, refund/cancellation treatment, and no-guarantee language are in the written proposal.
- No public testimonial, case study, review, metric, badge, or partner claim lacks evidence and permission.

## Evidence basis

The privacy and security controls reflect the [Japan PPC legal framework](https://www.ppc.go.jp/en/legal/), PPC guidance on clear notice and unintended data collection, and [IPA SME security guidance](https://www.ipa.go.jp/en/about/activities/security-action.html). Professional-boundary controls reflect the [Attorneys Act](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/4610/tb), [National Tax Agency description of reserved tax services](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/01.htm), and Immigration Services Agency application-intermediary rules. These sources guide conservative operations; Japanese counsel must determine how they apply to Hajime.
