# Why Hajime Exists

**Status:** Public copy ready for editorial use  
**Primary placement:** Homepage, About page, founder letter

## Headline

The problem is not finding a list of providers. It is making the whole launch work together.

## Narrative

A founder entering Japan can find an attorney, a scrivener, an accountant, an office provider, and a bank. What is harder to find is someone responsible for the space between them.

Who should act first? Which decision changes the office, capital, immigration, or banking path? Has a document merely been received, or has the responsible professional accepted it? Is a delay normal, or is the launch now at risk? Who tells the founder what happens next?

Without a coordination owner, those questions return to the founder. Information is repeated. Dependencies surface late. Each provider sees a valid part of the project, but no one is paid to keep the entire journey coherent.

Hajime was created for that gap.

We give the launch one operating plan and one accountable coordinator. We prepare the right questions for qualified professionals, maintain the responsibility map, track evidence and external reviews, and turn each completed decision into the next clear action.

We are not building another directory, law firm facade, or opaque referral marketplace. Hajime is useful only when the customer has more clarity, less coordination burden, and fewer preventable surprises than they would have managing the same providers alone.

## The promise

You may still face difficult decisions, external review, and uncertain timing. You should never be left wondering what is happening, who owns it, or what comes next.
