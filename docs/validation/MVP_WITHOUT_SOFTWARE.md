# Hajime Japan MVP Without Software

## Premise

If the Hajime application disappeared tomorrow, the service could still be sold and delivered. The minimum viable business is a founder, a disciplined operating method, a small trusted partner bench, standard documents, email, and a spreadsheet.

The customer is buying accountable coordination, not a portal.

## Minimum viable business

Hajime needs only:

- One narrow target segment.
- One paid readiness offer.
- One founder acting as sales owner and coordinator.
- Four core professional partner relationships.
- A written boundary and referral policy.
- Standard intake, plan, status, handoff, and closeout templates.
- A basic contract, invoice method, and secure document-handling process reviewed by appropriate counsel.
- A weekly operating cadence.

It does not need a dashboard, automation, customer login, workflow engine, CRM, API, database, notification system, or custom document tracker to validate payment.

## Manual tool stack

Use the smallest stack the founder can operate reliably. Do not adopt all tools merely because they are listed.

### Google Docs

Use for customer-facing narrative documents:

- Discovery summary.
- Japan Launch Readiness report.
- Responsibility and dependency map.
- Meeting agendas and decision records.
- Weekly status report.
- Provider handoff brief.
- Launch closeout summary.

Create one document from a locked template per customer. Share view/comment access only with named participants. Do not place highly sensitive identity or financial evidence in general working documents.

### Email

Use as the authoritative communication and approval channel:

- Discovery and proposal follow-up.
- Scope confirmation.
- Decision and assumption confirmation.
- Partner introductions with customer consent.
- Weekly status summary.
- Escalation and change control.

Use a consistent subject format:

`Hajime | [Customer] | [Project] | [Topic]`

Every material email should state the action, owner, and due date. Messaging applications may coordinate urgent scheduling but should not hold final decisions.

### Notion

Notion is optional. Use it only as an internal operating workspace for:

- Standard operating procedures.
- Reusable templates.
- Partner capability profiles.
- Lessons and experiment history.
- A non-sensitive project overview.

Do not require customers or licensed partners to use Notion. Do not duplicate every project fact across Notion and the spreadsheet.

### Spreadsheets

Use one workbook as the operating control plane.

Suggested tabs:

| Tab | Purpose | Minimum columns |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | Track validation funnel | Name, company, source, trigger, status, next action, owner, date, offer, outcome |
| Projects | Track paid engagements | Customer, scope, start, target, stage, health, next action, owner, due date |
| Tasks | Track all open work | Project, task, responsible party, dependency, status, requested, due, completed |
| Decisions | Preserve important choices | Project, question, owner, options, decision, date, source |
| Documents | Track requests without storing files | Project, document name, requested by, responsible party, status, due, secure location |
| Partners | Track capability and performance | Provider, role, license/qualification, language, location, scope, fee model, capacity, issues |
| Experiments | Preserve validation evidence | Hypothesis, cohort, variable, start, end, result, decision |
| Metrics | Calculate scorecard | Funnel counts, conversion, selling price, time to close, hours, margin, satisfaction, referrals |

The workbook contains operational metadata, not copies of passports, bank records, identity evidence, or confidential professional advice.

### Secure file storage

Use a business-controlled cloud storage account with least-privilege access, multi-factor authentication, clear folders, and a documented retention policy. Customer and partner permissions should be explicit and reviewed at closeout.

The validation kit does not prescribe a consumer storage account for sensitive data. Obtain legal and security review before collecting identity or regulated documents. During initial readiness work, collect only what is necessary and prefer recording that evidence exists over copying it.

## Manual customer workflow

### 1. Lead capture

Founder adds the person to the Leads tab within one business day and records:

- Company and contact.
- Source and referrer.
- Concrete Japan trigger.
- Target date.
- Immediate question.
- Next action and date.

### 2. Qualification

Founder conducts a 20-30 minute call using `SALES_SCRIPT.md` and marks the outcome:

- Qualified for paid readiness.
- Nurture until a named trigger.
- Refer to a professional.
- Decline.

No custom workflow is created for an unqualified lead.

### 3. Proposal and payment

Founder sends a standard written scope containing:

- Desired outcome.
- Included deliverables.
- Customer responsibilities.
- Professional boundaries.
- Price and payment terms.
- Delivery timing and intake dependency.
- Expiration and next action.

Delivery does not begin before signature and cleared payment or deposit.

### 4. Intake

Founder sends a structured questionnaire and holds one intake meeting if needed. Intake asks for business facts, owners, target city, activity, trigger, existing providers, deadlines, and open questions.

Record professional questions without answering them. Request sensitive evidence only when necessary and through the approved secure process.

### 5. Readiness production

Founder manually creates:

1. Current-state summary.
2. Known assumptions and unresolved questions.
3. Workstream and dependency map.
4. Responsibility matrix.
5. Preliminary timeline and cost ranges.
6. Prioritized next actions.
7. Stop, self-manage, specialist-only, or Hajime continuation recommendation.

Every action has one owner and one next date. External dates are ranges and assumptions, not guarantees.

### 6. Customer review

Hold a 60-minute review. Confirm:

- What is known.
- What requires professional advice.
- Current step.
- Next step.
- Responsible party.
- Estimated timeframe and dependencies.
- Whether the customer wants to self-manage or purchase continued coordination.

Send the corrected final document and decisions by email.

### 7. Partner handoff

With customer consent, send a concise handoff brief:

- Customer identity and contact.
- Business purpose and requested professional scope.
- Relevant facts and assumptions.
- Target timeframe.
- Documents available.
- Questions reserved for the professional.
- Hajime's coordination role.

The professional contracts directly with the customer for regulated work unless an approved arrangement states otherwise. Hajime does not paraphrase a professional opinion as its own.

### 8. Ongoing coordination

For Guided Launch or Concierge, the founder:

- Reviews open tasks each morning.
- Chases overdue owners with a specific request.
- Updates the spreadsheet after material events.
- Sends one concise weekly status email.
- Escalates changed assumptions, blocked dependencies, and scope changes.
- Records waiting time separately from active Hajime effort.

Weekly status format:

1. Overall stage and health.
2. What changed this week.
3. What the customer must do next.
4. What Hajime and partners are doing.
5. What is waiting and why.
6. Dates or assumptions at risk.
7. Decisions required.

### 9. Closeout

Founder delivers:

- Completed and outstanding items.
- Provider and obligation handoffs.
- Known deadlines and owners.
- Open risks and assumptions.
- Optional post-launch coordination scope.

Collect a value rating, one improvement, and a specific referral request. Apply the retention policy and remove unnecessary access.

## Responsibility model

| Work | Customer | Hajime | Licensed professional | Institution / government |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provide accurate facts and decisions | Owns | Tracks | Relies on within scope | May verify |
| Launch plan and coordination | Participates | Owns | Advises on professional dependencies | Does not own |
| Legal, immigration, tax, accounting, or regulated judgment | Receives and decides | Routes; does not advise | Owns within engagement | Applies rules / decides where relevant |
| Documents and evidence | Provides / authorizes | Tracks requests and versions | Specifies professional requirements | May accept or reject |
| External approval | Cooperates | Tracks and communicates | Represents where authorized | Owns decision |
| Next-action clarity | Confirms | Owns | Confirms its actions | Not responsible for project clarity |

## Partner coordination without software

Maintain a small bench, not a marketplace:

- Two viable providers for each core capability where possible.
- Written capability, language, location, fee model, capacity, and boundary notes.
- Customer consent before every introduction.
- One handoff brief and one owner per request.
- Direct professional engagement for regulated work.
- No paid ranking or undisclosed referral economics.
- Performance review after each matter.

Start with an accountant, judicial scrivener, administrative scrivener or immigration professional, and international business attorney. Add office, banking-readiness, translation, housing, insurance, and payroll relationships only when actual customer needs repeat.

## Founder operating cadence

### Daily

- Review actions due today and overdue.
- Respond to customers and partners.
- Update material status and decisions.
- Reserve a protected block for customer work and a separate block for acquisition.

### Weekly

- Monday: project and pipeline priorities.
- Wednesday: partner and blocked-item review.
- Friday: customer status emails, validation scorecard, hours, and experiment decision.

### After every customer

- Compare estimated and actual effort.
- Record scope surprises and repeated questions.
- Update the template only for a repeated, material need.
- Ask whether the work changed a decision or saved customer time.
- Ask for one introduction if value was demonstrated.

## Manual capacity limit

Until effort data proves otherwise, one founder should handle no more than:

- Three active Concierge launches, or
- Five active Guided Launch engagements, or
- Eight concurrent readiness engagements,

with fewer when complexity is high. Waiting projects still require communication and should count partially toward capacity.

Capacity pressure is not evidence that software is needed. First test qualification, scope, templates, cadence, and price. Build software only when repeated paid work exposes a stable bottleneck.

## Minimum proof before technology investment

The manual business is validated when:

- Ten customers have paid, or the scorecard's first-ten continuation gate is met.
- Six customers share one coherent segment.
- One channel produces at least three customers.
- One partner makes a second qualified referral.
- Contribution margin is positive with founder labor cost included.
- Customers value coordination independent of a portal.
- The same workflow repeats with limited customization.
- The most expensive recurring operational bottleneck is identified from measured delivery hours.

Only then should Hajime evaluate whether software removes that bottleneck. Technology is an implementation choice, not the validation result.
