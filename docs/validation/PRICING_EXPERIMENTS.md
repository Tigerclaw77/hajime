# Hajime Japan Pricing Experiments

## Objective

Learn what qualified customers will pay for coordination, which scope they understand, and whether the price supports manual delivery. The goal is evidence from signed and paid engagements, not stated willingness to pay.

## Rules

- Test one material variable at a time.
- Use the same qualification standard across cohorts.
- Quote one approved price to a prospect; never reveal an arbitrary range and ask them to choose.
- Keep professional, government, office, translation, and other third-party fees separate.
- Never tie a fee to bank, visa, registration, license, tax, or government approval.
- Do not discount the same scope during a call. Reduce scope or use a pre-defined experiment.
- Do not exchange discounts for positive testimonials.
- Record the offered price, scope, source, segment, response, reason, delivery hours, and direct costs.
- Review each test after at least five qualified offers where possible. Small samples are directional, not statistically conclusive.

## Baseline offer

**Japan Launch Readiness** provides:

- Structured intake.
- Current-state and readiness summary.
- Decision and dependency map.
- Responsibility map.
- Preliminary cost and timeline ranges with assumptions.
- Prioritized next actions.
- One 60-minute review session.

Target delivery: within ten business days after complete intake. No ongoing coordination or professional advice is included.

## Experiment sequence

Run experiments in order. Do not test packages or success elements before a customer has paid for the baseline fixed-fee offer.

### Experiment 1: fixed-fee willingness to pay

**Question:** Will qualified buyers pay for readiness before committing to a larger launch engagement?

| Cohort | Offer | Price | Minimum sample |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | Baseline readiness | USD 750 | 5 qualified offers |
| B | Baseline readiness | USD 1,500 | 5 qualified offers |
| C | Baseline readiness | USD 2,500 | 5 qualified offers |

Use sequential cohorts and keep segment, scope, and sales process as consistent as possible. Start with USD 1,500. Move to USD 2,500 after two acceptances; use USD 750 only as a deliberately narrower or early-learning cohort, not as a rescue discount.

**Intended learning:**

- Whether readiness is independently valuable.
- Price sensitivity and trust threshold.
- Whether higher price changes objections, speed, or customer quality.
- Whether delivery margin is viable.

**Measure:** proposal acceptance, days to payment, delivery hours, value rating, upgrade request, and reason for loss.

**Decision rule:** prefer the highest price with at least 30% acceptance among qualified offers, median value rating of 8/10 or higher, and positive contribution margin. Do not infer price failure if most losses are actually poor qualification or absent urgency.

### Experiment 2: fixed fee versus credited readiness

**Question:** Does crediting readiness toward a larger engagement reduce purchase friction without making readiness appear free?

| Cohort | Terms |
|---|---|
| A | Readiness fee stands alone |
| B | 50% of readiness fee credited toward Guided Launch or Concierge if signed within 30 days and assumptions remain valid |

Hold the readiness fee and scope constant.

**Intended learning:**

- Whether buyers hesitate because they fear paying twice.
- Whether a credit increases qualified upgrades.
- Whether the credit attracts serious launch buyers or premature package decisions.

**Success signal:** Cohort B improves paid readiness or upgrade conversion by at least 15 percentage points without reducing total collected revenue or increasing refund disputes.

**Failure signal:** Customers treat readiness as a sales consultation, rush into an unsuitable package, or wait for credits before buying.

### Experiment 3: one offer versus tiered packages

**Question:** Does visible choice improve self-selection, or create confusion before Hajime has enough proof?

Run only after at least three paid readiness engagements.

| Tier | Scope hypothesis | Price hypothesis |
|---|---|---:|
| Blueprint | Readiness deliverables; customer executes | USD 1,500-2,500 |
| Guided Launch | Blueprint plus named coordinator, core introductions, scheduled checkpoints, and defined handoffs | USD 5,000-8,000 |
| Concierge | Active cross-provider management, consolidated requests, and higher coordination ownership | USD 10,000-18,000 |

These are test hypotheses, not published list prices. External costs remain separate.

Compare five qualified prospects shown only the recommended package with five comparable prospects shown all three options.

**Intended learning:**

- Whether buyers understand the distinction in coordination ownership.
- Which package matches actual willingness to delegate.
- Whether three options increase decisions or invite price anchoring to the cheapest tier.

**Success signal:** Tiered presentation maintains at least 35% proposal acceptance, reduces scope objections, and does not push more than 60% of qualified multi-provider projects into Blueprint.

**Failure signal:** Customers cannot explain the difference, choose based only on price, or require substantial custom scoping.

### Experiment 4: milestone payment structure

**Question:** Can payment timing reduce perceived risk while preserving cash flow and commitment?

Test comparable larger engagements:

- **A: 100% upfront** for readiness or projects under USD 5,000.
- **B: 50% at engagement, 50% at delivery of the agreed Hajime milestone.**
- **C: 40% at engagement, 30% at approved launch plan, 30% at operational handoff** for longer projects.

Milestones must describe Hajime-delivered work, never external approvals.

**Intended learning:**

- Whether objections concern total price or commitment risk.
- Which structure supports reliable customer responsiveness.
- Cash-flow and collection burden.

**Success signal:** Faster signature with no increase in late payments, pauses, or collection time.

### Experiment 5: optional concierge add-ons

**Question:** Which variable needs customers will pay Hajime to coordinate instead of managing themselves?

Test add-ons only when the need is discovered before proposal:

| Add-on | Test price | What it includes | Learning goal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Additional stakeholder | USD 500-1,000 | Defined meetings, decisions, and reporting for one additional stakeholder group | Value of communication complexity |
| Second banking path readiness | USD 750-1,500 | Preparation and coordination for an additional institution; no approval promise | Value of additional path management |
| Expanded translation coordination | Fixed quote plus transparent external costs | Request preparation, routing, version control, and handoff | Whether customers value coordination separate from translation |
| Complex office search coordination | USD 1,000-2,500 | Requirements, shortlist coordination, provider handoffs, and decision tracking | Value of reducing office/address burden |
| Post-launch stabilization | USD 1,000-2,500 per month for a defined period | Open-item coordination and scheduled status reviews | Demand for continued ownership after formation |

**Intended learning:** attachment rate, margin, scope clarity, and whether the add-on solves a repeated problem.

**Keep an add-on when:** at least 25% of eligible customers buy it, customers rate it 8/10 or higher, and it produces positive contribution margin.

**Remove it when:** it is rarely selected, commonly disputed, or pulls Hajime into regulated advice.

### Experiment 6: recurring post-launch coordination

**Question:** Does coordination value continue after launch?

Offer only after the customer experiences the core service:

- USD 1,000/month for a three-month stabilization period.
- Defined request limits, one monthly operating review, obligation tracking, and provider coordination.
- Material projects and professional work separately scoped.

**Intended learning:** real recurring need, retention, support load, and willingness to pay without manufactured tasks.

**Success signal:** at least 30% of eligible customers accept, 80% complete the initial three months, and delivery maintains positive contribution margin.

**Failure signal:** customers use it only as unlimited support, cannot identify ongoing coordination outcomes, or cancel after one month.

### Experiment 7: success-based element

Pure success fees are not recommended. External institutions and professionals control many launch outcomes, and payment tied to approval can imply influence or guarantees.

The only acceptable validation test is a **Hajime-delivery holdback**:

- 80% fixed fee under the normal payment schedule.
- 20% due when Hajime delivers a named artifact or completes a customer-controlled coordination milestone.
- The milestone cannot be incorporation, bank approval, visa approval, licensing, tax outcome, or a government date.

Examples of valid milestones include an approved responsibility map, complete provider handoff package, or completed operational-readiness review.

**Intended learning:** whether a limited holdback improves trust and closing speed.

**Stop immediately when:** prospects interpret the structure as a guarantee, milestones become disputable, or collection effort exceeds the sales benefit.

## Pricing interview questions

Ask after understanding actual behavior and spending:

- What have you already spent on professional help and internal coordination time?
- Which work would you pay to remove from your team?
- What would you never delegate?
- What would make a fixed fee feel safer or riskier than hourly billing?
- At what price would this seem too inexpensive to trust?
- At what price would it feel like good value?
- At what price would it become difficult to justify?
- At what price would it be too expensive to consider?

Answers inform the experiment; they do not replace a paid offer.

## Pricing validation gate

Pricing is provisionally validated when:

- At least five comparable customers pay.
- At least three pay USD 1,500 or more for readiness, or deposits on larger engagements demonstrate higher willingness to pay.
- Proposal acceptance among qualified prospects is at least 35%.
- Median time to payment is 30 days or less.
- Median delivery contribution margin is positive after founder labor is assigned a realistic cost.
- Price is not achieved through hidden third-party markups or guaranteed-outcome language.
