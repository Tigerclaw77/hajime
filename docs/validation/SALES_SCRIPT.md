# Hajime Japan Sales Script

## Sales posture

Discovery is mutual qualification, not a presentation. The founder should listen for at least 70% of the call, ask about recent behavior before hypothetical preferences, and avoid solving the project live.

Hajime may explain coordination, process, ownership, and typical ranges. Hajime must not provide legal, immigration, tax, accounting, banking, insurance, or real-estate advice; guarantee an external outcome; or imply that a professional relationship is already available.

## Before the call

Review:

- Company, founder, and source.
- Public evidence of the Japan trigger.
- Submitted timing, business activity, and immediate question.
- Likely decision-maker and other stakeholders.
- Potential boundary or fit concerns.

Send a short agenda:

> We will spend 30 minutes understanding why Japan matters now, how you are handling the launch, what is blocked, and whether coordination is the right next step. I will leave time to explain how Hajime works if there is a fit.

## Discovery call outline

### 0-3 minutes: set the frame

> Thank you for making the time. I have a few facts from your note, but I would rather hear the situation in your words. I will ask about the trigger, the work already underway, and how a decision would be made. If Hajime is not the right fit, I will say so. Does that approach work for you?

### 3-10 minutes: understand why now

- What changed that made Japan an active project rather than a future idea?
- What specific event or date are you working toward?
- What happens to the business if that date moves by three months?
- Who is most affected by the outcome?
- What has already been approved or committed?

Listen for a customer, partner, hire, relocation, or board deadline. Do not manufacture urgency when none exists.

### 10-20 minutes: understand the current process and cost

- Walk me through what has happened since the decision became active.
- Who currently owns the overall plan?
- Which providers have you contacted, and what does each one own?
- Where are the dependencies, documents, decisions, and dates tracked?
- What has taken more time than expected?
- Where have instructions conflicted or work been repeated?
- What is the next action today, and who is responsible?
- What is still unclear?
- Roughly how much founder or team time has gone into coordination?

Use follow-ups such as "What happened next?", "Can you give me the most recent example?", and "How did you handle that?"

### 20-25 minutes: qualify the purchase

- Who would approve outside help?
- Who else would influence or block the decision?
- What budget exists for coordination, separate from professional and government fees?
- What work would you pay to remove from your team's plate?
- What would you always keep internally?
- What evidence would you need to trust a coordinator?
- When would you need to decide?

### 25-28 minutes: reflect, do not pitch

> Let me check that I understood. You are working toward [event/date]. The launch currently involves [providers/workstreams]. The main burden is [customer's words], and [person] is absorbing it. The immediate risk is [consequence]. Is that accurate? What did I miss?

If there is no fit, say so before describing the offer.

### 28-30 minutes: agree on one next step

For a qualified readiness buyer:

> The smallest useful next step is a paid Japan Launch Readiness engagement. We would document the current state, responsibilities, dependencies, preliminary ranges, and prioritized next actions. Licensed professionals remain responsible for their work. The fee is [approved test price], and delivery begins after intake and payment. Should I send the written scope today for a decision by [date]?

For a larger but clear need:

> Your needs appear broader than readiness alone. I recommend that we still define the assumptions and boundaries in writing before discussing a launch package. I can send that summary and the proposed scope by [date]. Who else should review it?

For no current urgency:

> I do not hear a dated project that needs active coordination yet. I suggest we do not start a paid engagement now. May I follow up after [specific trigger]?

For professional-only demand:

> That question needs a qualified [professional]. Hajime should not advise you on it. I can record it as a professional question, but I would not sell you coordination unless there is a broader project to own.

## Qualification questions

### Commercial fit

- Why Japan, and why now?
- What external commitment makes progress necessary?
- Is the company operating, revenue-generating, or funded?
- What does a successful Japan launch enable commercially?
- What is the consequence of doing nothing for six months?

### Coordination fit

- How many separate workstreams or providers are involved?
- Who owns cross-provider handoffs?
- What has already been delayed or repeated?
- Which tasks does the team want to delegate?
- Is the need broader than a single filing or professional opinion?

### Authority and budget

- Who signs the engagement?
- Who controls the budget?
- What other project competes for the same funds?
- Are professional and government fees budgeted separately?
- What has the company already paid for similar outside help?

### Timing and responsiveness

- What date is fixed, and what date is only preferred?
- Which customer actions or documents could delay the plan?
- Who will respond to requests during the engagement?
- Are all founders and beneficial owners prepared to provide transparent information?

### Boundary and risk

- Is the buyer seeking a guaranteed bank, visa, registration, tax, license, or government result?
- Is the primary requirement regulated advice or representation?
- Is the ownership, source of funds, and business activity transparent?
- Is the expected deadline physically possible?

## Qualification outcome

### Strong fit

- Concrete Japan trigger within 12 months.
- Three or more interdependent needs.
- Decision-maker is engaged.
- Budget includes coordination and external costs.
- Customer values one accountable coordinator.
- Business purpose and ownership are transparent.

### Nurture

- Credible company and likely future trigger, but no active date or budget.
- Follow up on a named trigger, not an arbitrary monthly cadence.

### Refer

- A single professional need is clear and Hajime coordination would add little value.
- Refer only with consent and without promising acceptance or outcome.

### Decline

- Cheapest filing is the sole criterion.
- Buyer demands guaranteed outcomes.
- Ownership, funds, activity, or identity is concealed.
- Timeline is impossible.
- The required service is unsupported or outside boundaries.

## Objection handling

Use four steps: acknowledge, clarify, answer narrowly, and check. Do not argue.

### "Why not hire an attorney or accountant directly?"

> You may only need one. What work do you expect that professional to own, and who would coordinate the remaining office, banking, document, timing, and handoff work?

If the customer has one narrow need, refer rather than force a sale. If the work is fragmented:

> The professional remains responsible for their advice and filings. Hajime is paid to prepare the project, coordinate across scopes, consolidate requests, and keep the next action clear.

### "JETRO or Tokyo provides this for free."

> Those are valuable resources and you should use them. Which parts of execution and follow-up will your team still own after the consultation?

> Hajime is not charging for access to general information. The paid work is dedicated preparation and coordination for your project.

### "The price is too high."

> Compared with which alternative or internal approach?

Then ask:

- Is the concern total budget, timing, or uncertainty about value?
- Which deliverable would you remove to make the scope worthwhile?
- What is the cost of the team's current coordination time or a missed deadline?

Reduce scope before reducing the price of the same promise.

### "Can you guarantee the bank account, visa, registration, or completion date?"

> No. Those decisions and timings belong to institutions, government agencies, or licensed professionals. Hajime can improve preparation, ownership, and escalation, but cannot guarantee their decision.

If a guarantee is required, decline.

### "We can manage this ourselves."

> You may be right. What has your team already mapped, who owns the cross-provider plan, and where would outside coordination create no additional value?

If they have capacity and clarity, offer the Blueprint/readiness scope only or do not sell.

### "We are not ready."

> What event would make the project active, and what would need to be true before you would pay for help?

Record the event and obtain permission to follow up then.

### "We need to think about it."

> Of course. What specifically remains unresolved: fit, trust, scope, price, timing, or internal approval?

> Who needs to participate, and when will you make the decision?

Agree on a dated next step or close the opportunity as no decision.

### "Can you discount for a testimonial or early-customer status?"

> We are testing the service and will be transparent about that, but we do not trade positive reviews for discounts. If the current scope exceeds your need, we can reduce the scope and price together.

### "Can you use our existing providers?"

> Yes, if responsibilities, communication, and professional boundaries are clear. Hajime's job is not to replace a provider that already fits.

## Closing questions

- Does the written summary match the problem you would pay to solve?
- What would stop you from approving this scope?
- Who else must review it?
- Are professional and external costs understood as separate?
- Is there any outcome you believe Hajime is guaranteeing?
- If the scope arrives today, when will you decide?
- If approved, are you prepared to pay the invoice and complete intake by [date]?

Avoid "Would you like to move forward?" when the next action can be made specific.

## Next-step language

### Proposal follow-up

> I sent the scope we discussed. It reflects [trigger], [coordination problem], and [desired outcome]. Please reply with corrections by [date]. If it is accurate, the next step is signature and payment by [date]; delivery begins after complete intake.

### Dated follow-up

> You planned to decide today. Is the answer yes, no, or not now? Any answer is useful. If something remains unresolved, tell me which issue it is.

### Graceful close

> It sounds like this is not a current priority. I will close the proposal rather than continue chasing it. If [trigger] occurs, you are welcome to reopen the conversation.

### Decline

> Based on what we discussed, Hajime is not the right provider because [plain reason]. The responsible next step is [public resource or professional category], subject to their own assessment.

## Call notes

Record immediately:

- Trigger and date.
- Current process and providers.
- Coordination pain in the customer's words.
- Consequence of delay.
- Decision-maker, approver, and blockers.
- Budget evidence.
- Fit outcome.
- Objections.
- Promised next step, owner, and date.
- Offer and price shown.
- Final outcome and reason.
