# Hajime Japan Validation Scorecard

## Purpose

Determine whether to continue, pivot, or stop using payment, delivery, and referral evidence. The scorecard measures the business, not software engagement.

## Definitions

- **Targeted visitor:** A visitor from a relevant partner, outreach, community, or high-intent search source; exclude team traffic and obvious irrelevant visits.
- **Booking:** A completed request for a discovery time.
- **Qualified discovery:** A completed call meeting the target-company, trigger, coordination-need, authority, budget, and boundary criteria.
- **Proposal:** A written offer with a price, scope, expiry, and next step sent to a qualified buyer.
- **Paying customer:** Signed scope, cleared payment or deposit, and scheduled delivery.
- **Referral:** An introduction to a named prospect made by a customer or partner, not permission to mention a name.
- **Time to close:** Calendar days from first completed discovery to cleared payment.
- **Average selling price:** Collected or contractually committed Hajime fees, excluding taxes, refunds, professional fees, and pass-through costs, divided by paying customers.
- **Contribution margin:** Hajime revenue minus direct external costs and delivery labor valued at a realistic hourly cost.
- **Customer satisfaction:** Post-delivery answer to "How valuable was this engagement?" on a 0-10 scale.
- **Repeat business:** A paid add-on, upgrade, or post-launch engagement from an existing customer.

## Threshold table

Use "Continue" only after the minimum sample is reached. Before then, mark the metric "insufficient evidence."

| Metric | Minimum evidence | Continue | Pivot | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Targeted landing-page visitor to booking | 100 targeted visitors | 8% or higher | 3-7.9% | Under 3% after two message tests |
| Booking to completed discovery | 20 bookings | 70% or higher | 45-69% | Under 45% after reminder/process correction |
| Discovery qualification rate | 20 completed discoveries | 50% or higher | 25-49% | Under 25% after one channel or targeting change |
| Qualified discovery to proposal | 15 qualified discoveries | 70% or higher | 40-69% | Under 40%; offer or fit is unclear |
| Proposal acceptance | 10 qualified proposals | 35% or higher | 15-34% | Under 15% after one scope/price test |
| Qualified discovery to paid | 20 qualified discoveries | 25% or higher | 10-24% | Under 10%, or fewer than 2 customers |
| Readiness average selling price | 5 paid readiness customers | USD 1,500 or higher | USD 750-1,499 | Under USD 750 without a profitable upgrade path |
| Median time to close | 5 paying customers | 30 days or less | 31-60 days | Over 60 days for founder-led target buyers |
| Contribution margin | 5 completed engagements | 40% or higher | 1-39% | Zero or negative after one scope/price correction |
| On-time Hajime delivery | 5 completed engagements | 90% or higher | 70-89% | Under 70% excluding documented customer/external waits |
| Median customer value rating | 5 completed engagements | 8/10 or higher | 6-7.9 | Under 6 |
| Customer referral rate | 5 completed customers | 30% or higher make a named introduction within 60 days | 10-29% | Under 10% after explicit asks |
| Partner repeat-referral rate | 4 active pilot partners over 60 days | At least one partner makes a second qualified referral | Referrals occur but none repeats | No qualified partner referrals |
| Repeat business or upgrade | 5 eligible customers over 90 days | 30% or higher | 10-29% | Under 10%, if recurring need is part of the thesis |
| Refund or material complaint rate | 10 paying customers | Under 10% | 10-20% | Over 20% or any unresolved boundary breach |

Landing-page thresholds are diagnostic only. Strong referral-led sales can validate the business even with low site traffic. Payment and contribution margin outrank clicks.

## Funnel calculations

Calculate monthly and cumulatively:

- Landing-page conversion = bookings / targeted visitors.
- Consultation completion = completed discoveries / bookings.
- Qualification rate = qualified discoveries / completed discoveries.
- Proposal rate = proposals / qualified discoveries.
- Proposal acceptance = paying customers / proposals.
- Discovery-to-paid = paying customers / qualified discoveries.
- Referral rate = customers making a named introduction / eligible completed customers.
- Repeat-business rate = customers buying again / eligible customers.
- Average selling price = Hajime fees / paying customers.
- Contribution margin percentage = (Hajime fees - direct delivery cost) / Hajime fees.

Never mix professional or pass-through fees into Hajime revenue.

## Stage gates

### Gate 1: problem and payment

Review after six qualified discoveries or three weeks, whichever is later.

**Continue when:** at least one customer pays and the customer reports that coordination, clarity, preparation, or saved time is valuable.

**Pivot when:** prospects have a real launch but want a narrower problem solved, a different entry offer, or a different price.

**Stop the current offer when:** no one pays after six qualified offers, or demand is almost entirely for regulated advice.

### Gate 2: repeatability

Review after 20 qualified discoveries and at least 10 proposals.

**Continue when:** at least five customers pay, proposal acceptance is at least 35%, and at least two customers share one source or trigger pattern.

**Pivot when:** two to four customers pay and their segment, channel, or purchased scope forms a coherent pattern different from the hypothesis.

**Stop when:** fewer than two customers pay or contribution margin is non-positive after one controlled correction.

### Gate 3: first-ten decision

Review when 10 customers have paid or after 16 validation weeks, whichever occurs first.

**Continue investment when:**

- Ten customers have paid, or at least seven have paid with three active late-stage proposals.
- Six or more fit one coherent segment.
- Three or more came from one repeatable channel.
- One partner made a second qualified referral.
- Proposal acceptance is at least 35%.
- Median time to close is 45 days or less.
- Median customer value rating is at least 8/10.
- Contribution margin is positive and has a credible path to 40%.
- Manual delivery stays inside professional boundaries.

**Pivot when:** five or more customers pay but segment, scope, pricing, or channel thresholds fail. Choose one primary change and run another four-week cohort.

**Stop or pause when:**

- Fewer than five customers pay after 30 qualified discoveries and 15 proposals.
- No acquisition source produces a second customer.
- Buyers will not pay enough to cover realistic delivery labor.
- Satisfaction remains below 6/10 after one delivery correction.
- The value customers demand requires Hajime to act outside its lawful or professional boundaries.

## Pivot diagnosis

| Signal | Most likely uncertainty | First change to test |
|---|---|---|
| Low targeted visitor-to-booking | Message or traffic quality | Rewrite around the concrete trigger; inspect sources |
| High bookings, low qualification | Targeting | Tighten trigger, company-stage, and budget criteria |
| Qualified calls, few proposals | Offer fit or founder reluctance to ask | Simplify next step; send offers within one day |
| Proposals, no payment | Trust, scope, price, or authority | Interview losses; change only the dominant issue |
| Sales close, margin fails | Scope or pricing | Remove open-ended work or raise price |
| High satisfaction, no referrals | Ask timing or low referral density | Ask at a demonstrated value moment with a specific profile |
| Partner introductions, poor fit | Partner education | Restate referral criteria with examples |
| Customers pay, but want professional advice | Boundary mismatch | Partner-led offer or refer; do not expand Hajime's role |

## Decision discipline

- Review weekly for activity, every four weeks for decisions.
- Do not pivot from one objection or one unusual customer.
- Preserve cohort history when changing a variable.
- Record why every opportunity was won, lost, disqualified, or delayed.
- Payment outweighs compliments; margin outweighs revenue alone; boundary safety overrides both.
